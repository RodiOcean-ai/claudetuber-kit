# Owner rules and lessons (read before any stage)

> **Shared kit:** "the owner" in these notes is the person who built this kit. Read it as **you**: replace his rules with your own as you go. His channel numbers and his PC set-up notes were removed from this copy.

Every rule here came from the owner or from a mistake we paid for. Newest lessons go at the bottom of their section.

## Talking to the owner
- Not a programmer: plain everyday English, short answers, lead with what changed.
- No file paths, code, commands or test names in replies unless asked. Explain any unavoidable technical word in a few words.
- Ask what you need clearly at the end, as one question. The owner often works from a phone (Remote Control): closing messages must stand alone.
- Be honest and judgemental about ideas; do not just agree.

- **He judges the video, not still pictures** (12 Nov 2026: after "Yes, build it" Claude stopped at test stills and asked again; the owner: "Where is the video?"). Once he has said build it, check the stills yourself and deliver a watchable cut. Offer choices as things he can see or hear (three thumbnail words on the picture, three endings, a draft voice), never as descriptions.
- **Count twice before telling him a number** (11 Nov 2026: "82 videos in a row" was really 85, and "his lowest recent video" was wrong, because a list had no view count for three videos). Any headline number gets a second reading from another place before it is said or written.
## Money (hard rules)
- Never spend ElevenLabs credits: not through the connector, not on the website, not through the API. Claude builds ElevenCreative boards and prices runs (`estimate_only`); the owner presses Generate. A settings hook enforces this; never ask to loosen it.
- Same for any other paid service (3D generators, the `generate_3d` step in the Blender pack, paid APIs): no spending without the owner's OK for that exact run.
- Voice takes: the owner generates them in ElevenLabs Text to Speech (about 2-4 takes per batch).

## Series rules ("How did X get Y?" creator series)
- **Our niche in simple words, never drift** (owner, 2026-11-04): Shorts that take apart a big channel's strategy: titles, thumbnail, hooks, storytelling (Save the Cat, Problem-Agitation-Solution, Hero's Journey, And-But-Therefore), music, sound effects, volume. Find what works in the niche and copy why it works (skill sf-niche-research: 10 search lines -> standout videos -> channel -> its standout video -> recommendations -> repeat).
- Critique the channel and its techniques, not one video's statistics. (The owner corrected a version that dug into one clip's view counting.)
- The creators stay anonymous in the hook and on the thumbnail ("two brothers", "one faceless channel"); the last line reveals who they are. That reveal is part of the reward. The faceless-channel study's shorts do the same.
- **Long videos name the creator from the start** (owner, 2026-11-06, the survival long video): "I don't want people to skip to the end and leave, I want their watch time." So in a long breakdown the creator is named in the first line (like the creator-breakdown study, which names the creator in every video), and a promise goes up front: "By the end of this video, you'll know exactly how he does it." The anonymous-until-the-last-line rule stays for Shorts. The creator-breakdown study's wording, measured: he/his about 2.4 per 100 words, the creator's name instead of "he", first person ("I've realised", "what I've noticed"), sentences of about 14 words, few fragments, "he says" never.
- Never show a real person's face or make an AI likeness of real people, especially children: silhouettes from behind, faceless toy figures.
- **No kids' channels as subjects** (owner, 2026-11-05): never pick a channel made for children or starring children (family-kid channels, toddler songs and cartoons, kid-prank and "family-friendly" kid Shorts). This rejected SASHA SHOW, the Pevidor family and Alan's Universe. Applies at the cover-test cut of the subject funnel.
- Credit the creator in the description, add "Not affiliated; analysis based on public data", first names only for kids, no real footage/thumbnails/logos in our video.
- Mark "Made for kids: No"; AI label on (YouTube "Altered or synthetic content" = Yes, TikTok "AI-generated content", Instagram "AI info").

## Script (the shorts-format study is the format authority for Shorts)
- 75-95 words, about 2.8 words/s, voice on frame one, no pause over 0.5 s.
- Open on a "you" situation, never on a statistic ("If you filmed yourself flipping a water bottle, you'd expect ten views.").
- Format blocks: hook -> wrong belief -> "But" twist (5-9 s) -> escalation ladder -> payoff (scale surprise, reveal or human sting) -> loop. A setback/comeback is optional (3 of his top 10).
- Keep it simple and fun: one cause-and-effect chain, 0-2 numbers is his habit (we allow up to 4). Too many numbers reads "busy".
- Captions are 1-3 words at a time; phrases and numbers must split cleanly.
- Accuracy: every claim with two sources in facts.md; "if" sentences can't make claims about the viewer ("as you" was wrong after "If you filmed...").
- Critic loops plateau around 6.5-7: fresh critics contradict each other. Keep only fixes two critics agree on, stop after 5 rounds, the owner's ear decides.
- The owner's own wording wins over a critic's rewrite.
- **One-sentence idea that resonates, two critics only** (owner, 2026-11-05, after the toast v8 came out "very bad" from a 5-critic panel: patched line by line, it lost its idea): before any script, write the idea in ONE plain sentence the owner could say to a friend, and it must touch something the viewer has lived (burnt toast, not a record). Then the script tells only that idea. The panel is **shorts-format-critic (lead) + creator-breakdown-critic only**; no story-critic, hook-oracle or creator critics unless the owner asks.
- **Every line needs a purpose** (owner, 2026-11-04, after a public storytelling talk; notes in `reference/methods/storytelling-talk-notes.md`): a job for each line and each lingering object, a villain (an obstacle or belief, never a real person), and a **bookend with a twist** rather than a predictable loop: the ending returns to the opening but the meaning has changed. Skill sf-story-craft, before every panel.
- **The survival-story lens** (owner, 2026-11-06, after the survival-story study: survival premise in 9 of 9 of the strong videos vs 0 of 3 weak): the next Short tells the creator's growth as "will he survive?". Line 1 points at the anonymous hero and names a clock; by 5-9 s the "But" says what he loses if he fails; the middle is 3 links that cause each other ("so… but…"), not a list; one real setback at about 18-22 s, then the turn; facts only when they change what happens next; the last line reveals who it is and returns to frame one with a changed meaning. Skill survival-story-method at stage 3, alongside the subject's own method. The shorts-format rules still win, and the panel stays shorts-format-critic + creator-breakdown-critic (survival-story-critic only if the owner asks). Judge it on its 24-hour numbers like every Short.
- **Pick the structure by the feeling** (owner, 2026-11-04): Save the Cat for satisfying rhythm (long videos), Problem-Agitation-Solution for momentum, Hero's Journey for transformation, And-But-Therefore for clarity (our default on the format spine).
- Vague notes become a numbered change list (lesson from a former lead editor of a top channel on a podcast, 2026-10-27): when the owner says "not there yet" or "doesn't feel right", Claude writes 3-6 concrete changes, shows them, and rebuilds only after an OK. Never guess at a vibe and re-render.
- **Story first, not arithmetic** (owner, 2026-11-11, the cola episode, after five drafts that read like a lab report): "the critic cannot change the whole script because it is 3.75-4.5 instead of 4"; "I need you to be more creative than mathematical, pull storytelling skills, not math". What went wrong: Claude told the critics that numbers must be exact and never hedged, so they marked rounding as an error, and Claude rewrote the script around their decimals each round. The hard-numbers lesson below was over-applied. **The way we work now (owner said yes, 11 Nov 2026):** (1) write the story first, the way you would tell a friend: someone to follow, something in the way, a turn, a payoff; (2) say numbers the way a person would ("every four seconds"), with the exact figures on screen; (3) a number budget of three or four spoken numbers, each a surprise; (4) the critics get a story-only brief: hook, belief, turn, weakest moment, ending; they may not comment on numbers or rounding, and Claude takes at most a few notes instead of rebuilding the script each round; (5) one honesty pass at the end, with one question: is this false in substance? Only a false cause, a promise or a wrong direction counts. **The story is the priority: the honesty pass may NEVER bolt a qualifier onto a line** (owner, 11 Nov, on "half an hour or longer": "you want to stay with facts but you compromise the story line; the storytelling should be the priority"). No "or longer", "about", "I could compare", "in everything I measured" in the voice. If a line truly cannot stand, rewrite it so it is true AND just as strong, or put the caveat on screen or in the description; (6) compare the hook with the subject's own best hooks before showing it: a name everyone knows, a contradiction, one picture that sticks, a stake that reaches the viewer, a promise.
- **Hard numbers, not "sometimes it works"** (owner, 2026-11-09, pause-2-seconds): a do-this Short must not end on a hedge such as "most of the weak ones did it too": "this is not interesting to hear that sometimes this works". Use exact measured numbers in the voice and keep the caveats in facts.md. Never overclaim to get there: the numbers must be true as counted, and no cause is implied.

- **Write like the format benchmark speaks, not in fragments (owner, 11 Nov 2026: "we lose the tone battle, [the format benchmark] always wins").** Measured on its 10 top Shorts against our 8 voices: its sentences run 14-25 words (ours 5-11), it stops about 10 times a minute (ours 16-26), never pauses half a second, and stresses half as many words. Every full stop makes the AI voice drop and restart, which sounds like an advert. So: 5-8 flowing sentences joined with "then", "but", "because", "and", "meaning"; no "X. Y. Z." triplets; no one-word sentences.
- **Script 1 to 1 on one format-benchmark Short (owner, 11 Nov 2026):** pick one of the benchmark's Shorts as the model, measure its sentences (count, words each, job of each), and write ours sentence for sentence. First model: "Why Your Brain Focuses On Negative Things" (38 s, 100 words, 8 sentences): one person does one odd thing -> a question -> the obvious answer -> "But" -> what nobody saw -> the meaning in one example -> "Because" -> "Meaning ..." returning to the first picture and speaking about "us". The last line gets the most care: the owner rejected six of Claude's endings and wrote his own on the model's pattern ("...can make us forget how much...").
## 3D (Blender)
- Bright toy look by default (pale-blue sky, soft daylight); cartoon outline approved for our first episode.
- One continuous world, camera holds joined by whips timed to the voice; no scene longer than 4 s and no still image held.
- **Quick flights, not snap whips** (owner, 2026-11-03, the chess episode): he dislikes the whip that flicks and swings the view; he likes the camera moving quickly from one place to another. Flights of about 0.4-0.55 s that leave and arrive at the holds' own speed, rise a little over the board, and turn the view smoothly (never chase a moving point). Light motion blur only.
- **The camera never stops, and moves twice as fast as ours did** (owner, 13 Nov 2026: "we need really to upgame our camera movement... most of [the format benchmark's] wins with nice camera movements across the whole video"). Measured with scripts/camera_motion.py on the format benchmark's 10 top Shorts + its "Negative Things" Short against our 10 finished Shorts (medians): camera moving 95 % of the time vs our 78 %; longest still moment 0.2 s vs 0.84 s (toast held still 2.2 s); slow moves cross 13 % of the picture width a second vs our 5 %; push-ins zoom 6 %/s vs 3.4 %/s. Our holds only crept 5 % of the way toward the subject. Rule: every framing carries its own move, the type changes each framing (push-in, slow orbit, crane, track alongside, pull-out reveal), nothing stands still over 0.3 s, and the preview is measured before the full render (aim: moving 90 %+, longest still 0.3 s or less, slow moves 10-18 % of the width a second). Notes: reference/creators/shorts-format/camera_motion.md. **Built into the blocky kit the same day** (scene.py `pick_moves` / `moved` / `move_fits`; owner approved the build): see sf-blocky-world, step 2.
- **The chess look for every Short** (owner, 13 Nov 2026: "The only thing chess and last clip have a nicer 3D quality look - I want to use that one"): the chess Short and the squirrel Short are the look to keep; every Short's final render uses `LOOK=chess`, checks and previews stay in the fast normal look.
- **The loop must not feel forced** (owner, 2026-11-03): never fly backwards or swerve to get back to frame one. Make the last move one more forward flight, e.g. into a live copy of the first station placed after the last one.
- **Pictures must carry the words, not hide them** (a former lead editor of a top channel on a podcast, 2026-10-27): fast cutting is losing viewers when it covers a weak story. Every new picture shows what the voice is saying at that moment; never add a cut or camera move just to look busy. Keep our one-camera, no-hard-cut world.
- **Copy structure, not someone else's look:** take a creator's story moves (stake, ladder, payoff), never their production style. Our bright toy-like 3D world is our identity.
- **The scene plan says what each scene should make the viewer feel** (curious, worried, surprised, "no way"), not only what it shows.
- **The series Easter egg:** one tiny water bottle mid-flip hidden somewhere in every episode's world (never in the hook's focal point, never covering the subject), the same model and colours each time. It rewards fans for re-watching and sends them to older episodes.
- Opening and ending mirror each other so the Short loops (measure: last vs first frame difference no bigger than a normal frame step).
- Nothing may pass through anything: run the CHECK overlap pass before every full render (it caught the bottle-through-brain and viewer-through-phone problems).
- Show the real thing faithfully (e.g. the record clip's 8 real shots), and make "impressive" tricks impressive (a lone cone landing is not).
- Real physics (rigid bodies baked to keys) for bounces, falls and showers; rocking settle on landings.
- Test stills, one per scene, go to the owner before the full render.
- **Blocky characters** (owner, 2026-11-05, look test after the October faceless growth leaders): the head is a smaller rounded can shape (not a big square box), bodies have adult proportions (legs and arms as long as the torso); the first try "looked like the kids in the town". The kit lives in templates/blender/blocky (cast.json, face bank, blocky_kit.py).

Because') a pause before it is fine and the video ends 0.02 s after it. **Never split one phrase with a pause** (owner, 14 Nov 2026, on "stopwatch [0.7 s] ticking": "Why again the ticking is lagging", then "just use the full word ticking from there and dont mess up the loop"): play the take unbroken to the end of its own last word and move the last camera move earlier instead, re-rendering only those frames (cleaning v16: 63 frames). - **Action reads at eye level, not from above** (owner, 13 Nov 2026: the top-view race scored 4/10 next to the real Short): film a race from fixed-ish cameras at waist height that the person tears through, and let the person do each job with the body (moves sprint, slide, dive, scoop), never things flying by themselves while they stand there. From above our figures are dark blobs.

- **'away A-B' hid the person from the very start** (14 Nov 2026, fixed in scene.py): Blender holds an object's first visibility key back to frame 0, so a key 'hidden at A' with nothing before it hid them from 0 to A too; the ice fan vanished mid-shot where the re-render began. ## Sound and edit
- Rotate the three music beds (sprint, clocks, steps); place the track so its final hit lands on the last word and frame one sits on a beat.
- Voice 14-18 LU above the music; master to -12 LUFS, true peak -1 dB; 2-5 soft whooshes on the big whips.
- A pause before a punchline must be truly silent (check the word timing against the waveform; Whisper can be 0.6 s early).
- **No-voice test and a sound for every action** (owner, 2026-11-06, survival-story recipe): before the final render, watch a preview with the voice muted (music and effects only); every beat must still read. Each on-screen action (a fall, a crack, a door, a phone buzz) gets its own real-sounding sound, on top of the 2-5 whooshes. Fix a failing beat with the picture or a sound, never with more words.

- **Long-video edit, owner's first review (the survival long video, v1, 7 Nov 2026):** (1) every hand-drawn circle must land on its target: position circles from known boxes, never from guessed screenshot pixels, and check a still; (2) no words may leave the frame (labels now clamp themselves inside 1920 px); (3) no empty page while the voice talks: whatever the voice promises appears as it is said; (4) never show the same graphic twice (the step cards appear once); (5) at most one short label on the creator's own clips, none on the emotional ones; (6) let the creator finish his thought: cut his clips at the end of the idea, not mid-way (15-40 s blocks like the creator-breakdown study); (7) scenes with our hero use the blocky world (WIDE=1 renders 16:9), not generated cartoon pictures; (8) real footage from the creator's own videos beats generated stand-ins for anything that happened in his videos; (9) no thumbnail as the end card; (10) stray breaths or clicks at the start or end of a voice piece are cut automatically; (11) a sentence comped from two takes can double a consonant at the join ("chickk"): re-cut it from one take; (12) the video must explain itself with the voice muted.

- **Lay Shorts out for the player's buttons** (2026-11-10, pause-2-seconds v10): on a 1080 x 1920 frame nothing important goes above y 235, right of x 890 below y 900 (like, comment, share), or below y 1480 (title and channel text). Captions sit at about y 1340-1480. Check every edit at 2 frames a second with those zones drawn on, not from a handful of stills. Board changes fade in place: page slides leave near-blank frames.
- **Fact Shorts use the saved board templates' look** (2026-11-10): the owner's saved motion-graphics board templates (not included in this kit), ported into Remotion at 9:16 (white dotted paper, soft cards, one accent per idea, everything lands on its word, a number rolls and stops while the voice says it). When the owner says "the templates we have", look there first. Compare two edits blind (critics not told which is newer): it is a fairer test than two separate scores.
- **Shared sound library first (owner, 7 Nov 2026):** every paid music cue and sound effect lives in assets/house_sound (config key "sound_library"; catalogue in its README.md): the 8 house music cues and 7 whiteboard effects from the "House sound" ElevenLabs board, plus 170 effects collected from earlier projects. Check it before generating any sound; save every new paid sound there straight away, named by what it is.

- **Voice tone = one calm storyteller (11 Nov 2026).** One mood tag for the whole read, no CAPS stress words, 2 pieces at most, and no phrase-by-phrase comping across moods. Generate the WHOLE script in one node (the squirrel episode, 11 Nov: 2 raw takes scored 8 of 9; the tags sit where the format benchmark's own delivery changes: [calm] at the start, [curious] on the "But" line, [thoughtful] on the example). Finish = one fixed volume and NOTHING else: no cuts or added silence inside a good take (the take brings its own 0.25-0.35 s breaths, like the format benchmark's). Capping gaps and loudness-normalising made a good take worse (6 of 9), and a silence placed from Whisper's word time cut a word's tail in two (owner heard it at once, 11 Nov): Whisper's times can be 0.2 s off, so any cut is placed by the sound level, never by the word times alone. Check with `voice_check.py <wav> format-real` (the format benchmark's real ranges; the older "format" mode was too soft and passed us). A free local draft voice (VibeVoice) first, so the owner hears the flow before spending credits.
- **The in-house voice replaced ElevenLabs** (14 Nov 2026, after five blind listening rounds; owner: "Lets use voice 1 method - its not perfect but it usable"). What he called "not crisp" was the DELIVERY ("Flat, low energy"; ElevenLabs "emphasis some where or part of words"), not the sound: EQ, super-resolution, an enhancer and a radio finish all failed by ear while every sound measure (tone, highs, consonants, echo, Whisper certainty) already matched. What worked: Chatterbox copying a LIVELY sample with more exaggeration (0.75), then a voice swap (Chatterbox VC) to the CALM sample to get the original speaker back, then ClearerVoice highs (`scripts/voice_make.py`). Compare voices only on the SAME sentence; ask the owner what he hears in plain words (muffled / roomy / fizzy / flat) before measuring more.
- **Cut the loop words off as soon as the takes arrive** (owner, 13 Nov 2026: "make sure you remove the audio that suppose to loop - you didn't do that in these clips"). The voice script reads on into the first line ("...because when you take ice") so the last word keeps its lift; every take the owner hears, and voice/final_voice.wav, must stop right after the last real word. Place the cut by the sound, not Whisper: for "because" where the s/z hiss ends (high-band energy drops), for "-ing" after the nasal tail, not at the first dip (a cut at the dip made "ticking" sound like "tick"). 12 ms fade, lead silence trimmed to 0.03 s. Check by transcribing a 3 s tail alone (the word must survive) and a loop test (last 3 s + first 3 s must read straight into "When you...").
- **A free AI voice must be cleaned up before use** (owner, 13 Nov 2026: Chatterbox sounded "like an old microphone inside his car" next to ElevenLabs' studio sound). Chatterbox and Qwen3-TTS make 24 kHz audio (nothing above ~12 kHz), 5-9 dB less crispness at 4-10 kHz and a little more boxy 500-1000 Hz; settings can't change that. Run a voice-polish script (the owner's `voice_polish.py`, not included in this kit) on `<take.wav> <out.wav>`: match EQ to the approved ElevenLabs takes, the missing top grown from the voice's own harmonics, de-essing only when clearly harsher than the reference (de-essing every s dulled it again), same loudness. It prints a 10-band table: aim within ~1.5 dB of the reference. Chatterbox try 2 cleaned: within ~1 dB in every band, s-sounds no harsher, every word intact.
- **No sonar ping at the start of any video** (owner, 12 Nov 2026). **The sea-wave transition** is a fast right-to-left wipe at one constant speed (0.4 s), not the slow wave crash.
- **Render in parallel** (owner, 12 Nov 2026: renders must take half or a quarter of the time): `scripts/render_parallel.py <scene.md> <out_dir> 6` runs six Blenders on six stretches of frames. Measured on the lab: 120 frames in 41 s against about 1.7 s a frame before, the same pictures (difference 0.006 of 255).

## Keeping the plugin up to date (for Claude)
- **Each plugin update copies the whole master folder into Claude's plugin cache and keeps every old copy** (12 Nov 2026: 57 copies of up to 6.6 GB filled the C: drive to zero). So: (1) nothing big lives in the master folder: look tests are in `<projects>/short-factory-lookdev`, renders and studies stay in the episode and studies folders; (2) do NOT bump the version for every lesson line: collect the changes of a working session and update once at the end; (3) after every update, delete the older version folders in `~/.claude/plugins/cache/rodiocean/short-factory/` (keep only the one `installed_plugins.json` points at) and check `df -h /c`.
- Claude runs an installed copy of the plugin. The master folder is the kit's `short-factory` folder; every script reads and writes the master tracker and reference files there (config.json "home").
- After changing anything in the master folder: raise the version in .claude-plugin/plugin.json, then `claude plugin marketplace update rodiocean` and `claude plugin update short-factory@rodiocean`; the change applies in the next session.
- If the kit folder moves: update every path in config.json, then `claude plugin marketplace remove rodiocean`, `claude plugin marketplace add <new short-factory path>`, `claude plugin install short-factory@rodiocean` (raise the version first).

- **Animals and lying people:** the camera aims at the real head (not a standing height), so an animal or someone flat on the ground is framed right; a scene that opens on a `sit` starts already seated.
- **Scripts must not read as AI** (owner, 2026-11-07, GPTZero: our v15 100% AI, the creator-breakdown study's transcript 100% human). The AI tells are the narrator explaining or summing up ("That's why…", "Notice the pattern…", "So from day one…"), neat triplets and short tidy sentences. Write events and concrete details, the narrator's own reactions in the first person, and the creator-breakdown rhythm: sentences median about 14 words with some over 25, a few "so / like / actually / right?", a couple of small pauses (…) and restarts (—) for the voice. Measure it before showing the owner.
- **AI detectors judge the whole script, not single lines** (owner asked us to check how GPTZero works, 2026-11-07; notes: reference/methods/ai-detection-notes.md). Line-by-line rewriting or rarer word choices don't clear it (an AI chapter edited sentence by sentence still scored 87 % AI; our v16/v17 stayed 100 %); explainer writing is flagged more; polish is itself a signal. What passes is text that started as a person talking. Method for long scripts: Claude writes NOTES (facts, beats, numbers, hooks), the owner talks each part in his own words, Claude only cuts, reorders and fixes facts, lightly.
- **No edit of an AI-written script has passed GPTZero** (2026-11-07): v15, v17 (creator-breakdown rhythm) and v18 (blader/humanizer skill) all 100 % AI. The humanizer still removes habits a viewer hears as generic, so use it for style, not to pass detectors. Only text that starts with the owner's own words can read as human to GPTZero.
- **What passes GPTZero (Model 5.0b, re-checked 2026-11-07):** the Didit text from 28 Oct still scores 86 % human (AI 14 %). Its recipe: short plain statements of WHAT HAPPENS ('he eats the gummy.', 'its a gold gummy.'), almost no commenting, summing up or explaining, no slogans, no lists of three; written like a quick message (lower case, 'its', 'thats', 'worked too good', no full stop at the end). Capitals and correct spelling are fine: the capitalised Didit text scored 94 % human, and the survival long video's opening written this way scored 99 % human (test G). Lower case or typos alone do NOT pass: 11 test passages that kept commentary lines ('I had to check that twice', 'I think it's three things') were all 100 % AI.
- **Short passes don't add up to a long pass** (2026-11-07): the plain style passed GPTZero at 140 words (99 % human) but the full 1,120-word script in the same exact form scored 100 % AI, and a 193-word block with a few extra framing lines also flipped to 100 % AI. A few AI-looking lines seem to tip the whole piece (owner's reading, and it matches the Kriukow experiment: halves at 0 % joined to 100 %). GPTZero's sentence colours follow its overall verdict, so they can't pinpoint the culprit lines. Don't chase a full AI-written script past GPTZero; spend the owner's scans only on text that starts as his own words.
- **The owner's own read of a voice script is the best guide for the AI voice** (2026-11-07, skills/natural-ai-voice/references/owner-read-lessons.md): he slows down by himself on the dramatic lines and pauses in the right places (before the turn of a contrast, before a name, after a false lead); copy those pauses and stresses into the ElevenLabs tags. Written-in fillers trip a human reader (5 of 8 skipped or swapped), so script 2-3 at most, only where he paused anyway.
- **Before every creator clip, ask the question it answers** (owner, 2026-11-08): a card or line like "How much time?", then he answers. A clip dropped in without one feels unrelated even when it isn't. - **Scene pieces that start mid-scene count their label times from the piece** (2026-11-08): label times written relative to a part of a scene must be wrapped so the part has its own clock, or every word appears before the voice says it. Check a part's first frames, not just its middle.

- **New channels break through with a burst spaced 1-3 days apart, not with a same-day dump** (2026-11-14, studies/niche_claude-code-video, first videos of 8 rival channels): Tensaikun 4 videos in 4 days (2 on day one, all 31K-193K); Mr omi 10 in 29 days, every 2-3 days (videos 1-2 flopped at 3.6K/7.6K, video 3 hit 121K, video 8 hit 1.05M); Sanji Nai-Chien 8 in 35 days; kaye.creatives 8 in 45 days.
