# MrBeast: how he does it

A study of his 10 most-viewed Shorts, his 10 most-viewed long videos, 3 weaker Shorts in the same formats, and the titles and views of all 205 Shorts and about 800 long videos. Made on 31 October 2026.

Each video was downloaded, transcribed word by word and split into voice and background. I measured cuts, loudness, speaking speed and sound effects, looked at frames from every shot, and had the hook critic score every opening. The numbers are in "mrbeast - all data.xlsx". The on-screen techniques table is techniques.md, and the story-block chart is story_blocks.png.

**What we can and can't take from him.** His power comes from money, his own face and real people reacting. We can't copy any of those (series rules: no real faces, no real footage). What we can take is the **structure**: how a stake is set up, how it climbs and how it pays off, plus his pacing and title formats.

---

## 1. The big picture

- **524 million subscribers. 205 Shorts with 87.2 billion views; about 800 long videos with 54.5 billion.** His Shorts now earn more views than his long videos.
- **No lottery.** His #1 Short is only **2.2 %** of all Shorts views, and the top 10 together are **15 %**. The typical Short (the median) gets **348 million**. Compare ColinAmazing, where one clip was 39 % of everything. This is a formula, not luck.
- **But it is fading.** Median views by upload age: oldest third **552M**, middle third **310M**, newest third **146M**. Part of that is just time (new videos haven't finished collecting views), but the best newest-third Short (1.2B) is also below the best older ones (1.9B).
- **Shorts sell the long videos.** "$100,000,000 Car Doors" ends with a "WATCH FULL VIDEO" card pointing to "$1 vs $100,000,000 Car!", his #3 long video.

## 2. Shorts: the recipe

### The numbers (top 10 Shorts), against Zack D. Films

| | MrBeast range | MrBeast typical | Zack typical |
|---|---|---|---|
| Length | 14–47 s | **39 s** | 30 s |
| Words | 45–153 | 89 | 85 |
| Speaking speed | 1.8–4.0 words/s | **3.0** | 2.8 |
| Voice starts | **0.0 s in 10 of 10** | | under 0.5 s |
| Cuts a minute | 0–58 | **40** (a new picture every 1.5 s) | none (one flying camera) |
| Median shot | 0.7–5.4 s | **about 1.1 s in 8 of 10** | |
| Voice above the background | -3.5 to +17 LU | **+5** (music and crowd loud) | +16 |
| Music under the voice | 73–100 % | 85 % | 90 % |
| Sound effects a minute | 4–32 | **9** | a few whooshes |
| Loops back to frame one | **0 of 7** measurable | | yes |

There are no pauses-over-half-a-second rules here: the hits pause 0–8 times, because people react on camera. His Shorts are **reality clips, not narration**, so voice and timing numbers don't carry over to us one to one. The cut speed and story shape do.

### Hook (first 3 seconds): hook critic average **7.3/10** for the hits, **5.4** for the 3 weaker ones

1. **The voice starts at 0.0 s** in all 13 videos measured. This alone doesn't separate the hits from the misses.
2. **A stake or challenge in the first words** in 8 of 10 hits: "If I give you $100, would you go to Paris…", "Guess the gift and you win it", "Can you slice a bullet with a katana?". The 3 weaker ones also have one (2 of 3). **The difference is in the picture, not the line.**
3. **The prize or object is big and readable by 1.2 s** in 8 of 10 hits, against 1 of 3 weaker ones (in those, the key and the prizes are specks).
4. **Camera movement or a cut by 1 s** in 7 of 10 hits, against 0 of 3. All three weak ones are a fixed camera on him talking.
5. **Someone other than the host on screen in the first second** in 6 of 10 hits, against 0 of 3.
6. **Captions on screen by 1.2 s** in 7 of 10 hits, against 1 of 3.
7. Best hook: **Katana Vs Bullet (8.8)**, a yes/no question in big text plus the slow-motion money shot with a red arrow at 0.8 s. **The ending is shown first.**

### Script as story blocks (chart: story_blocks.png)

| Block | Hits (of 10) | Weaker (of 3) |
|---|---|---|
| HOOK with an offer or challenge | 10 | 3 |
| TENSION, one try ("will it land?") | **9** | 1 |
| ESCALATION LADDER (stakes climb) | **6** | 1 (and it runs downhill: Mac → Switch) |
| SETBACK (a miss, a wrong guess) | 4 | 3 |
| WRONG BELIEF + "BUT" twist (Zack's spine) | **2** | 0 |
| PAYOFF as the highest point | **10** | 1 |
| Ends on a joke sting | **5** | 0 |
| LOOP | 0 | 0 |

His spine is **Offer → climb the stakes → one tense try → payoff → sting**, not Zack's "wrong belief → But". The weak Shorts sag in the middle: setbacks pile up with nothing rising. "Find This Key" even ends on a sponsor read after the payoff.

### Footage
- **Live action, handheld, often first-person** (his hand holding the $100 bill or the iPhone into the frame, 3 of 10).
- **Everyday wants as prizes:** iPhones in 4 of 10, cash in 5, a car, a baguette. A thing everyone wants, shown in hand.
- **A "game board" on screen** in the strongest: glowing green price tags on each car ($100K → $1M → $2M → $10M → $50M → $100M), money visible inside the glass case, a strength tower with face icons as a scoreboard.
- **Clean, bright, single-colour sets:** a white showroom, a red-and-white floor of subscriber names, a green garden. The hero object always has the strongest colour.

### Transitions and pace
- **Hard cuts, about every second** (40 a minute). No whips or fly-throughs. Slow motion for the key moment (bullet, coin flip).
- **The two one-shot hits** (Subscribe for an iPhone, $1M in Gifts) are under 19 s and fill the frame with the prize. The one-shot miss (Subscribe to Win $50,000, 95M) is a man talking with nothing to look at.

### Sound
- **Music almost always on (85 %) and loud:** the voice sits only about 5 LU above the background (Zack: 16). It's party energy, not a calm explainer.
- **About 9 sound effects a minute:** pops on the money tags, risers before the try, hits on the payoff. The streamer Short has 32 a minute.

### Captions and graphics
- **1–2 words at a time,** white bold italic with a black outline, centred low. Built for mute.
- **Money in glowing green** ("$100,000", "$20,000") popped onto the object.
- **Red arrows, red circles and red X marks** point at the one detail that matters (the bullet, the "not subscribed" answer).

### Titles (all 205 Shorts)
- **5–6 words, no emoji** (1 of 205 has one). A median of 5 words for the top 10.
- **Formats and their typical (median) views:**

| Format | Count | Median | Top 10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| "X vs Y" matchup (Katana Vs Bullet) | 13 | **553M** | 1 |
| "How many X to Y?" physics test | 9 | **552M** | 0 |
| "World's most / $100M object" | 15 | 483M | 1 |
| Guess / Spot / Find | 20 | 462M | 1 |

- **"Do X, Win $Y" is his most-used format but below his median.** The formats with a **visible test of a physical thing** (vs, how many, most expensive) have the highest medians. Titles with "?" have a median of 449M against 342M for the rest.
- **Remakes:** 6 titles were remade. The remake won 3 times ("Subscribe for an iPhone" 299M → 1.2B) and lost 3 times ("Guess The Gift" 1.1B → 654M). Every remake still got 209M or more.

- **Cold open:** the first sentence is "I + a huge action" in **9 of 10** (recreated, built, bought, rented, put 100 people, gathered 50). The prize and the rule follow within about 15 s. A **red line you must not cross** is the rule in 4.
- **"$1 vs $X" (4 of the top 10; the highest median of his recent title types, 307M):** he **shows the top tier first** ("This is a $1 billion super yacht"), lists the ladder, then **drops to the $1 version** and climbs back up. The price is a big glowing green number on screen in the first 2 s (3 of 4).
- **The biggest payoff is held to the end** in all the competitions (the winner is revealed in the last minute). "Last To Leave Circle" ends on a cliffhanger: "This video is actually two parts".
- **Sponsor / own-brand plug** in the middle, roughly 3:30–8:00 when present (Feastables chocolate at 3:35 and 7:28, others). It's never in the first minute.
- **Subscribe asks are tied to a reward** ("subscribe and you can be one of them", "subscribe so you can join us next time") in 5 of 10.
- **Pace: 26–37 cuts a minute and 2.7–3.5 words a second, for 10–41 minutes.** He never slows down.
- **Thumbnails: his face in 10 of 10** (we can't copy that). **Text in 6 of 10, always a number:** "$1 / $1B", "$1 / $100M", "DAY 97", "DAY #150". **Split screen** in the 4 "$1 vs" thumbnails: a cheap, broken version on the left and a gold, shiny version on the right.

## 4. Honest note

- His Shorts are **reality clips with real people**. We make **faceless 3D explainers**. Voice levels, pauses and the "host in frame" findings don't transfer.
- His formula is **expensive by design** (iPhones, cars, cash). For us the "prize" must become **a thing that rises in value or size on screen**, like a price tag that climbs, not real money.
- **The trend is down** in his newest third. The format still works (1.2B in the newest third) but hits less often.
- **Not measured:** like and comment rates, and upload dates of the full list (the age order comes from the channel order). Three hits have no loop number because their last frame couldn't be read.

## Save the Cat check, top 10 long videos (added 6 Nov 2026, owner's question)
His 10 most-viewed long videos (484M-979M) mapped onto Blake Snyder's 15 beats from the transcripts (a beat counts only with a timed line; spot-checked).
- **Close fit (12+ of 15): 5 of 10** (Grocery Store 14.5, 100 Days Trapped 14.5, 50 YouTubers 13.5, Last to Leave Circle 12.5, Squid Game 12). **Loose: 4** (Island 11.5, $1 Car 9.5, $1 Plane 9, $1 Yacht 8.5). **Not: 1** ($1 Hotel 7).
- **By format:** survival videos average 13.5 (a real low point, a family B story, an ending that answers the opening); elimination contests 12.7 (rounds = Fun and Games, a twist at the midpoint, a favourite out at 72-77 %); the **"$1 vs $X" ladders 8.5**: not three-act stories but price ladders with no midpoint, no All Is Lost and no Dark Night; they keep only the frame (open on the top tier, climb, "the most expensive…" at about 78-80 %, mirror the opening).
- **He cuts the slow first act everywhere:** the rules by 1 % ("if you touch the red line, it's game over", Circle 0:08) and the challenge started by about 2 %, not at Snyder's 10-12 %. The back half lands on the marks: midpoint about 50 %, the turn to the final about 80 %, a mirrored last line about 91-99 % ("A hundred days ago, they were strangers", 100 Days, 91 %).
- **Rare:** an early "save the cat" moment (3 of 10), a strong Dark Night (3 of 10: "God, I miss my family so bad", Grocery, 91 %), a stated theme (thin).
- For us: ladders for "how much / how big" topics; the full Save the Cat shape when we want a satisfying story, with the challenge started in the first seconds and one real setback at about 75 %.
- **Same beats every time?** Not quite. In his 5 closest fits every beat is present somewhere (one has no stated theme, one no mirrored last image); what moves is the timing: the Catalyst and Break into Two always come too early (squeezed into the first seconds), and All Is Lost / Dark Night are weak in 2 of 5. Always full in all 10: Opening Image, Set-Up, Fun and Games. The "$1 vs $X" ladders drop the same three every time: Midpoint, All Is Lost, Dark Night.
