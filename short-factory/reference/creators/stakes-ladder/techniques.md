Techniques (seen and measured):
1. **A stake in the first breath.** The voice starts at 0.0 s in 13 of 13. In 8 of the 10 hits the offer or challenge ("If I give you $100...", "Guess the gift and you win it") is said within 3.3 s.
2. **The prize is a physical object on screen**, not a number in the air: 9 of 10 hits (iPhone, $100 bill, price tag, cash case, gift pile, money in the glass). In 6 of 10 it is visible inside the first 3 s. The 95M contrast is just him talking.
3. **Fast pictures:** median shot 0.7-1.6 s in 8 of 10 hits; median of all 10 is 40 cuts a minute. The contrasts: 2.1 s, 2.8 s and one 14-second shot (0-20 cuts a minute). The two one-shot hits are giveaway announcements where the prize pile fills the frame.
4. **Ladder of rising stakes** in 6 of 10 hits ($100 -> $300; $100K -> $100M car doors). The 69M contrast runs its prizes the wrong way (Mac -> Switch).
5. **"Will it land?" tension** in 9 of 10 hits: one try, one coin, one phone call, one bullet. The viewer stays to see the outcome.
6. **Ends on a joke sting or the reveal, then stops:** sting in 5 of 10 (peanut butter, "just needed one", first Ferrari, "don't talk to anyone", iPhone car). No slow outro.
7. **On-screen money tags:** glowing green dollar figures pop on the object ($100,000 ... $100,000,000; $20,000 on the glass case), red arrows and red X marks point at the one detail that matters.
8. **Captions:** 1-2 words, white bold italic with a black outline, centred low; built to be read muted.
9. **No loop:** none of the 7 measurable hits returns to its first frame (seam 14-103 against a loop limit of 12). He ends on the punchline instead.
10. **Shorts sell the long video:** the car-doors Short ends with a "WATCH FULL VIDEO" card and an arrow; the $1 vs $100M Car video is his #3 long video.
