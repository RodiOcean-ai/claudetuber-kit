# What we learned from Isaac (@isaacverse) — 29 Oct 2026

**The channel:** 231,000 subscribers from only 9 long videos, each with 154k to 1.06M views. It's faceless, every word is an AI voice (ElevenLabs), with a cartoon mascot and heavy editing. He teaches how to make videos, and every tutorial is secretly a story.

## The 5 biggest lessons
1. **An AI voice sounds human when it's built from pieces.**
   - Generate 2-4 sentences at a time, keep 3 takes of each, and combine the liveliest phrases.
   - Keep pauses only on the key points, put ONE stressed word in capitals, and use "!" and "..." for feeling.
   - The proof is in his numbers. His 2023 videos, made before this method, are nearly flat in tone. Since 2024 his voice moves about five times more between phrases, and the speed varies twice as much.
2. **Under every tutorial there's a story.**
   - The narrator wants something and a mentor friend pushes him.
   - He hits a low point, where the real, emotional problem shows up (perfectionism, patience, fear).
   - The ending flips the topic.
   - Viewers stay for the story and learn on the way: he calls it the Trojan horse.
3. **A series beats a viral hit.** His 3 most-viewed videos share one title format, "How I Actually..." (4 of his 9 use it). A viewer who likes one watches the rest, and each new episode lifts the old ones.
4. **Music is his comedy tool.**
   - The music cuts out right before a punchline, then slams back on the next line. A long video does this 5-15 times.
   - The emotional ending starts with 7-8 seconds of near-silence.
5. **Value over retention.** He refuses to cut useful parts or add rage bait to please the retention chart, and says it in the video. Viewers trust him for it.

## His numbers (median of 9 videos)
- **Cuts:** 14 cuts a minute; average shot about 4 seconds; something new on screen every 2-4 seconds.
- **Voice and music:** the voice is about 9 steps above the music, and music plays 90 % of the time.
- **Speaking speed:** fast, about 3.6 words a second inside sentences.
- **Thumbnails:** the same mascot every time, 1-2 huge glowing yellow words or a big number, and one prop that tells the story.
- **Titles:** first person ("How I Actually...", "I Tried X for N Days!"), no emoji.
- **Descriptions:** a 2-line promise, chapters written as search phrases, and a free guide link.
- **Weak spot we won't copy:** his videos are mixed too quietly and clip at the peaks.

## The shorts lesson (he set out to make shorts like Zack D. Films)
- The script structure was copied easily. The pictures were the gap, so he moved to one consistent AI art style with a hand-drawn storyboard.
- Judge each short after 24 hours on two numbers: viewers who chose to watch (aim for 80 %+) and retention (aim for about 90 %).
