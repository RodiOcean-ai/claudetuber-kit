# Dude Perfect study (@dudeperfect, 62.9M subscribers)

Made on 15 November 2026. Studied: the **top 10 Shorts** and **top 10 long videos** by views, plus **3 weak Shorts** from the same channel picked for contrast. All 23 measured by machine (cuts, loudness, voice/music split, Whisper words, voice style, sound classifier, hook frames); Claude then looked at a 24-frame sheet of every Short, the first 30 s of 5 long videos, all transcripts and all covers. Channel list numbers are YouTube's on 15 Nov 2026 (one list); upload dates come from each video's own page data and confirm the list's order.

Data: studies/dudeperfect ("dudeperfect - all data.xlsx", analysis/<id>/), copies of the workbook and both sheets in this folder, story_blocks.png.

## The big picture
- **Five friends who do sport trick shots**, filmed in their own big indoor arena (walls with the logo, a court, a turf pitch, a stairwell). Live action, no narrator, no captions: the voices you hear are the players reacting.
- **62.9M subscribers. 384 long videos (17.5B views, median 28M, 36 over 100M) and 242 Shorts (4.2B views, median 5.25M, 82 over 10M, 8 over 100M).** The top 10 Shorts are 35 % of all Shorts views (1.47B of 4.22B), so it is a deep catalogue, not one lucky clip (ColinAmazing's record was 39 % on its own).
- **Biggest in its niche by a wide margin:** the next trick-shot channel we checked, How Ridiculous, has about 24M subscribers (one outside source, October 2026; not checked twice).
- **The long-video empire is old:** all 10 top long videos were made between Feb 2015 and Jan 2019. The newest 25 long videos have a median of 6.8M; numbers 51-150 back in the list (newest first) have 22.5M.
- **2026: many more Shorts, far fewer views each.** After 25 Feb 2026 they posted 121 Shorts (about 14 a month); the 121 before them took about four years (2-3 a month; the 4th oldest is from June 2022). The typical Short fell from **10.0M to 2.8M** views (median of the older 121 vs the newest 121), and the newest 25 sit at **1.2M**. All 3 weak Shorts come from this period.

## Hits vs weak: the psychology
The owner's question: why does this channel get far more views than others in its niche, and what do its hits do that its weak videos don't? Every line below is a count from what we saw. "Hits" = the 10 most-viewed Shorts (94M-281M). "Weak" = 3 Shorts from Oct-Nov 2026 with 0.28M-0.57M. **Read the honest note first: the weak ones are young (1, 4 and 30 days old) and from the channel's low period, so age and era are mixed in with craft.**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One person at a time, big in the picture, aiming at one target you can see, and the deciding moment lands in the middle of the frame** (the puck through the tiny slot, the Oreo into a cup, the diving catch, the ball bursting on the bounce) | **10** | **0** | **Mirror-neuron suspense:** watching one body throw, your own body rehearses the throw, so you feel the miss and the make. **Prediction and surprise:** a visible target lets the brain bet on the outcome in the first second; the answer then breaks or beats the bet. **What is NOT there (0 of 13 Shorts):** outrage, fear, a "you" opening, a statistic opening, word-by-word captions, a designed loop (every last frame differs from the first by 25-100 on a 0-255 scale, against 0-8.5 for a normal step between frames). Fear of missing out shows up only in packaging, not inside the videos.

**What does NOT separate hits from weak** (both have it): a scoreboard or try counter on screen (4 of 10 hits, 2 of 3 weak: the drone game's face board, the derby's 10-ball tracker); fast cutting (hits median 15.2 cuts a minute; the drone Short is the fastest of all 13 at 31); voice in the first half second (8 of 10, 2 of 3); a bright first frame (hits median 110 of 255, weak 128); likes per view (hits median 2.6 %, weak 2.2-3.5 %); music (see below).

### Why this channel beats the rest of its niche (what we can count)
1. **A tribe built over 15 years.** The first upload ("Backyard Edition | Our 1st Video!") has 45M. 8 Shorts have "Dude Perfect" in the title: median **28.5M**, 5x the channel's 5.25M. Five of them promise a look behind the curtain ("EXPOSED", "Faked It!"): 15M-68M, strong in both periods (among the newest 121: 48M and 15M against a 2.8M median). **Identity and curiosity:** fans want to belong and to see the secret of the trick.
2. **A format bank they reuse.** The easy/hard/impossible wall made 3 of the top 10 Shorts (281M, 127M, 98M). "Impossible" in a title: 13 Shorts, median **11M** (2x); it holds in both periods (newest 121: 7.2M vs 2.8M; older 121: 17M vs 10M). Five of the top 10 long videos are sequels ("2", "3").
3. **Stars and pro sets no small channel can get.** A Stanley Cup, a Champions League pitch with PlayStation branding, an NBA arena: 5 of 10 hits. **But a famous name in the title is no edge:** 31 Shorts with a star's name or an @tag have a median of 5.0M, the same as the channel (5.25M). The star must deliver the payoff on screen.
4. **Wordless sport moments anyone understands:** median 34 words, nothing to translate, a ball and a target everybody has played with.
5. **Volume, which is now working against them:** 14 Shorts a month in 2026 and the typical Short down from 10.0M to 2.8M.

## Hooks (first 3 seconds)
- **Something about to move, big in the middle, at 0 s** in 10 of 10: a hand pressing the puck through the tiny "IMPOSSIBLE" slot; the Oreo already rolling down the table toward six labelled cups; a football at the moment of the throw; a silver ball on its way to the floor; a ping pong ball in a rally; a ball already in the air above the stairs with a 1-12 meter on the left; a hammer over a diamond with the question "Can you break a diamond with a hammer?" written on screen.
- **The rule announced in the first 3 s** in 4 of 10 ("We got easy, we got hard, and we've got impossible." / "Easy, hard, and impossible." twice / "Level 1 to 12."), plus the written question in 1. Weak: 1 of 3 ("Hide and seek! Easy game.").
- **Other first lines are reactions, not narration:** "Check this out.", "Three, two, one!", "Yes!"; 2 hits say nothing at all in the first 3 s (the ping pong rally, the sequel).
- **Weak openings:** a drone sitting still on a red pad with five face icons and a "RING DRONE CAM" label (a product placement); a wide shot of six crouching people around a ball with no word until 3.7 s; a batter seen small from behind, ball invisible.
- Claude's hook scores (the hook-oracle agent was not run in this step): hits 7-8.5 (median 8; best: the diamond question 8.5); weak 4-5.

## Script formula (story blocks, chart in story_blocks.png)
| Block | Hits (of 10) | Weak (of 3) |

- **The engine is a bet repeated:** the picture asks "will it go in?" every few seconds and answers it on screen. The best Shorts stack the bets into a climb (easy → hard → impossible → the pro), and save the hardest for the last 8-23 % (the makes land at 77 %, 83 %, 92 % and 92 % of the way through the wall and stair Shorts).
- **The setback-then-rescue arc** (3 of 10, all wall Shorts): the member misses the impossible hole 24-30 times on the counter, says "above my pay grade" or "time to bring in the big guns", the specialist walks in under a name card, and lands it on try 34, 30 or 21. A subscribe button pops up on or just after the make in 2 of them.
- **Very short ones are pure surprise:** the two 8-second Shorts (154M and 119M) are one burst + the laughing pranksters + his face, nothing else.

## Footage and pictures
- Live action, phones and handheld cameras, the five members plus staff and guests; no animation, no narrator.
- **Fixed camera aimed at the target** in the wall, stairs, Oreo and ping pong Shorts (6 of 10): the frame never moves, so the target is always in the same place and the eye only has to watch the ball.
- **Two hits are one unbroken take** (the Oreo roulette, the run through the building): no cut means no cheating, which matters for trick shots (they even have Shorts titled "Dude Perfect Faked It!").
- **The set is a game board:** coloured walls with three holes and green rings on the made hole, labelled cups, a 1-12 meter of circles turning green, yellow, red.
- On-screen words are labels only: try counters (4 of 10), name cards (3), a question (1), cup labels (1), an emoji sticker (1), a subscribe button at the make (2).

## Sound
- **Music is not what makes the hits.** 6 of 10 have a music bed (66-100 % of the time), 4 have none at all (the run through the building, the ping pong rally, the stairs, the diamond). All 3 weak Shorts have music 80 %+.
- Two of the wall Shorts use the same vocal pop song (Whisper picked up its words), and the third has a bed at the same tempo (117.5 BPM): the template comes with its own song.
- **Real sound does the work:** puck and ball impacts, footsteps, sneaker squeaks, cheers. The classifier hears about 18 such sounds a minute in the hits (its labels are rough: puck hits come out as "chopping").
- The voice sits only about 3 LU above everything else: you hear the room, not a presenter.

## Captions
- **None.** 0 of 13 Shorts have word-by-word captions. The hits don't need them: the story is the ball.

## Titles and covers
- Hit titles: 10 of 10 have an emoji (🤯 in 5); 7 are a reaction ("CRAZIEST IMPOSSIBLE SHOT YET", "bro was just doing his routine 😭🙏🏻"), 2 a question, 1 a game name with 🤢. Weak: game names, 1 of 3 with an emoji.
- Channel-wide (242 Shorts): emoji median 5.6M vs 4.4M without; a "?" median 3.5M vs 5.8M (questions do worse overall); "Dude Perfect" in the title 28.5M; "impossible" 11M; a star's name or @tag 5.0M.
- **Shorts covers** (the feed rarely shows them; the channel page and search do): **4 of 10 hits have a designed cover**, two-colour block text with white arrows or a red circle on the ball ("IMPOSSIBLE HOCKEY WALL", "IMPOSSIBLE GOLF WALL", "IMPOSSIBLE SOCCER WALL", "BRO DID IT AGAIN" with a laughing emoji); the other 6 hits and **all 3 weak ones are plain frames** from the video.
- Long-video covers: the members around the big object (rockets, a sandcastle), a trick mid-air, the logo; one hero object in most.

## Long videos (all 10 from 2015-2019)
- 4.3-7.9 minutes (median 6.2). 8 of 10 titled "[Thing] | Dude Perfect"; **5 of 10 are sequels** ("Water Bottle Flip 2" 692M, "Real Life Trick Shots 2", "Ping Pong Trick Shots 3", the Pringles one = Ping Pong 2, "Model Rocket Battle 2").
- **7 of 10 are trick-shot compilations**: a new trick about every 9-10 s, each one a small "will it go in?" bet; 1 RC-car course, 1 rocket contest with a scoreboard of faces, 1 "Stereotypes" comedy skit.
- **Opens on a trick or a launch in motion at 0 s** in 9 of 10 ("This is the second story bounce back.", "Launching in 3, 2, 1!"); the skit opens on a gag. "What's up guys, we're Dude Perfect, welcome to…" in the first 45 s in 3.
- Ends: "thanks for watching", subscribe, merch or next video, and the "pound it" sign-off in 6 of 10; a sponsor thanked at the end in 2 (a jeweller, an RC-car maker).
- Machine numbers: a cut every 4.1 s, 1.6 words a second, music under 94 % of the time, voice about level with the music (0.2 LU), 4.5 action sounds a minute, -12.7 LUFS.

## Honest note
- **The weak side is small and young.** Three Shorts, two of them only 1 and 4 days old (they may still grow) and the third a month old; all from 2026, when every Dude Perfect Short does about a quarter of what it used to. Part of the gap is age and era, not craft. A fairer test: download 3-4 of the best 2026 Shorts ("This Trick Shot Almost Made Him QUIT" 82M, "Rocket League In Real Life" 78M, "Break The Glass, YOU LOSE" 49M) and 3-4 of the worst that are over a month old ("DON'T Go Over 1 Minute!" 0.56M, "POOL FIGHT!" 0.66M, "Can We Teach A Bear to Subscribe?" 0.68M, "Bro Is The GREATEST Athlete" 0.68M).
- "One person, one visible target", "the difficulty climbs" and "a star on screen" are Claude's reading of the frame sheets; words, sounds, cuts, loudness and the loop seam are machine counts. Story-block levels are judgement; the block counts are counted.
- The title and era numbers are from YouTube's list for this channel (one source). How Ridiculous's 24M is one outside source.
- Views are not craft: topic, age, the feed's mood and the brand are tangled. These are things the hits share and the weak ones lack, not proven causes.
- Real people are in every video: we copy the structure, never a face or a likeness, and nothing downloaded goes into our videos. Stars are named here only from on-screen name cards.
- **Kids rule check:** this is a family-friendly sports channel, not a channel made for children; 0 of 13 Shorts star a child (one long video shows a toddler for a few seconds). The owner decides whether it fits the "no kids' channels" rule.
- No weak long videos were downloaded, so the long-video section describes the hits only.
