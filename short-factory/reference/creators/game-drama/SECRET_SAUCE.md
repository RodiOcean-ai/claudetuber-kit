# GothamChess: the secret sauce (2 Nov 2026)

## The recipe in one paragraph
**Turn a chess moment into a tiny drama. Say a feeling or a broken rule in the first breath ("I hate…", "I'm sorry…", "kings can touch?"), and make the subject obvious by 1.6 s. Then let something go wrong on the board (a blunder, a betrayal, the clock running out) before it lands anyway, and stop hard on the punchline.** The board is always on screen and shows the story: highlighted squares, a red arrow, and a live number (the rating, the clock) that rises and falls. He does it with his own face and famous players. We do it with a 3D board, faceless pieces and a glowing counter.

## Format, not luck
- #1 Short = **1.3 %** of all 4.47B Shorts views; top 10 = **10.8 %**. That's the most spread-out channel we have studied (MrBeast 2.2 % / 15 %, ColinAmazing 39 % for one clip). His median Short gets 1.5M across 1,216 Shorts.
- Remakes: **"WIN / CHECKMATE in N moves" was made 13 times**. Three passed 18M (44M, 29M, 18M) and the median is 6.0M, four times his channel median.
- **No decay:** the newest third of Shorts has a median of 1.7M against 1.85M for the oldest third, with 31 Shorts over 10M in the newest third.
- The same opening line, two results: "I'm done with this." (44M) vs "I'm done. Bye." (1.0M). The words are the same; the hit shows its subject (the chess.com logo, "a bot of me") at 1.6 s and the miss doesn't. This is the cleanest proof in the study.

Compared with Zack (our format authority): GothamChess talks faster (3.5 words/s against 2.8), keeps the music close under the voice (10 LU against 16), uses hard cuts and **doesn't loop** (0 of 2 measured). None of these separate his hits from his misses, so Zack keeps the final word on all of them. **The real difference is the story:** Zack's top 10 have a setback in only 3 of 10 and a ladder in 8 of 10. GothamChess's hits have a setback in 7 of 10, and his misses have none. For a story about a *game*, a setback beats a smooth climb.

## What we copy (the craft)
1. **The live counter that falls and rises**: a glowing rating or clock number on the board (our "game board"), dropping on each mistake and jumping on the payoff. It fits our climbing-number tag from MrBeast, but it's allowed to fall before the final jump.
2. **One setback beat inside the ladder**: a blunder or a "no, not that move" moment between 40 % and 70 % of the Short, then the comeback.
3. **Subject clear by 1.6 s**: frame one already shows the board, the piece or the hero object. Never a blank face, a dark room or a borrowed clip.
4. **A feeling or rule-break word in the first line**, kept inside our "you" opening: "If you've ever lost a game of chess, you'd hate this…"
5. **Board language**: yellow squares for the last move, one red arrow or ring on the key piece, a clock with seconds ticking (tick-tock sound).
6. **Black-and-white "bad moment" that snaps back to colour** for the setback (2 of 10). This is cheap in 3D: a desaturate key in the edit.
7. **Short titles with a number promise**, inside our question + one emoji pattern ("How Did a Chess Teacher Get 8.4 Million Subscribers? ♟️").
8. **Comment-question ending, moved to the pinned comment.** His "Do you agree?" and "which one next?" endings are the reason the pinned-comment ask already sits in our post kits.

## What we don't copy
- His face, name, voice, "Ladies and gentlemen" or "Get out of here", his reaction-cam layout, real footage of him or of real players (Magnus, Hikaru, Botez). Series rules: faceless figures, and the creator stays anonymous until the last line.
- **Claims about real people** ("partying drunk"). That's a factual and fairness risk for us.
- **Talking speed 3.5 words/s, music only 10 LU under the voice, hard cuts, no loop.** Zack's format wins: about 2.8 words/s, +16 LU, whips, loop to frame one.
- "Click the link below" endings and course plugs.
- Famous faces as the hook. Two of his weakest hooks (4.8) still hit 41M+ because Magnus was in frame. We have no star, so the board and the counter must do that job.

## How this changes our next Short
Frame one: **a 3D chess board with a glowing counter already on it**, the subject clear at once. Line one: **a you-situation with a feeling word**. The middle: **the counter climbs, then a blunder knocks it down** (the screen goes grey for a beat, the counter drops, there's a "no, not that move" moment), then the comeback. Pay off at the biggest number and stop hard. Loop to the board. In the pinned comment: "Which creator should we take apart next?"
