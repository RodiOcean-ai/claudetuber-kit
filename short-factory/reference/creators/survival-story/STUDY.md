# Dr Plants study (@DrPlants, 5.97M subscribers)

A study of **all 12 of his videos** (17M-123M views each, 8-14 minutes), with his own words from a 42-minute Think Media interview (INTERVIEW_NOTES.md). **The channel has no Shorts.** Made on 6 November 2026.

## The big picture
- A 21-year-old who builds a small world in a glass tank (a desert, a forest, an ocean, an ant colony), puts real animals in it and tells what happens over 100 days as a **survival story**. Live action, macro lenses, his own narration, his face only in the vlog moments.
- **12 videos, 852M views in all.** The median video has **81M**; the weakest has 17M. 3-4 uploads a year (2022: 1, 2023: 3, 2024: 4, 2025: 3, 2026: 1). He says he posts "whenever it's done".
- Spread, not one lucky clip: the #1 video (Egg, 123M) is **14 %** of all views (Paddy 20 %, ColinAmazing's record 39 %). 8 of 12 are over 50M.
- **Growing, not fading:** views per month of age rose from 0.85M (Jungle, 2022) to 2.1-2.9M (2023-24) to 4.7M (Ant), 5.4M (Bees) and **10.0M (Egg, Nov 2025)**. The newest (Apocalypse, May 2026) is slower at 3.0M a month.
- **The craft is one template, and the template changed once.** His first video (Jungle, 2022) is a calm show-and-tell diary: talked wall-to-wall, music under 42 % of it. From the next one on (Desert, Feb 2023: 117M) every video is a **survival movie**: a trailer cold open, a clock, a villain, setbacks, music under 94 % of it and the voice stepping back. Same title format, same tank, 43M → 117M.

He is the **slowest, quietest-voiced, most music-led** channel we have studied. Inside a phrase he talks at Zack's speed (2.8 words a second), but he stops often: about 5 holds of a second or more every minute, and a sixth of every video has no voice at all, only music and sound.

## Hooks (cold open, first 30 s): hook critic average **6.1/10** for the top 10, **5.05** for the 2 weaker
1. **A trailer cold open** in 12 of 12: fast clips of the whole video (a new shot every 1.8 s) under 5-8 slow lines, then a rewind: "we must go back to day one" (said in 4) or a day card on screen ("DAY 1", "DAY 3", "99 → 100 DAYS") in 3 more.
2. **The first words at 0 s** in 11 of 12, and the line points at the subject: "Here is the world's biggest egg… and here is one of the smallest." / "This is a queen ant and she has the next thousand days to create an empire." / "Here are 10,000 bees, and over the next hundred days they'll live in my home."
3. **A clock in the first 10 s** (100 days, 1,000 days, "a year ago today") in 7 of 12, in the very first sentence in 4; a day count said out loud later in 9 of 12 ("By day 50…", "On day 94…").
4. **A "But" or catch within 10 s** in 9 of 12: "But a rival colony is watching their every move." / "The only catch? She's lived her entire life in a pet store." / "But as they grew, so did the problems."
5. **Survival or death named in the first 20 s** in 9 of 12: "This desert is a dangerous place." / "fighting fear, death and war" / "Or is it all doomed to die?" Missing in exactly the three slowest-earning videos (Jungle, Planet, Frogs).
6. **A promise of change:** "I promise you will never see hamsters the same way again"; "the most expensive, dangerous and emotionally draining video I've ever made". A flash-forward line that pays off later (Egg's "I can't do this anymore" at 0:26 comes back at 12:50).
- **Best hooks have one living hero on frame one:** ant on white 8.2, hamster 7.7, bees 7.6, egg 7.2. **Wide tank shots score 4.9-6.0** (Desert 117M scores 4.9: the title and cover carry it, not the cold open).
- **His leaks:** the hero hidden in a wide shot; the question arriving at 14-19 s; the trailer showing the ending (the adult ostrich by 10 s). A number is on screen in the first 5 s in only 2 of 12.

## Script formula (story blocks, chart in story_blocks.png)
| Block | Top 10 | 2 weaker | 3 slowest per month (Jungle, Planet, Frogs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| HOOK: trailer + subject + clock | 10 | 2 | 3 |
| "BUT"/catch in the first 10 s (Ocean's at 11 s) | 7 | 2 | 1 |
| TEASE ("…the danger I didn't see coming", "only just begun") | 9 | 2 | 2 |
| LADDER: each step a new animal, problem or test | 10 | 2 | 3 |
| …built as **problem → "I needed a predator / a fix" → "Meet the…" → new problem** | 7 | 1 | 2 |
| TENSION scenes (a stalemate, "who would make the first move?") | 9 | 2 | 2 |
| SETBACK (things get worse: a war lost, a storm, a death) | 8 | 2 | 1 |
| COMEBACK after a setback | 4 | 1 | 1 |
| PAYOFF that answers the opening question | 6 | 2 | 1 |
| Ending that **returns to the opening with a changed meaning** (cycle, "the one thing we got right") | 6 | 1 | 0 |

- **His engine is And-But-Therefore with animals.** A population booms ("the beetles were breeding faster than the scorpion could eat them") **so** he brings a predator ("I needed something deadly, something fast… Let me introduce you to the Dune Scorpion"), **but** the predator creates the next problem. "I needed…" is said in 5 of 10 hits, "Meet the… / Let me introduce you to…" in 5 of 10 (12 times in Desert, Forest, Ocean and Africa alone).
- **One real loss, about two-thirds in.** Peanut the ostrich dies at 10:51 of 14:18 (76 %); the ant colony is overrun at 2:05 and again at 6:30; most of the bees die at 5:46 of 9:20 (62 %); the forest burns at 9:19 of 10:18 (90 %). A character or colony we followed dies in 6 of 12 (5 of the top 10). He says this is planned: "I plan out the emotion… at each moment", sympathy → awe → stress → excitement.
- **Weather as the act-three disaster** in 5 of 12: a flood, a sandstorm, a monsoon, a hurricane, a wildfire, fallout and a mudslide.
- **Facts dressed as plot.** Facts arrive as a turn in the story, never as a list: "But ants are one of the few animals that respect their dead. So the remaining few build a graveyard." (that exact line is reused word for word in the bee video); "They're turning the wind into water."; "They have three superpowers" ("superpower" in 3 videos). The interview: "I only say that fact if it pushes the story forward."
- **Signature phrases** (counts across all 12): a foreshadow line ("didn't realize", "was about to", "only just begun", "little did I know") in **11**; "by day N" in 9; "Or so I thought" / "Or not?" right after a safe-looking moment in 7; the **mother-in-law joke** in 5 ("Something almost as terrifying as my mother-in-law", "the charm of my mother-in-law"), his running gag; "for good reason / with good reason" in 2.

## Sentence rhythm and word choice
- **Short sentences in waves.** Whisper sentences average 10 words (spread 6.4); in the captions 8-42 % of sentences are 1-5 words (median 29 %) ("Everything depends on her." "It is his home." "Queen. Queen. Queen."). A long setup sentence, then 2-4 very short ones.
- **Triples and pairs in the cold open** in 9 of 12: "my space, my sleep and my sanity"; "fear, death and war"; "predators and prey. Wind and rain."; "resilient yet ruthless".
- **"But" about 1.5 times a minute** in the hits (Egg 3.3, Ant 2.6, Bees 2.7), half of them opening a sentence. The slowest earners use it least (Jungle 0.6, Planet 0.4).
- **Drama words** (war, empire, queen, death, doomed, enemy, survive, predator): median 2.0 per 100 words in the 9 videos that earn most per month (Egg and Hamster lower, 0.6-0.7), 1.0 or less in all 3 weak ones (Jungle, Planet, Frogs). Ant (4.5) and Bees (3.9) are written like war films: "empire", "army", "fortress", "the enemy had already made their way underground".
- **Present tense for danger, past tense for the diary.** He drops into the present for the tense scenes ("He is being hunted. His only chance of survival…") and back into the past for the setup.
- Very few fillers (1.4 a minute): the narration is scripted. The weaker videos are chattier (Planet 3.3, Frogs 2.8, Jungle 2.9 a minute) because they are lighter, vlog-style.

## Voice and tone (his own voice, measured on the voice stem)
- A young male voice, median 139 Hz, a wide range (14 semitones between his low and high notes), **2.2 semitones of swing from phrase to phrase** (Zack and Isaac 1.3-3.2: normal-lively), speed changing 23 % from phrase to phrase, about 24 stressed words a minute.
- **Two voices layered:** the slow, low, "nature-film trailer" narrator and his live on-camera reactions ("Oh, my God!" 15 times in the Egg video; "Ow!", "Bro… that's actually so insane"), which are shown as small captions.
- **The hold is his main tool:** about 5 pauses of 1 s+ a minute (Zack 0, Isaac 1.5-3.5). The music fills them.

## Music
- **Always on** (95 % of the time in the hits), **loud** (only about 7 dB under the voice while he talks, against Zack's 16), and **it swells when he stops talking** (+5.9 dB on average, 12 of 12).
- Style: cinematic strings and plucked guitar (the classifier hears violin, bowed strings, guitar and plucked strings most), 108-130 BPM. **Licensed famous songs** at the emotional peaks in 3 videos: "The End of the World" (Sharon Van Etten) over the Africa ending, "Paradise" (Coldplay) and "Thrift Shop" in the frog video; free cinematic tracks (Scott Buckley) in the 2022 one.
- **Short dips, not big cuts:** about 1.6 dips a minute, each about 0.9 s and 17 dB deep, and **94 % are followed by a spoken line within 1 s** (200 of 213). But they are not saved for punchlines: a dip comes before 10 % of all sentences and before 13-33 % of jokes, reveals and "But" turns (mother-in-law jokes 2 of 11, "Meet the…" 2 of 15, death lines 2 of 9). **The "music drops out before the punchline" idea is not his rule.** His rule is the opposite: the music gets louder in the gaps.
- In the 2022 video (Jungle) the music was audible only 42 % of the time, 24 dB under a non-stop voice with 3 dips. From Desert on, it became the second narrator.

## Sound effects
- **Few designed effects** (booms, whooshes, impacts, heartbeats, sirens): about 2 a minute, 3 in the first minute. The most common across all 12: low booms (57), whooshes (27), knocks and doors (29), heartbeats (15, e.g. on a hatching egg), thunder and rain in the storms, a siren on the night drive to the vet.
- **Real sound does the work:** animal calls, quacks, crickets, rain and thunder, about 3 a minute, much of it recorded or made by him ("I'll go take a cabbage in front of a mic and rip it" for plants growing).
- His check (interview): "my final pass… I'll remove all voice over and see if it's watchable with just sound." Our numbers agree that the video is built to survive that: 17 % has no voice, the music swells in those gaps, and every action has its own sound.

## Footage and pictures
- **Macro close-ups** of the animal, eye-level, shallow focus, in most shots; **ultraviolet glow** scenes (scorpions, millipedes, coral) in 5 of 12; **"if we look underground" cross-sections** of burrows and nests in 5 of 12; time-lapses of plants and colonies.
- **The tank wide shot** on a dark or white set returns between chapters as the "map".
- **On-screen words are labels, not captions:** "DAY 78", "100X ZOOM", "CLOWNS / ZEBRAS", "COMING UP…", a red arrow on a venom drop, red circles on eggs. Small white captions only for his live speech.
- Scale objects: a coin next to a quail chick, a finger next to a shrimp, "a piece of pasta".
- **Frame one got brighter over time:** the 2022-23 videos open dark (frame-one brightness 61-116 of 255), 7 of 8 videos since 2024 open bright (128-214). His interview rule "brighter intros lead to higher retention" — but the dark-opening Desert, Forest and Ocean are still hits, so brightness doesn't separate hits from misses here.

## Titles and covers
- **"Simulating [a real place] for [N] Days"**: 6 videos, **median 83M**; all other titles: median 44M. A day count in the title: 7 videos, median 82M. Titles are short: 22-38 characters in 11 of 12 (Egg: 53), matching his "under 52 characters" rule.
- **Covers:** a **DAY 1 → DAY 100 split** (or PEACE → WAR) with a white arrow in 6 of 12 (median 84.5M); one hero subject big in the middle (egg, ant, queen bee, hamster, crab, frog) in 6 of 12, the before → after tank in most others; 0-2 words (12 of 12); a red circle on the one detail that matters in 3; a "life?" question word in 2. **His face** in 1 (Planet, 34M).
- **Same cover, different result:** Forest (84M) and Apocalypse (17M) use the same cover idea (a sprout in a red circle among death + "life?"). Like Paddy's same-title pair, the idea, not the packaging, made the difference: a real forest you could own vs a make-believe apocalypse in a model house.

## Long-video structure (all 12 read in full from his own captions)
- Cold-open trailer (20-30 s) → "day one" → **5-12 chapters, each one a new animal or a new problem** → a weather disaster → a loss → a return to the opening.
- **Sponsor reads: none** in 12 of 12. **Ending:** "check out this video… see you in the next one" in 10 of 12; a donation ($10,000 to coral restoration) in 1.
- **The last payoff starts in the final 15 %** in 10 of 12 (the final war, the hamster's wheel, the burnt forest's sprout, the ranch).

## Contrast: what the strong videos have that the weak ones lack
Raw views favour old videos, so the fair comparison is **views per month of age**: 9 videos earned 2.1-10.0M a month; 3 earned under 1.1M (Jungle 2022, Planet 2024, Frogs 2024). The 2 lowest by total (Frogs 29M, Apocalypse 17M) are also shown.
| | 9 strong (2.1M+ a month) | 3 weak (under 1.1M a month) |
|---|---|---|
| Survival or death named in the first 20 s | **9 of 9** | **0 of 3** |
| A real setback in the middle | **9 of 9** | 1 of 3 |
| An ending that returns to the opening with a changed meaning | 7 of 9 | **0 of 3** |
| A day clock said out loud | 8 of 9 | 1 of 3 |
| Fillers ("so", "like", "okay") a minute | 0.6-2.6 | **2.8-3.3** |
| Speed inside a phrase | 2.6 words/s (median) | 2.9-3.3 |
| Hook critic | 6.0 (median) | 4.7 (median) |
| Premise | a real living thing that might not survive | a diary (Jungle), a list of 12 biomes (Planet), a stunt (frogs playing video games) |
- **Planet and Frogs have his modern sound** (music 84-97 %, 10-25 % voice-free, the same pauses) and still earned a third as much. So the sound is part of the house style, not what picks winners. **The idea and its stakes pick winners**: "can it survive?" beats "watch me do something funny".
- Apocalypse (17M, 6 months old) has stakes and setbacks but no hero you can love (a jumping spider shows up at 2:40) and a set nobody believes is real. It may be the "spectacle-driven" video he calls his worst in the interview; we can't confirm which one he meant.

## Honest note
- **No Shorts.** None of his pacing numbers apply to a 30 s Short; Zack stays the authority on format. What transfers: the survival premise, the hero + clock opening, the problem → predator chain, one loss, the ending that returns changed, facts as plot, and the "works without the voice" sound check.
- He reposted clips of all 12 to Facebook and Instagram (he says 600M views); we could not see or measure those.
- The sound-effect counter is a classifier: it hears loud, distinct sounds and labels them; soft foley under the music is missed, and some animal sounds may be his own added effects. Treat "about 2 designed effects a minute" as a floor.
- The voice/music split counts his live reactions and animal sounds on the voice side or the music side imperfectly; the "about 7 dB" is measured only while he talks.
- The Ocean video has YouTube's automatic captions (no punctuation); its sentence counts are left out of the rhythm numbers (Whisper was used instead).
- Views per month are lifetime averages; young videos look better than they will later.
- Story-block levels in the chart are our judgement, sentence by sentence; the block counts are counted, not judged.
