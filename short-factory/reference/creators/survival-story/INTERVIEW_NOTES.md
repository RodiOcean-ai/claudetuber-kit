# Dr Plants in his own words (first-hand evidence)

Source: Think Media Podcast, "He Got 6 Million Subscribers With Only 12 Videos" (youtu.be/iYPcnhNNjW0, 41:44, 182k views, recorded at VidSummit). Host: Sean Cannell (not studied). Interviewee: Dr Plants (@DrPlants), 21, 5.97M subscribers, 12 long videos, no Shorts tab.
Read from YouTube's own transcript (auto captions, so a few words are garbled). Times are the podcast's. Quotes kept short; the rest is summary.

## Who and how much
- Started "under my desk in high school", filming and editing on his phone, faceless at first and "put on a voice" (16:28). Teachers played his videos in class without knowing it was him.
- The make-or-break video: 3 months, ran out of phone storage and finished on his mom's phone, spent $300; "now at 110 million views", edited on his phone (17:07-17:30). First editor iMovie; now Sony FX3 cameras, macro lenses, Final Cut Pro (19:46-20:48).
- Posts about 3 videos a year "on random dates... whenever it's done" (2:11). Claims about 67M average per video, "second most viewed YouTube channel in the world on a views per video basis" (3:52). Most videos cost "less than $1,000" (24:24). He writes, scripts and edits alone (24:30).
- Clipped all 12 long videos onto Facebook and Instagram last month: about 600M views "with no effort" (2:52). (Other platforms; we can't measure these.)
- 3 days to a week editing, then about a week of test viewings (13:28). Picking an idea takes about 5 minutes; executing it about 500 hours (9:02).

## Story is the product (his main claim)
- "YouTube is just a storytelling contest. The people telling the best stories are the people getting the most views" (1:59).
- Biggest creator mistake: too much worry about title and thumbnail, not enough about the story (1:54).
- "Greater story", not "greater spectacle", correlates with views. "People fall in love with characters. They don't fall in love with spectacle" (14:49-15:12). The ant video: viewers watch "for the story of what happened to that ant" (15:06).
- His one outlier-theory video (made "purely spectacle driven") was his worst performer (9:14). He dislikes copying outliers.

## He plans feelings, not plot points (26:41-27:45)
- "I plan out the emotion and exactly how I want the viewer to feel at each moment in the video and then the story writes itself."
- Ant colony emotion map, in order: **sympathy** (one lone queen "by herself", "it started with one") -> **awe** (she builds an empire of thousands) -> **stress** (war against another colony, "Avengers level stuff") -> **excitement** (they win) -> then (32:10) **remorse** (the ants build a graveyard for their dead).
- Movie influence: Christopher Nolan; he wants the viewer "in my seat shaking... so stressed" (27:56).

## Facts only when they push the story (31:47-33:04)
- "I'm very big on tricking the viewer into learning." "I sneak in the facts in the story... I only say that fact if it pushes the story forward."
- Example: ants build graveyards -> placed after the war, so it lands as a feeling (remorse), not a lesson.
- "Not one person woke up this morning out of the 84 million... saying I want to watch a video about ants" (25:16). He tests on people who don't like animals.

## The idea rubric (9:08-10:13, 28:16, 34:53)
1. Original, never done before.
2. Explainable in a single sentence ("go to your mom, your sister, your grandma... 'Oh, I get it'").
3. Timeless (hatching eggs works the same 100 years from now; no holiday specials).
4. Fun to make ("a direct correlation between how much fun I've had making a video and how many views it gets").
5. Appeals to any age; paints nature in a positive light; "you end this video not seeing the animal the same way again" (the hamster "debunking" video).
- Method: lock yourself in a room for a week, write ~1,000 ideas, score them on the rubric, one stands "vastly above the rest" (8:12, 34:42).

## Sound: the part he ranks above the picture
- "The most important thing to your video is sound." A camcorder video can get 100M views; bad audio and "no one can watch it." First buy: a good microphone (4:34-4:51).
- **The no-voice pass:** "my final pass of the video, I'll remove all voice over and see if it's watchable with just sound" (5:17). Repeated at 37:57: "If you cannot watch it without your voice, then you should not upload that video." The host: "storytelling with sound effects... on top of the music" (5:22).
- Foley he makes himself: plant growth = ripping a cabbage in front of a mic (20:53). Some (grass) from stock sites.
- Music: wants custom composed music; ending of the Africa video used a licensed famous old song ("The End of the World") for a "premium film story feel" (21:19-21:54). The host describes the music "ebbing and flowing" with sound effects coming in, and the glass-shatter sound added at the end (4:57-5:10).

## Script, voice and polish
- Learned to write by hand-copying scripts: watched trending videos on loop, and on the second watch wrote "the entire script as the video goes... programming my brain to talk like them and write like them" (6:15). Pen and paper, not an AI transcript (6:47-7:03). Three years of this before starting.
- Learned to tell stories at 50-person family dinners: "you had to be the one that could tell the best story and it would get everyone to be silent" (7:37).
- Done = "when I'm on the floor and I physically cannot hear the words anymore... I know the script to every single video... beginning to end" (13:48). Lines a friend is sick of hearing get cut (14:24).
- Small rules he names: titles under 52 characters; "brighter intros lead to higher retention" (37:05). Worth "the difference between 50 million and 100 million views."
- Not a documentary: "I'm trying to tell stories and I really want people to feel like they're there with the animals", using camera tricks he won't share (22:25).

## Testing (his "stranger test")
- At the gym: hands a stranger his phone, "Do you mind watching the first 10 seconds?" If they hand it back after 10 s with "great video", "your video is going to fail." If they keep watching and ask to finish it, it works (10:31-11:24).
- Now: calls (never texts) people A to Z in his contacts, screen-shares, always asks for "the first 10 seconds", lets them pause. One at a time, never groups (they copy each other's reactions). Example: 10 of 10 paused at the 30-second mark; he colour-graded the background from blue to yellow and the next 10 didn't pause (12:50-13:10). Rounds go "until I run out of my contact list."
- Watches for boredom (phone, pause) -> tighten; confusion -> add clarity (26:09).

## What this means for us (to check against the measurements)
- Claims to verify on his videos: an emotion arc per video (sympathy -> awe -> stress -> win); a lone hero at the start; facts placed inside story beats; sound design that tells the story without the voice; music that rises and falls; bright openings; titles under 52 characters.
- Claims we can't verify: the stranger-test results, the 600M clip views, the per-video costs.
