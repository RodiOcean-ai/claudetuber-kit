# Dr Plants: the secret sauce (6 Nov 2026)

## The recipe in one paragraph
**Put one real living thing in a small world, give it a clock, and ask whether it will survive.** Open by pointing at the hero and naming the clock in the same breath ("This is a queen ant and she has the next thousand days to create an empire"), and within 10 seconds add the catch that makes it life or death ("But a rival colony is watching their every move"). Then tell it as a chain: a problem grows, **so** he brings something in to fix it, **but** the fix creates the next problem. About two-thirds in, something we have come to care about is lost (a war, a storm, a death), and the end returns to the opening with a changed meaning ("so much we're told about hamsters is wrong; there is one thing we got right"). Facts only appear as plot turns. Underneath it all, the music is the second narrator: always on, loud, and rising whenever his voice stops, so the video still works with the voice taken out. His one change in 2023, from a calm diary to this survival movie, took the same title format from 43M to 117M.

## Format, not luck
- 12 videos, 852M views. The #1 (Egg, 123M) is **14 %** of all views: the most evenly spread channel we have studied (Paddy 20 %, ColinAmazing's record 39 %). 8 of 12 are over 50M.
- **A remake proves it:** "Simulating a Jungle for 365 Days" (2022, diary style, 43M, 0.85M a month) and "Simulating the Desert for 100 Days" (2023, survival-movie style, 118M, 2.6M a month): the same title format, the same tank on a desk, the same creator. What changed: a trailer cold open, a clock, a villain, setbacks, the music raised to 94 % of the video and his voice slowed with holds. His "make or break" video in the interview (3 months, $300, "now at 110 million views") is most likely this one: it was his next upload after Jungle, and the other videos near that number (Ant 99M, Egg 123M) are from 2025, long after high school.
- **Still rising:** views per month of age went 2.1-2.9M (2023-24) → 4.7M (Ant) → 5.4M (Bees) → **10.0M (Egg, Nov 2025)**. The newest (Apocalypse, 3.0M a month) is the first step down.
- **But his sound and editing are the same on hits and misses.** Planet and Frogs (2024) have his modern sound (music 84-97 %, the same holds, 10-25 % voice-free) and earned about a third as much a month as the videos around them. What they lack is the survival question.

## Against Zack's baseline and the other channels
- **Story:** Zack builds a climbing ladder and uses a setback in only 3 of 10 Shorts. Dr Plants puts a real setback in 9 of 9 strong videos and a loss in 6. He is closest to **GothamChess** ("a setback and a But turn: drama, not a lesson") and to **Paddy's** And-But-Therefore chain, but his villain is nature, and something dies.
- **Game board:** ColinAmazing's numbered list and GothamChess's live counter are his **day counter** ("By day 50…", "DAY 78" on screen, said in 9 of 12).
- **Sound is the opposite of Zack's.** Zack: voice wall-to-wall, about 16 LU above a soft bed, 0 pauses. Dr Plants: voice about 7 dB above loud cinematic music, 5 holds a minute, a sixth of the video with no voice. He's the most music-led channel we have measured (GothamChess 10 LU, Paddy 14 LU, MrBeast +6.6 but skits).
- **Sound effects:** few designed ones (about 2 a minute: low booms, whooshes, knocks, heartbeats), about 3 real animal and weather sounds a minute. Sparser than MrBeast (9), Nick (8.8) or GothamChess (6); his sound comes from real sound plus music, not cartoon effects.
- **The "music cuts before the punchline" idea is not his rule.** His music dips (about 1.6 a minute, 0.9 s, 17 dB) come before 10 % of all sentences and 13-33 % of jokes and turns. His rule is the reverse: the music rises in the gaps.

## What we copy (the craft)
1. **The survival lens for our series.** Tell each creator's growth as "will he survive?": one person, alone at the start (sympathy), a clock, a catch that makes it life or death for the channel, a loss, a comeback. It turns "How did X get Y?" from a lesson into a story.
2. **Line 1 = point at the hero + the clock** ("This is one boy with one phone, and he has one video to save his dream."). Our you-line rule from lessons.md still comes first where it fits; the clock goes in the same sentence or the next.
3. **The catch at 5-9 s names what is lost if it fails** ("But if it flops, he goes to college."). That is our "But" twist, on Zack's timing.
4. **The middle as a chain, not a list:** problem → so he tried → but that caused → so… Three links in 30 s.
5. **One real setback at about two-thirds (about 20 s of 30), then the turn.** Something the viewer cares about goes wrong, on screen.
6. **Facts only as plot turns.** A number or fact is allowed only if it changes what happens next.
7. **An ending that returns to the opening with a changed meaning** (already our bookend rule from sf-story-craft; he is the strongest proof of it: 7 of 9 vs 0 of 3).
8. **The no-voice test on our edit:** before the final render, watch the Short with the voice muted. Every beat must still read from the picture, the music and the sound of each action. In 3D: every action gets its own real-sounding sound (a crack, a thud, a rip), not only whooshes.
9. **Cover: before → after split** (DAY 1 → DAY 100 style) with one white arrow, one hero, 0-2 words. It matches Paddy's before → after number and our anonymous-hero rule.

## What we don't copy
- His face, his voice, his real footage and animals, his catchphrases ("Within this tank lives a miniature world", "we must go back to day one", "Let me introduce you to…"), the mother-in-law gag (we have Ginger), and his licensed famous songs (we can't license them; our own music rotation stays).
- **Long-form pacing:** 1.9 words a second, 5 holds a minute, music only 7 dB under the voice, a 25-second trailer montage. **Zack wins on Shorts format:** 27-35 s, about 2.8 words a second, no pause over 0.5 s, voice about 16 LU above the music. A trailer in a 30 s Short gives the ending away (the hook critic flagged it).
- Gimmick premises (a stunt, a list of everything): his weakest format.
- Real harm to animals, and any claim from his own mouth (600M clip views, "second most viewed channel per video") without two sources.

## How this changes our next Short

Line 1 points at him and names the clock; by 5-9 s the catch says what he loses if it fails. Then three linked beats (problem → try → new problem), one real setback at about 20 s, the turn, and a last line that reveals who it is and returns to frame one with a new meaning. The 12 videos / 6 million subscribers contrast is a strong "How did X get Y?" hook. Every number goes through the fact-checker first.

## Honest note
- **No Shorts.** None of his pacing applies to a 30 s Short. What transfers is the premise, the story chain, the loss, the ending and the sound check.
- Only 12 videos, and only 3 weak ones. "9 of 9 vs 0 of 3" is a clean split but a small sample; patterns 1-3 overlap (survival premises also bring setbacks and returning endings).
- Views per month are lifetime averages and favour young videos; Apocalypse (6 months old) may still settle lower.
- His Facebook and Instagram clips (he says 600M views) were not measured.
- The sound-effect counter is a classifier and misses soft foley under music; "about 2 a minute" is a floor.
- His interview claims (the stranger test, the 10-of-10 pause fixed by a background colour, "brighter intros") are his words, not our measurements. Brightness doesn't separate his hits: Desert, Forest and Ocean open dark and are hits.
