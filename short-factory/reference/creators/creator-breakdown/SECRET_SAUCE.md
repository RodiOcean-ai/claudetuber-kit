# Paddy Galloway: the secret sauce (4 Nov 2026)

## The recipe in one paragraph
**Pick a creator the mass audience is talking about *right now*, at the moment of a fresh growth spike, and put the growth number in the title ("How Beluga Gained 4 Million Subscribers in 3 Months"). Keep him a nameless character on the cover ("THE KID WHO BROKE YOUTUBE") over a rising red line. Open on the creator's own clip and the number, say what everyone believes ("he just got lucky"), flip it ("this was all planned"), and ask the question ("what does he know that the rest of us don't?"). Then answer it in 3 steps, where each step ends on a new problem that the next step solves, and save the best, most surprising step for last and give it a name.** The script and the cover are a fixed template he used on every video, hits and misses alike. What made the hits was the subject and the timing, packaged with a number.

## Format, not luck
- #1 video = **20 %** of all 34M views; top 10 = **78 %**. More hit-driven than GothamChess (10.8 %) or MrBeast (15 %), but the hits are spread over 10 videos about 8 different creators. Not one lucky clip.
- **Repeat subjects kept working:** MrBeast 6 times (median 1.15M, 2.6 times the channel median of 447k), Mark Rober twice (2.2M, 1.2M), Ryan Trahan twice.
- **No decay:** the newest third of his videos has a median of 553k against 200k for the oldest third. He stopped uploading in August 2022 while the format was still rising.
- **But the template doesn't separate hits from misses.** His 2 weaker videos use the same belief flip, the same planted question, the same 3 steps and the same cover. The hook critic scored the openings 5.9 (hits) vs 5.3 (weaker): a small gap, and two of the biggest hits have the weakest openings.

## What we copy (the craft)
1. **Pick the subject like he did:** a creator the mass audience knows and is talking about now, with a fresh, clean growth number ("4 million in 3 months", "2.7 million in 30 days"). Niche-adult creators (finance, photo, tech) did 6 times worse for him. This goes into how we choose each episode's creator.
2. **The "luck → plan" belief flip** as our wrong-belief block: "You'd think he just got lucky. But it was planned."
3. **"What does he know that we don't?"** as the planted question. It's the question our whole series answers.
4. **Before → after numbers** for the hook: "800 subscribers a month … then 4 million in 3 months". It's the "How did X get Y?" title, spoken.
5. **Three steps chained with "But… so…"**: each step creates the problem the next step solves. That's And-But-Therefore, our default structure. In 30 s: three steps of about 5-6 s each.
6. **Save the counter-intuitive step for last and give it a name** ("statement plus intrigue"). The payoff is a rule the viewer can repeat.
7. **Proof cards in 3D:** a glowing view-count or subscriber card with a green ring or red arrow, **on frame one** (fixing his late-number leak).
8. **The cover idea:** a rising red line behind the anonymous hero, with "THE KID WHO…" style words. It's our series rule (anonymous until the last line), and his whole channel used it.

## What we don't copy
- His face, name, voice, whiteboard look, graph-paper cover design, catchphrases ("which takes us on to step…", "I'll see you at the top"), and any real footage or screenshots of the creators. Series rules: faceless figures, silhouettes, no real clips in our video.
- **Ten-minute pacing:** a picture every 2.5 s, a lo-fi music bed under 58 % of the video, and a "get your notebook out" intro. Zack's Shorts format wins: 27-35 s, about 2.8 words/s, voice +16 LU, whips, loop.
- **Unchecked claims taken from a creator's own mouth** ("I think we might be the fastest channel ever"). Every number goes through facts.md with two sources.
- Sponsor reads, affiliate links, giveaways, "subscribe" asks, predictions about a real person's future.
- "How much does X make?" money-only titles: his weakest format (297k).

## His own playbook, checked against our numbers (added 4 Nov 2026)
Source: "Open Residency x Paddy Galloway: The Virality Playbook" (podcast episode 41, a 10-page summary the owner downloaded). These are **his claims about his client work**, not measured by us. Where our numbers back them, it says so.

| His rule (in our words) | What it says | Our study agrees? |
|---|---|---|
| **The idea ceiling** | Before execution, ask "why would *most* people in this niche watch this?" A weak idea with great production still gets a few thousand views. Same niche, wider framing, much higher ceiling ("I built a wildlife pond" 25-30M vs "pond lining guide" 10k). | **Yes, strongly.** His own channel: mass-audience creators 651k vs niche creators 115k with the same script. |
| **Click, Watch, Satisfy, Repeat** | Title + cover are about half the result; his team makes up to 50 covers per video with 5 designers. A video must feel *finished* (satisfaction: comments, likes, replays), and the system must repeat. | Partly: his growth-number titles beat his other titles (1.1M vs 447k median). The "50 covers" is unmeasured. |
| **Core, Casual, New** | Every video should work for fans, occasional viewers and total strangers. Never say "as we discussed last week", never assume viewers know a character, every episode stands alone. | Fits our series: each Short must make sense to someone who has never seen us or the creator. |
| **Outliers = 3-4× the channel's average** | Find ideas from (1) your own outliers: copy yourself first, (2) outliers on other channels and next-door niches, (3) combining two formats nobody joined before (about 20 % of uploads). Concept from one place, cover from another, title from a third. | Same as our niche research and ColinAmazing's remakes. |
| **100-10-1** | To present 5 ideas, generate 500, filter to 50. Fastest filter: **can the idea be a compelling cover?** It kills about two-thirds. | Not measured; cheap for us to copy (free text work). |
| **The 80 % overlap rule** | 80 % of people who watched the last video should want this one. Keep 20 % for experiments. | Fits: our viewers came for big famous creators; a niche creator breaks the overlap. |
| **The glance test** | One second from the cover alone; the idea in one short sentence; "would I unpause for this?"; "would I interrupt something important to watch it?"; "would both the biggest YouTuber and a non-creator parent find it interesting?" Title + cover together pose a question only the video answers. | Matches our anonymous cover + question title. |
| **Comments ask for the old thing** | "Ask your comment section what they want and they will ask for more of the old thing that stopped working." | **Changed our pinned-comment rule** (owner, 5 Nov 2026): comments *suggest*, the idea-ceiling and glance tests *decide*. The most-liked reply no longer picks the next creator on its own. |

What this adds to the recipe: **choose the creator with a 100-10-1 funnel and the idea-ceiling question before anything else, make several cover concepts and keep the one that passes the glance test, and make every Short stand alone for a total stranger.**

## How this changes our next Short
**Choose a creator with a fresh, mass-audience growth spike.** Open on a you-line with **the before → after number on screen on frame one**. Line two: **"You'd think he got lucky. But it was planned."** Then **three quick steps**, each handing a problem to the next ("That got the clicks. But how did he keep them?"), with **the counter-intuitive step last and named**. Reveal the creator in the last line and loop. Cover: the anonymous hero with a red rising line and 2-3 glowing words ("BROKE YOUTUBE?").

## Honest note
- **He has no Shorts.** Nothing here is evidence about Shorts format. It's evidence about story, packaging and subject choice for our exact topic.
- Patterns 1 and 2 overlap: mass-audience subjects also got the number titles. We can't fully separate "subject" from "title" with 33 videos.
- "Right now" is read from what the videos say ("in the last month", "in under four days"), not from the creators' real growth charts.
- Only 2 weaker videos had captions and 3 had covers; none was downloaded as video. Their frames, pacing and sound are unmeasured.
- The channel stopped in 2022, before Shorts-era packaging changed; covers may have been swapped since upload.
