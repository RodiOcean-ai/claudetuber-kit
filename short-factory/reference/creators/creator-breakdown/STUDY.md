# Paddy Galloway study (@paddygalloway8780, 533k subscribers)

A study of his 10 most-viewed videos (0.9M-6.8M), 2 weaker videos in the same formats (161k and 204k, captions and covers only) plus one more weaker cover (220k), and the titles and views of all 33 of his videos. **The channel has no Shorts.** Made on 4 November 2026.

## The big picture
- A YouTube strategy consultant who explains **how a famous creator grew**: one creator per video, 8-11 minutes, a whiteboard-style animated breakdown cut with the creator's own clips. This is our series' exact subject in long form.
- 33 videos, 34M views in all. The channel median is 447k; the top 10 are **78 %** of all views and the #1 (Beluga, 6.8M) is **20 %**. Hit-driven, but the hits are spread over 10 different videos and 8 different creators, not one lucky clip.
- **He stopped uploading in August 2022** (he now consults for big creators). So this is a finished body of work: no decay to watch, and the newest third of his videos did *better* (median 553k) than the oldest third (200k).
- **The craft is a fixed template.** His weaker videos use the same openings, the same three steps, the same covers. What separates a 6.8M video from a 161k one is mostly **which creator he picked and when** (see "Contrast").

## Hooks (first 3-30 s): hook critic average **5.9/10** for the hits, **5.3** for the 2 weaker ones
1. **The subject's own footage on frame one** in 9 of 10. Beluga's own voice opens #1: "This has been the craziest YouTube journey of all time… the fastest channel to hit 4 million subscribers in 3 months." The narrator comes in second.
2. **A growth number in the first 20 s** in 8 of 10, often as a **before → after** (5 of 10 by 45 s): "his subscriber growth was minus 1 … in the last 30 days he's grown by 2.7 million"; "up until a few months ago Beluga was gaining about 800 subscribers a month. But then in June, this happened."
3. **The wrong belief "he just got lucky" flipped to "it was a plan"** in 8 of 10: "If I told you three years ago that this guy right here was a YouTube genius, you'd probably laugh in my face"; "a lot of people would look at growth like this and think it was just a fluke … this guy had a master plan".
4. **One planted question** in 7 of 10: "What does a 16-year-old know about growing on YouTube that the rest of us don't?"
5. **"This guy right here"**: he points at the creator without naming him first in 4 of 10 (with a drawn arrow on screen), the same "anonymous hero" move as his covers.
- **The gap is small, and the openings don't explain the views.** The weaker Graham Stephan opening (6.0) beats four hits; two of the biggest hits have the weakest openings (MrBeast "GENIUS" 4.3 with 6.2M; Peter McKinnon 3.4 with 1.1M). Best opening: "If I was to tell you that this guy right here was a genius entrepreneur, you'd probably call me crazy" + "GENIUS ENTREPRENEUR?" on screen (7.4).
- **His repeating leak:** the proof number comes late (6-20 s) and there's almost never text on screen in the first 3 s. If we borrow his spine, the number goes on screen on frame one.

## Script formula (story blocks, chart in story_blocks.png)
| Block | Hits (of 10) | Weaker (of 2) |
|---|---|---|
| HOOK (the creator's clip + a growth number) | 10 | 2 |
| WRONG BELIEF ("luck", "just a kid", "just wholesome stuff") | 8 (9 counting "not just a big budget") | 2 |
| "BUT" twist ("this was all planned") | 8 | 1 |
| TEASE: "the 3-step formula" promised up front | 9 | 2 |
| TENSION: "what does he know that we don't?" | 7 | 2 |
| LADDER: step 1 → step 2 → step 3 | 10 | 2 |
| SETBACK between steps ("But there's a problem…") | 7 | 1 |
| TEASE before the last step ("there's still a piece missing") | 5 | 1 |
| PAYOFF: the biggest or most surprising step last, then a named rule | 10 | 2 |

- **His spine is And-But-Therefore on a loop.** Every step ends on a new problem that the next step solves: "So that's how MrBeast gets people to click. But how does he get them to stay?" / "this doesn't explain how his videos are getting picked up in the first place, which takes us on to factor number two". The phrase "which takes us on to step N" appears in 8 of 10.
- **Best step last.** In 5 of 10 the last step is teased as the real reason ("in my opinion the main reason Beluga gained 4 million subscribers"), and in 4 of 10 it's **counter-intuitive**: Beluga's blurry thumbnails and typos are "all intentional"; Mark Rober grows by uploading *less*; Ryan Trahan's daily vlogs "aren't supposed to work".
- **He names the rule at the end**: "statement plus intrigue", "the three element rule", "quality problem, not a quantity problem". The viewer leaves with a label.
- His oldest hit (Peter McKinnon, 2017) has none of the belief, "But" or tension blocks: it opens "Hello guys, welcome to the first episode" and lists three steps. It still got 1.1M as an early mover, and it's the flattest curve in the chart.

## Footage
- **Whiteboard animation** in 10 of 10: black line icons on a white page, built up piece by piece (a crowd of grey heads, an arrow, "FIRST 1000 VIEWERS"), labels in green (good) and red (bad).
- **Screenshots as proof** in 10 of 10: the creator's channel page, a view count, an analytics graph, each with a green circle or a red arrow.
- **The creator's own clips** in 10 of 10, used as proof inside every step ("What is the secret?" "I do like wholesome stuff.").
- **A rising chart drawn on screen** in the first 30 s in 4 of 10 ("BELUGA'S PLAN…" with a red arrow on the jump).
- Step cards: "STEP 1 / STEP 2 / STEP 3" on white, next to the creator's avatar (Beluga's cat with sunglasses).

## Transitions
Hard cuts and whiteboard wipes. The cut counter sees only 3-30 cuts a minute because the drawings build inside one shot; counted once a second, a **new picture arrives every 2.5 s** (1.8 s in the first minute, the fastest part in 8 of 10).

## Sound
- A soft lo-fi music bed under 58 % of the video (median), 14 LU below the voice; it drops out in some explanation stretches (as low as 10 % of the video in the 2017 one).
- The creator's own clips bring their own sound (screams, game audio) and act as the loud moments.
- Sound effects were not measured (see the honest note).

## Captions
None burned in. The on-screen words are labels inside the drawings (2-5 words, black caps, one word green or red), not subtitles.

## Titles and covers
- **"How [creator] [did a big number thing] (Genius Strategy)"**: 22 of 33 titles start with "How" (median 705k); 18 of 33 say "Genius" (median **546k** vs 297k without); 13 end in a "(Genius …)" bracket (median 651k). Median title: 10 words.
- **Formats by median views** (all 33, channel median 447k):
  - "…Broke YouTube": 2 videos, **median 2.6M**
  - "Gained N subscribers in [time]": 5 videos, **median 1.1M** (2.5 times the channel)
  - a creator named MrBeast: 6 videos, median 1.15M
  - a number in the title: 13 videos, median 893k
  - "beating the YouTube algorithm": 3, median 896k; "averages N views per video": 2, median 702k
  - "built his business empire": 4, median 527k
  - **"How much does X make?"**: 3, median 297k; **"Here's why/how…"**: 5, median 188k; a "?" in the title: 3, median 366k. Money-only and question titles do *worse* for him.
- **Covers are one fixed template** in 13 of 13: white graph paper, a red zig-zag line rising to the top right, the creator's face cut out on one side with a green or red outline, 3-5 heavy black italic words with one red word, the YouTube logo and a curved arrow pointing at the face. **The creator is never named on the cover** (13 of 13): "THE KID WHO BROKE YOUTUBE", "THE MAN WHO BEAT YOUTUBE", "THE SMARTEST MAN ON YOUTUBE". The title names him; the cover keeps him a character.
- Against MagnatesMedia's covers (one centred hero, dark, painted, 0-3 words): Paddy's are light, clean, off-centre and wordier. They read as "a lesson with a chart", not "a crime story".

## Long videos (all 10 reviewed from transcripts, chapters and openings)
- **Cold open** on the creator's own clip or a big number in 9 of 10 (not the 2017 one).
- **Chapters = the steps**: 4-9 chapters, named after the steps ("Step 1: Crowdfunding", "Session time king").
- **The best payoff sits in the last 20 %** in 7 of 10 (step 3 or the named rule).
- **Sponsor**: at the very end in 4 of 10 (Skillshare, TubeBuddy, NordVPN); never in the middle of a hit. The weaker Graham Stephan video has a full sponsor read at 4:20.
- **Ending**: a prediction about the creator in 3 of 10 ("could well be YouTube's first billionaire", "20 million subscribers this time next year"), "who should I analyse next?" in 2 of 10, sign-off "I'll see you at the top" / "in the next one" in 8 of 10. No loop.

## Contrast: what the hits have that the weaker videos lack
| | Hits | Weaker |
|---|---|---|
| Same opening moves (belief, question, 3 steps) | yes | **yes** |
| Same cover template | 10 of 10 | 3 of 3 |
| Subject: a mass-audience / young creator (MrBeast, Beluga, TommyInnit, Dream, Ryan Trahan, Mark Rober) | 9 of 10 | 1 of 3 |
| Made while the creator's growth spike was news ("in the last month", "in under four days") | 8 of 10 | 0 of 2 ("a year after", "biggest in his niche") |
| Title has a growth number or "broke YouTube" | 7 of 10 | 1 of 3 |

- All 33 videos: **mass-audience creators median 651k (25 videos) vs adult-niche creators (finance, photo, tech, fitness) median 115k (8 videos)**, almost 6 times.
- The cleanest pair: "How Mark Rober Averages 37 Million Views Per Video" (1.2M) vs "How Dream Averages 27 Million Views Per Video" (204k). **Same title, same cover, same 3-step script**; the Dream one came a year after his spike and opens on "every now and then, someone comes along", with no fresh number.
- So his hits are not a better *script*; they're the same script aimed at **the right creator at the right moment**, with a growth number in the title.

## Honest note
- **No Shorts.** None of his numbers say anything about Shorts format; Zack stays the authority on that. What transfers is the story spine, the covers and the subject choice.
- The 2 weaker videos were judged from captions and covers only (no video downloaded); their frames and sound weren't measured. SSSniperwolf's (220k) has no captions, so only its cover was used.
- The YouTube captions of the Dream video were garbled; its opening was re-transcribed with Whisper.
- Covers may have been changed after upload; we see today's versions.
- The "right moment" count is read from what the videos say ("in the last 30 days", "in under four days"), not from the creators' real growth charts.
- Sound effects were not measured (skipped to save time on 10-minute videos); the music and voice numbers above come from the voice/background split, which counts the creators' own clips as "background" too.

## Save the Cat check (added 6 Nov 2026, owner's question)
All 10 hits and the 2 weaker videos mapped onto Blake Snyder's 15 beats from the transcripts (a beat counts only with a line and its time; spot-checked).
- **Close fit (12 of 15): 4 of 10** (Beluga, MrBeast GENIUS, MrBeast empire, TommyInnit). **Loose (8-11.5): 5 of 10.** **Not (under 8): 1** (the 2017 Peter McKinnon list). Top 3 average 11.8, other hits 10.9, the 2 weaker 10.25: structure is a common floor, not what decides the views.
- **On the clock every time:** Theme Stated 9 of 10 at about 4 % ("And the best part is, this was all planned", Beluga 0:24); Break into Two 10 of 10 at about 20 % ("Which takes us on to step number one", Beluga 24 %); Midpoint 8 of 10 at about 55 %, a false win that opens a new problem ("…gets people to click, but how does he get them to stay?", MrBeast 59 %); Break into Three about 77 % ("there's still a piece missing", TommyInnit 76 %); a Finale with a named rule 9 of 10.
- **What he skips:** Dark Night of the Soul 0 of 10 (the sponsor read often sits there); a real All Is Lost only 1 of 10 (Squid Game, 84 %: "had all but died down"); Bad Guys Close In 4 of 10. Every setback is a "but there's a problem" handed straight to the next step.
- **The top 3 all end on a line that mirrors the first one** (Squid Game: "on course to hit 100 million views" → "first video to pass 200 million views"); the 2 weaker videos don't, and their sponsor reads land on a key beat.
- **Same beats every time?** Yes. Always there (9-10 of 10): Opening Image, Theme, Set-Up, Catalyst, Break into Two, Fun and Games, Midpoint, Finale. Always dropped or thin: Dark Night (missing in 9 of 10), Bad Guys Close In (missing in 5 of 10, 3 of his 4 closest fits), All Is Lost (never full; weak in all 4 closest fits). His 12 are the same 12.
