# Paddy Galloway: every sound and every piece of music in his top 3 long videos

Made 7 November 2026 from the sound of the three videos (sound only, downloaded for private study). The voice was split from everything else, the "everything else" track was searched for sound effects with our sound tagger plus a second, more sensitive pass, and the music was measured section by section (tempo, instruments, level against the voice, where it changes, where it stops). What was on screen comes from the frame study (one frame every 5 s) and the word-timed transcripts.

The three videos:
- **Beluga**: "How Beluga Gained 4 Million Subscribers in 3 Months (Genius Strategy)", 10:37, 2021.
- **MrBeast GENIUS**: "Here's why Mr Beast is a GENIUS", 10:18, end of 2019.
- **Squid Game**: "How MrBeast's Squid Game Broke YouTube (Genius Strategy)", 9:11, 2021.

## The short version

1. **Paddy hardly uses sound effects.** His own layer (outside the creators' clips) has only about **0.4 clearly audible effects a minute** in the 2021 videos and **none clearly audible in the 2019 one**. Counting every faint candidate, it is 6-9 a minute in the 2021 videos, but most of those sit 18-30 dB under the voice and many may be part of the music. Labels being written on, ellipses being drawn and the camera panning across the page are **silent**.
2. **Most of the "sound effects" you hear in his videos belong to the creators' clips**: Discord pings and typing in Beluga's skits, applause and cheering in MrBeast's free-bank video, a camera shutter in MrBeast's 2016 phone clip, the tick and pop of the Squid Game devices. Inside clips there are 4-12 effects a minute; outside them, almost none.
3. **His real sound tool is the music, and he switches it at every part of the story.** A soft mallet tune for the first 15 s, a loud head-nod hip-hop beat for the hook, a quiet drumless keys pad for the "what are we missing?" questions, a steady chill-electronic groove for the explaining, a lo-fi flute groove for the long title-and-thumbnail breakdown, and the loud hook beat again for the last step.
4. **He reuses the same tracks across videos.** The hook beat in Beluga (0:20-0:45) is the same recording as the last part of Squid Game (7:20-8:00). The explaining groove in Beluga (3:00-4:20) is the same as MrBeast GENIUS (2:00-3:20) and Squid Game (3:00-3:45). The flute groove in Beluga (4:30-5:35) is the same as Squid Game (4:47-6:42). It is a small "house sound" of about 5 tracks.
5. **Silence is his loudest effect.** The music cuts out completely for 0.4-4 s, about 3-10 times a video, right before or on the punch line ("Beluga had a... catalyst", "But there's a problem", "Not bad for a college dropout"), and before each creator clip. Then the music comes back on the next sentence or a new track starts.
6. **The music sits far under the voice and is never ducked.** Typical music is 18-27 dB under the voice; the hook beat and the last part are louder (8-11 dB under); the "thinking" pads are barely audible (25-33 dB under). The music is no louder in the pauses than under the speech (+0.2 to +0.6 dB), so there is no pumping.
7. **The few effects he does add mark changes of part**: a music "tape stop" falling in pitch into Step 1 (Beluga 2:37), a falling whistle as a cap flies in for "#2" (MrBeast 3:58), a 20-second swell with no drums that builds into Step 3 (Squid Game 7:00-7:20), and small pops, about one a minute, usually at the start of a new sentence when a new item appears.

## 1. How it was measured, and how sure we are

- **Voice and background were split** with a music separator. The background track holds music, Paddy's effects and the creators' clip sound. Some short tonal effects can leak into the voice track instead, so the voice track's pauses were searched too (only breaths and 3-4 unclear blips per video turned up).
- **Effects were found two ways**: our sound tagger (the one used for the other creators), which found only 1.5-2.9 effects a minute because the music drowns them out; and a second pass that first removes whatever repeats bar after bar (the music loop) and then looks for hits that are left over, throws out hits that come back at a regular spacing (drum hits of the music), and names each one with the tagger. Every candidate is listed in section 8 with how sure we are.
- **Transitions were checked by eye** on sound pictures (spectrograms) around every music stop, step card and clip start (about 60 places). That is how the tape stop, the falling whistle and the swell were found; the tagger missed them.
- **Music sections** were measured in 5-second blocks (tempo, drums on/off, bass on/off, level, instrument and style tags), and tracks were matched across the three videos by their chord patterns. Matches above 0.9 that line up second by second over a minute or more are counted as the same recording.
- **Limits**: we can't listen like a person; style names (lo-fi hip-hop, chill electronic) are our reading of the measured instruments, tempo and tags, not a track ID. Effects quieter than about 25 dB under the voice can't be told apart from the music. We don't have the picture at full speed, so "on screen" comes from frames every 5 s.

How the music is used across a video (the same in both 2021 videos):
1. **0:00-0:15**: creator's clip or the big number over the soft mallet tune.
2. **~0:15**: the hook beat **drops in on a sentence** ("This kid in his bedroom..." / "...going viral"), loud, until the first creator clip (~0:50-1:05).
3. **After the first clip**: the music falls to an almost silent drumless keys pad for the "why?" question and the "3 steps" promise.
4. **Each step gets its own bed**, usually changed at the step card or right after a creator clip.
5. **Before the last step**: a near-silence or a build, then the **hook beat returns** for the last step, at hook loudness.
6. **End**: the beat carries the summary and the subscribe ask, then stops dead.

The 2019 video is softer: one family of chill electronic and organ pads with almost no drums, 23-27 dB under the voice for most of the video, and loud only for the outro.

## 9. What this means for our Dr Plants breakdown

1. **Build the sound around 5-6 music beds, not around effects.** One bed per part of the story, switched at the step card or straight after a clip. Reuse the same beds in every future breakdown so the channel gets a "house sound", as Paddy did.
2. **Loud beat only twice**: the hook (from about 0:15 to the first clip) and the last step. Everywhere else the music sits 18-25 dB under the voice, and the "question" parts drop to an almost silent drumless pad.
3. **Use silence as the big effect**: cut all music for 0.5-1.5 s on the punch word or number (3-6 times in the video), and for 10-15 s once or twice on the key idea. Bring the music back on the next sentence.
4. **Keep effects rare**: about one clearly audible effect a minute, only at changes of part (step cards, the turn, the last step, the end). Labels, ellipses and pans stay silent; at most a very soft pop when a new item appears.
5. **Clips carry their own sound.** Our short Dr Plants audio clips should play with the music fully cut, at about the voice level, announced first ("Listen to how he says this").
6. **No ducking or pumping**: set the music level once per part and leave it.
7. Rough timing for a 10-minute video: mallet intro 0:00-0:15, hook beat 0:15-1:00, first Dr Plants clip, thinking pad 1:00-1:45, Step 1 explainer groove, Step 2 flute groove (the long screenshot breakdown), a 15-20 s build, Step 3 on the hook beat, outro groove, hard stop.

### Can we make these for free on this PC?
- **Yes for music.** The local picture-and-sound program (ComfyUI, running on this PC) already has two music models downloaded: **ACE-Step** (3.5 billion parameters, makes instrumental music from a description; the tempo can be written into it) and **MiniMax Music 3** (the downloadable open version, music from a description). Both run on our own graphics card, so they cost nothing. M1-M6 can be made there.
- **No dedicated free sound-effect model.** The node for Stable Audio (a free sound-effect model) is there, but the model itself is not downloaded. The sound-effect and music nodes for ElevenLabs, Sonilo and MiniMax's online service are paid partner services; we must not use them without your OK for each run.
- **Most of our effects don't need a generator.** Pops, ticks, the tape stop, the riser, the shimmer and the music cuts can be made on this PC by shaping simple sounds and by editing the music itself. Real-world ones (pencil, marker, paper slide, watering can, soil thud, pot clink, scissors, seed rattle) would sound best recorded with your Maono mic in a few minutes at a desk.

## 11. Honest limits
- Sound effects quieter than about 25 dB under the voice can't be told apart from the music; some quiet pops and swishes may be missed, and some "faint" ones in section 8 may be music.
- The separator sometimes puts short tonal effects in the voice track; the voice track's pauses were checked, but an effect hidden under speech can still be missed.
- Style names (lo-fi hip-hop, chill electronic) are our reading of the measured instruments, tempo and tags. We did not identify the tracks by name.
- Tempos can be off by half or double (78 bpm could be heard as 156).
- "On screen" comes from frames every 5 seconds, so an action within a second of an effect is matched by time and transcript, not seen.
- The 2019 video (MrBeast GENIUS) is in an older, softer style; the 2021 videos are the better model.
- The audio files, the separated voice and background tracks and the measurements are kept in the study's audio folder.
