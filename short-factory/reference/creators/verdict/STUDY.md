# Lionfield study (@Lionfield, 17.1M subscribers)

Two adult Italian friends (Matteo and Emiliano; channel line: "Pasta protectors during the day, musicians at night") who react to food videos from around the internet and judge them. Made on 15 November 2026.

Studied: the top 10 Shorts and top 10 long videos by views, plus 2 weak Shorts from the same channel for contrast. All 22 were downloaded and measured by machine (cuts, sound, Whisper words, voice), and Claude watched every Short at 2 frames a second. On top of that, the titles and views of **all 2,093 Shorts and 90 long videos** from the channel list (one source: YouTube, 15 Nov 2026).
Data: studies/lionfield ("lionfield - all data.xlsx", analysis/<id>/, frames_2fps/ for the 12 Shorts), copies of the workbook and sheets in this folder, story_blocks.png.

## The big picture
- **2,093 Shorts, 30.0 billion views. 90 long videos, 90 million views.** The long videos are 0.3 % of the channel's views. This is a Shorts channel; the long videos are mostly compilations of the Shorts.
- **The floor is the story, not the peak.** The median Short has **5.5M** views. 657 of 2,093 (31 %) passed 10M, 34 passed 100M, and only **45 (2 %) stayed under 1M**. The top 10 Shorts are 12.5 % of all Shorts views (Dr Plants' #1 alone: 14 %; ColinAmazing's record: 39 %). This is a machine, not a lucky clip.
- **About one Short a day for years.** The list runs newest first and matches the dates of the 12 Shorts we downloaded: about 300 Shorts before January 2022, about 1,800 since (roughly one a day).
- **The typical Short got 4-5 times bigger as they learned.** Median per block of 200 Shorts, in upload order: about 1.8-2.2M in 2022, 3.0-5.0M in 2023, 7.5M in 2024, **10.0M around 2025**, 5.2M for the newest 200 (2026; some are days old). The giant one-offs (250M-717M) all came in 2022-2024. Over the same years their titles changed (see "Titles"): food in the title rose from 52 % to 83 %, other creators tagged with "@" from 0 % to 32 %, songs/pranks/life topics fell from 20-24 % in the oldest Shorts to 1-4 %.
- **One formula:** someone online does something to food (or to an everyday thing) → the two of them, one always in the blue Italy football shirt, react and judge → a verdict or a twist, all in about 14 seconds. **The internet supplies a new villain every day; they supply the judges.**

## Hits vs weak: the psychology
**The owner's question:** why does this channel get so many more views, and what do its hits do that its weak videos don't?

Two levels of evidence. **(A)** The 10 hits and 2 weak Shorts, watched frame by frame. **(B)** The titles and views of all 2,093 Shorts: each block of 200 Shorts (in upload order) split into its top 10 % and bottom 10 %, so old videos are compared with their own era: **209 strongest vs 209 weakest**. Also the top 40 of the whole channel vs the 45 under 1M. Two weak videos is a thin contrast on its own; where A and B agree, the pattern is strong. The psychology names are our reading; the counts are what we saw.

### 1. Something everyone knows gets broken in the first two seconds (surprise + curiosity gap)
**In the viewer's mind:** the brain predicts every next moment. When a thing everyone knows is treated wrongly (spaghetti snapped, a pizza on the floor, concrete sliced like a pizza) the prediction fails and attention locks on: surprise. Because the thing is specific, a question forms at once ("what happens to it?"), and that gap pulls the viewer to the end.
- **Hits: 10 of 10** show it by 2.5 s: dry spaghetti snapped (717M, 334M) or its packet cut open with scissors (422M), a pizza sliding onto a kitchen floor, a "pizza" of wet concrete, cheese slices counted up to 500, a marshmallow cut open and microwaved, a "pasta gun", a phone dropped into a water jug, a tourist fighting an Italian window.
- **Weak: 0 of 2.** "Argue" opens on a woman doing Italian hand gestures; "Honey" opens on a tidy kitchen tip that works.
- **Same trigger, wrong place:** snapped spaghetti is in the first 2 s of 2 hits (717M, 334M) and by 6 s in a third (422M), but it is the *last* beat of the argue flop (7.5 s of 9.1).
- **Titles:** a food or kitchen word in 154 of the 209 strongest vs 118 of the 209 weakest; median 6.8M with one, 3.5M without. A vague teaser fails: a "?" sits in 25 strongest vs 34 weakest, in 5 of the top 40 vs 15 of the 45 under 1M ("WHY? 😳", "FOR REAL? 😳", "WHAT ARE YOU DOING? 😱"). A question works only on a named thing: "CONCRETE PIZZA?" 327M, "FAKE PIZZA?" 322M.

### 2. A tribe in uniform guarding something sacred (identity + safe outrage)
**In the viewer's mind:** people love to watch a group defend its rules. Italians feel proud and represented; everyone else enjoys poking the tribe (pineapple on pizza) or being judged by it, and both sides comment. Food is the perfect battlefield: everyone has an opinion and nobody gets hurt, outrage without the bad aftertaste of politics. The two of them are always the guardians.
- **The blue Italy national-team shirt** on one of them: **10 of 10 hits**, weak **1 of 2** (honey: a plain T-shirt). The pair is always blue shirt + white T-shirt.
- **Both of them in the video:** **10 of 10 hits**, weak **1 of 2** (honey: one man alone).
- **They play the "sacred" joke on purpose:** a wooden crucifix held up against food crimes on 3 of 10 long-video covers, a pizza shaped like a cross on a 4th, and "Call the exorcist" said in the spring 2023 compilation.
- **Saying it does nothing:** "Italian/Italy" in the title: 70 of the 209 strongest vs 70 of the 209 weakest; median 5.1M with, 5.7M without. The tribe is *shown* (shirt, faces, gestures), not written.
- **Leaving the role costs views:** songs, pranks on mum, break-up skits, tourist tips: 10 of 209 strongest vs 35 of 209 weakest; 3 of the top 40 vs 18 of the 45 under 1M; median 2.6M across all 136 such Shorts (others 5.8M).
- **The most comments of any hit** went to "Italian windows" (101,000; the other hits had 4,200-46,000): a "so true!" thing anyone who has stayed in Italy recognises and wants to add their own story to.

### 3. Borrowed attention: a clip that already won, a famous partner, a side to pick (social proof)
**In the viewer's mind:** a clip that already went viral has been tested on millions of thumbs; a face we have seen before feels safe to watch; "A vs B" makes us pick a side and wait for the winner.
- **9 of 10 hits open on another creator's clip** (all but the prank sketch), **but so do 2 of 2 weak.** The borrowed clip gets them in the door; it does not decide the hit.
- **"@another creator" in the title:** 49 of the 209 strongest vs 12 of the 209 weakest; 10 of the top 40, **0 of the 45 under 1M**; median **12.0M vs 4.6M**. Still true now: of the newest 30 Shorts, the 7 with "@" have a median of 5.1M, the 23 without 1.7M.
- **"vs" in the title:** 37 vs 12 of 209; 9 of the top 40 vs 1 of the 45 under 1M; median 10.0M vs 5.1M.

### 4. A verdict that closes the loop (completion urge + the judge's status)
**In the viewer's mind:** an open question itches until it is answered (the Zeigarnik effect), and the answer is the reward. Watching a confident judge rule is satisfying in itself: they are "the real Italians", the authority on pasta.
- **Every hit ends on a clear answer: 10 of 10** (an American peanut-butter cup crushed in a vise as revenge for the snapped spaghetti; ✓ on their own plate; "Approved!"; the pizza on the floor was fake; the friend on the sofa gets shot by the pasta gun; "Bravo my friend… Approved!"). **Weak: 1 of 2.** The honey test ends "Oh, almost… I don't know… half approved": the loop never closes.
- A spoken "Approved" or a ✓/✗ mark in 4 of 10 hits. "Approved" in the title: 7 of the 209 strongest vs **0** of the 209 weakest (median 9.7M for the 33 such titles vs 5.4M). The 8 long compilations say "approved" a median of 8.5 times each.

### 5. Their faces tell you what to feel, fast and without words (emotional contagion)
**In the viewer's mind:** before we understand a scene we copy the faces in it (the "mirror" reflex). A wide-eyed gasp says "this is outrageous" in a fraction of a second, in any language.
- **Their reaction faces are on screen by 3 s in 8 of 10 hits** (median 3.0 s); weak **1 of 2** (honey: 5.5 s, and a smirk, not a gasp).
- **The story reads with the sound off and without English in 10 of 10 hits** (faces, ✓/✗, a counter, gestures); weak **1 of 2**: the honey hook is a 30-word English caption to read in 5 seconds, followed by a 7-second shot of honey slowly dripping.
- A median of 13.5 words per hit. Words barely matter, so one video works in every country.

### 6. A twist in the last seconds (a second surprise)
- **5 of 10 hits** flip the expected ending: the prank revenge was for nothing (his phone was never in the jug, by our reading of the frames); the guardians march on a famous chef and get force-fed snapped spaghetti instead; horror at concrete pizza turns into "this is so relaxing… Approved!"; the pizza on the floor was fake; the "wasteful" chef cooks the cheese into hot dogs for a person in need. Weak: 1 of 2 (the snap at the end of "argue"). A house move, but it does not separate hits from weak.

### What we found no sign of
Fear of missing out or scarcity (0 of 12), money or status bragging (0 of 12), a "you" opening line (0 of 12), a loop built back to frame one (0 of 12 by eye; the machine loop check gave no number for the Shorts).

### The surprise: the weak Shorts were liked MORE
Likes per view: hits 2.5 % (median, 1.2-3.1 %); weak **5.0 %** and **3.7 %**; comments per view the same way. Our reading: the weak ones were shown mostly to fans, who like whatever the two post; the hits were carried to hundreds of millions of strangers, who watch without liking. **Fans don't make a hit; strangers do**, and strangers need points 1-5.

### Why this channel gets far more views (in plain words)
We did not measure rival channels in this study, so this answers it from the inside:
1. **Endless free villains.** The internet makes new food crimes every day; 9 of 10 hits react to someone else's clip, so they never run out of ideas and can post about once a day.
2. **A fixed role in a uniform.** Guardians of sacred food, in the Italy shirt, always two of them: recognisable in one frame, in any feed, in any country.
3. **A broken rule on frame one and a verdict by the end, in under 20 s** (9 of 10 hits): the viewer is hooked by surprise and finishes for the answer.
4. **Borrowed audiences.** Partners and rivals in the title ("@", "vs") carry the biggest medians on the channel (12.0M, 10.0M).
5. **Almost no words** (median 13.5): the whole planet is the audience, not only English speakers.

## Script formula (story blocks, chart in story_blocks.png)

| TENSION: their shocked faces, "what's happening?", the microwave wait | 8 | 1 |

- **No narrator and no spoken "But".** The shape is Problem-Agitation-Solution in about 14 seconds: the problem is shown, the agitation is done by their faces, the solution is the verdict.
- "Keep watching" level at the end (our judgement, 0-11): hits median 8.75; weak 7.5 (argue) and **4.5 (honey: the only curve that falls at the end)**.

## Music
- Music or the borrowed clip's own sound under 68 % of a hit (median); 3 of 10 hits have almost none (the prank, concrete pizza, pasta gun: 0-11 %). Tempo 83-144 BPM.
- Their voices sit only about 2 LU above it (Zack: 16): they shout over the clip rather than talk under it.

## Sound effects
- About 13.5 a minute by the classifier (0-37.8), weak 6.6 and 0. The most common: doors and slams (part of the action in the prank and the Chef Rush hallway), dings and buzzers (5: the ✓/✗ verdict sounds in "Spaghetti tutorial", a ding on the marshmallow test). The verdict has its own sound.

## Transitions
- **Hard cuts only, 12 of 12.** No whips, no fly-throughs.
