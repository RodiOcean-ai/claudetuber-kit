# Lionfield: the secret sauce (15 Nov 2026)

## The recipe in one paragraph
**Be the judges, not the cooks. The internet supplies a new food crime every day (spaghetti snapped in half, a pizza on the floor, wet concrete sliced like a pizza); the same two judges, one always in the blue Italy football shirt, let you see the crime in the first two seconds, let their shocked faces tell you what to feel, and always end on a verdict. Put the villain or a "vs" in the title. And never leave the judge's chair: the songs, pranks and life skits got about half the views.** In the viewer's head it is three feelings in 14 seconds: **surprise** (a rule everyone knows is broken), **belonging** (a tribe in uniform guarding something "sacred", outrage that hurts nobody), and the **itch of an open question** that the verdict scratches.

## Format, not luck
- **A machine, not a lottery:** 2,093 Shorts, 30.0 billion views. The #1 Short (717M) is **2.4 %** of them, the top 10 are **12.5 %**, the top 100 are 40 %. That is as flat as Nick DiGiovanni (3 % / 13 %) and MrBeast (#1 2.2 %); ColinAmazing's record clip was 39 %, Dr Plants' #1 14 %.
- **The floor:** median Short **5.5M**; 657 (31 %) passed 10M; only **45 (2 %) stayed under 1M**.
- **The remake test passes, with a twist:** at least **15 Shorts name broken or cut spaghetti in the title** (spread over about four years, "Breaking spaghetti AGAIN" twice: 17M and 15M), median **14.0M**, about 2.5 times the channel's median, the lowest 2.5M. The same crime keeps working because **the villain is new every time and only the judges repeat**. (Nick DiGiovanni's remakes of his own idea lost 7-18 times the views: there the creator repeated himself.) Counted by title words only, not era-adjusted.
- **But the giant hits are luck on top of the floor.** The 10 hits (251M-717M) are 45-130 times the median. The patterns below say what the hits share and the weak ones lack; nothing here explains 717M rather than 50M. All 10 came in January 2022 - September 2024, during the Shorts boom.
- **The floor rose while the ceiling fell** (medians per 200 Shorts, in upload order, dated by the 12 Shorts we downloaded): 1.8-3.0M from January 2022 to spring 2023, with 14 Shorts over 100M in those 600; 8.6-10.0M from about late 2024 to spring 2026, with 9 over 100M in 600 and none over 225M. Early on: a lottery with low tickets; later: a machine with no jackpots.
- **Decay flag:** the newest 200 Shorts have a median of **5.2M** (the 200 before them 8.6M, the 200 before those 10.0M) and **none passed 50M** (best 45M). The newest 30 sit at 1.8M, but some are days old. Part age, part cooling; check again in three months before calling it decline.

## Versus the other channels we studied
- **Zack D. Films (our format authority):** 13.9 s vs 30-35 s; 1.5 vs 2.8 words a second; voice 46 % of the time vs about 100 %; their voices about 2 LU above the background vs 16; hard cuts in 12 of 12 vs no hard cuts in 7 of 10; no loop (0 of 12) vs our loop rule. Story: Problem-Agitation-Solution (the crime shown, the faces agitate, the verdict) vs belief, "But", ladder. Payoff 10 of 10 in both. **Zack wins on format.**
- **Nick DiGiovanni:** the same raw material (an everyday food with one rule broken; Chef Rush appears in both). His lesson was "never twice"; Lionfield repeats the same crime 15+ times at 2.5 times its median. The difference is who repeats: **repeat the judge, never the villain.**
- **MrBeast:** a new picture about every 1.1 s; here 1.36 s (both about 40 cuts a minute). His "X vs Y" titles beat his own challenge titles (553M vs 302M); here "vs" doubles the median. **An opponent wins on a third channel too:** Mark Rober had one in 6 of his 10 top long videos, 0 of his flops.
- **GothamChess:** subject clear by 1.6 s in 7 of 10 hits vs 0 of 3 misses. Here the broken rule by 2.5 s in 10 of 10 vs 0 of 2. Same rule, second channel: **know what you're watching in two seconds.**
- **Dr Plants:** a setback in 9 of 9 strong videos. Lionfield: no setback at all; in 14 s the faces do the agitating.
- **Khaby Lame** (titles only): "no words, so the whole world gets it" was already our light-switch episode. Lionfield's near-silence (13.5 words) is real, but it is not this episode's lesson.

## What we copy (the craft)
1. **The crime on frame one:** something everybody knows (spaghetti, pizza, a microwave, a window) treated wrongly, moving, readable by 1.5 s, no words needed.
2. **The judges in uniform:** two blocky judges, always together, one in a plain team-blue jersey and one in a white T-shirt (colours only: no crest, no number, no badge). The tribe is shown, never said.
3. **Faces first:** a gasp or horror expression (from our 12) on screen by 3 s, so the Short passes the no-voice test.
4. **Always a verdict:** a big tick or cross stamp with its own sound (ding or buzzer) at the payoff. Never end on a shrug.
5. **A villain to judge:** in our rules the villain is a habit or a belief, never a real person: a faceless blocky "cook" commits the crime.
6. **Stay in the chair:** every episode stays inside our series role (the lab, the question title, the reveal). Their one-offs outside the role got about half the views.

## What we don't copy
- Their faces, names, voices, footage, the Italy national-team crest, the crucifix "sacred food" joke (religion reads badly without their faces and tone), "Approved" as our own catchphrase (we may report it as a fact about them).
- Reacting to other people's clips: we never show real footage.
- 14 s, 1.5 words a second, voices 2 LU over the background, hard cuts and no loop: Zack's 27-35 s, 2.8 words a second, voice about 16 LU over the bed, flights and the loop stay.
- "No words, whole world" as the lesson (used in the Khaby episode).
- The giant-hit dream: their 250M-717M Shorts came in the 2022-24 boom and have stopped. Copy the floor, not the lottery.

## How this changes our next Short
Frame one is a crime against something the viewer knows (a blocky cook snapping a giant bundle of spaghetti in the lab), the two judges' horrified faces by 3 s, the middle climbs like a court case (the crime, the reaction, a worse crime), and the last beat before the reveal is a verdict stamp with its own sound; the reveal ("two friends from Italy") comes in the last line.
