# Nick DiGiovanni study (@NickDiGiovanni, 48.3M subscribers)

Studied 5 November 2026: top 10 Shorts (182M-669M) and top 10 long videos (69M-203M) downloaded and measured; 4 weaker same-format Shorts (10M-30M) from captions, covers and descriptions; all 489 Shorts titles and views. Picked by our subject funnel (Paddy's 100-10-1, no kids' channels).

## The big picture
- **A very even channel.** 489 Shorts, median **27M**, and his weakest Short still has 8.6M. The #1 Short (669M) is 3 % of all Shorts views; the top 10 are 13 %. Not a lottery: he has a floor most channels never reach.
- **Still rising:** the newest third of his Shorts has a median of 34M against 20M for the oldest third.
- **Two kinds of Short:** the **narrated mission** ("Let's make the world's largest sushi roll", voice wall-to-wall, almost no music) and the **silent skit** (a gag with a famous partner, music bed, few words).
- **His secret isn't cooking. It's casting.** 8 of his top 10 Shorts star another well-known creator (7 tagged in the description, QCP on screen only: Zach King, MrBeast, Lynja twice, Chef Rush three times, QCP, nine creators at once); a 9th rides an event (the Winter Olympics torch relay). Only "Fresh Chicken Nuggets" is solo.
- **The idea must be new.** Every weaker Short we checked repeats an earlier hit: the sushi-roll script reused for a donut (290M → 10M), the very same cake-pop video re-posted (182M → 10M), smallest pasta remade without the guest (200M → 30M), "1 second vs 1 hour" chicken → egg with a sponsor read (132M → 16M).

## Hooks (first 3 seconds): hook critic median **6.7/10** for the hits (5.7-8.8), about **5.4** for the 2 weaker (estimated from covers)
- A food everyone knows is on screen or named by 2 s in 9 of 10: toast, pasta, nuggets, sushi, ice, a cake pop.
- First line is a **mission** in 5 of 10: "Let's make…", "Let's rate…", "Let's feed…". Said at 0.0 s.
- A famous partner is on screen by 3 s in 7 of 10.
- The #1 Short opens with no words at all: a man drops bread in a toaster, and at 2.5 s tiny chefs appear inside it.
- **The opening score barely follows the views.** The #1 Short (Toast, 7.6) has a plain frame one; the best opening (Gourmet Ice, 8.8) is 8th by views. What the hook critic rewards most is **an absurd twist on an everyday food** (toast, nuggets, ice, pasta, lobster: 6 of 10, median 7.9) over giant-food and creator-crowd openings (median 6.4).
- "Let's make…" openings score lower (median 6.4) than a line said in the moment ("Hey Chef, can you turn my ice gourmet?", "This is some of the rarest pasta in the world": median 7.6).
- Crowded frames (the sushi pile with 6 people, 20 faces for the knife throws) score lowest.

The shape is MrBeast's (mission → ladder → payoff) plus a joke sting that flips the scale: "Nick, why are yours so small?" "It's average." The wrong-belief "But" of Zack's spine is rare (Nuggets: "First we'll select our chick… on Day 1 I named him Nugget").

## Footage
- Real kitchen, real food, real people, bright daylight. One big object per Short (the giant sushi roll, the record cake pop, about 97 lb (Guinness), the single tiny bite of pasta).
- 2025 Shorts are filmed like skits (a chef on a video call, a lobster on a beach); 2021-23 Shorts are process cooking at a counter.
- The #1 Short is one continuous take with a visual-effects trick (tiny chefs fighting inside a toaster): 0 cuts in 16.5 s.

## Transitions
- Hard cuts, a new picture every 1.2 s in the narrated Shorts. No whips, no flights. Toast: no cut at all.

## Sound
- Narrated Shorts: the voice carries everything, no music in 5 of 6, the background sits 3-40 LU below the voice.
- Skits: a music bed under 69-92 % of the video, the voice at or below it, 8-18 sound effects a minute (coin drop, bang, bell).
- Loudness -15 LUFS, like Zack and MrBeast.

## Captions
- 2025 Shorts: 1-2 white words with a black outline, lower middle, built for mute. 2021-23 Shorts: no burned-in captions, only labels ("Day 17") and emoji graphics.

## Titles and covers
- Shorts titles: **2-4 words**, the food and the twist ("Gourmet Ice", "World's Smallest Pasta", "Fresh Chicken Nuggets"); no emoji in 489, a "?" in 8.
- Title formats across all 489 (median vs 27M): "Gourmet [thing]" 129.5M (2), "World's Smallest" 109M (3), MrBeast in the title 89.5M (6), "N Levels Of" 55M (11), "1 Second vs 1 Hour" 51M (4), Lynja 48.5M (12). Luxury words (wagyu, A5, gold, $) **22.5M (44)**: below his median.
- Shorts covers are frames from the video. Long-video thumbnails: his face + one giant, saturated food object + 0-2 words, 10 of 10.

## Long videos (10 of 10 from transcripts and openings)
- 19-47 minutes, "In this video, I'm…" as the first line in 7 of 10, a list or ladder of N items in 9 of 10 (50 ways, 10 levels, every colour, every country, every animal).
- **"$1 vs $100,000"** is a long-video winner (median 50.5M, 1.8x his long median), the format I picked him for, but in Shorts the money and luxury hook sits **below** his median.
- MrBeast again: "Can I Turn MrBeast Into A Chef?" 116M.

## Honest note
- Weaker Shorts were not downloaded as video: their pacing and sound are unmeasured.
- Guests are counted from descriptions and the screen; guest-less titles across all 489 can't be counted (the full list has no descriptions). "ft." in the title alone is flat (40 Shorts, median 27.5M), so the guest only helps when the guest is the idea.
- Partners' own reach (Zach King, MrBeast) inflates those views; we can't separate the guest's audience from Nick's craft.
- "First time the idea appears" is checked against his own titles only, not against other channels.
