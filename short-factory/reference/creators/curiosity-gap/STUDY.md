# Lingualin study (15 Nov 2026)

Channel: @lingualin ("I make videos to make STEM more interesting and easier to learn"), 291,000 subscribers. 257 Shorts with 421M views in all; 7 long videos (all under 1M).
Studied: top 10 Shorts and top 7 long videos by views, plus 3 low-view Shorts from the same channel for contrast (the "weak" ones, about 100K each). All 20 measured by machine (cuts, loudness, voice/music split, Whisper words, sound classifier, hook frames). Claude watched every Short as 10-frame sheets, the first and last frames side by side, and the 0-3 s hook frames, and read the titles of all 257 Shorts. View numbers are YouTube's on 15 Nov 2026 (one source).
Data: studies/lingualin ("lingualin - all data.xlsx", analysis/<id>/), copies of the workbook, hook sheet, cover sheet, first/last frame sheet and story_blocks.png in this folder.

## Big picture
- **Faceless, wordless math animations.** Black background, thin neon lines, one bold two-colour title in the top third, a small watermark. **9 of 10 hit Shorts have no voice at all**: music and the picture tell it. (Whisper "heard" "Thank you." in 8 of them; that is the usual mistake on music, not speech.)
- **One continuous shot.** 0 cuts in 9 of 10 hits (1 cut in the 10th). Nothing is edited; the camera zooms and the drawing grows.
- **A high floor, not a single lucky clip.** The middle Short has 518K views. 255 of 257 Shorts are over 100K, 92 over 1M, 25 over 5M, 7 over 10M. The top 10 make 33 % of all Shorts views; the biggest single Short is about 5 % (22M of 421M).
- **The feed carries it, not subscribers:** about 1,450 Shorts views per subscriber.
- **All 10 hits are from 2026** (10 Feb - 12 Sep). We have no upload dates for the other 247 Shorts, so we can't say when the channel took off.
- **Long videos don't work for it:** 7 long videos, median 53K, best 614K. The 3 narrated explainers are the 3 lowest (42K, 27K, 2.6K); the 2 best are wordless (103 paper folds, 54 math sounds).

## Why it out-views its niche (what we can say, honestly)
We did **not** measure rival math-animation channels, so this part is reasoning from this channel's own numbers, not a head-to-head count. Four things stand out:
1. **It needs no language.** 9 of 10 hits have no voice and 3-6 words of title. Anyone in any country gets it, so the feed can show it everywhere. Most math channels explain out loud.
2. **It sells a feeling, not a lesson.** 7 of 10 hits turn the math into the viewer's own life (age, love, effort, fate, longing). You don't need to like math to watch "What is the TRUE Midpoint of Life?".
3. **It asks, it never tells, in the title.** Titles that start with "What" (30 Shorts) have a median of 1.19M, more than twice the channel's 518K.
4. **It re-posts its winners under new titles** (see "Same idea, different title" below): at least 5 ideas appear 3-8 times each.

## Hits vs weak: the psychology
The 3 weak Shorts: "The Insane Butterfly Effect" (98K, Aug 2026), "The Coolest Way to Cut a Pizza for 32 People" (103K, posted 4 Nov 2026, only 11 days old), "If Chaos Had a Sound" (103K, Jan 2026). They are made in the same house style: same black screen, same neon, same music-only sound in 2 of 3, same length range. So **the look, the music and the pace do not separate hits from misses here. What separates them is what happens in the viewer's head.**

| What the viewer's mind does | What the video does | Hits (of 10) | Weak (of 3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Completion urge** (an unfinished thing nags until it is finished) | Ends on a clear payoff picture or number: a heart forms, 314,159 lands, "exactly 18", the hidden slant in the chocolate | **9** | 1 (pizza: 32 pieces) |
| **Suspense before the answer** (we lean in when the moment of truth is close) | A TENSION stretch just before the payoff: two line ends creep toward each other, the zoom onto the chocolate's edge, the counter's last run | **8** | **0** |
| **Curiosity gap** (a question we can't answer yet holds us) | A question or promise on screen from frame one that only the end pays: "What π really means?", "The Secret Behind the Golden Spiral", "Wait for it...π?", "The ending will inspire you", "PARADOX EXPLAINED" | **7** | **0** |
| **"That's me"** (we attend to anything about ourselves) | The math is given a meaning about your life: midpoint of life, Stranger → Friend → Lover → Ex, slow progress wins, you can't fight fate, "So Close, Yet So Far", "We'll meet again" | **7** | **0** |
| **Someone to root for** (we read two moving shapes as two characters with wishes) | Two things the eye follows: two curves racing, two line ends that almost meet, two ages on a spiral, a big block and a small one | **6** | **0** (100-10,000 balls, 14 pendulums, a pizza) |
| **Surprise** (the brain jumps when a prediction breaks) | A result that should not happen: free chocolate from nowhere, the midpoint is 18 not 40, the slow curve wins, block collisions spell π, random rays draw a heart | **5** | **0** |
| (not separating) **anticipation of a number** | A counter or ladder climbs on screen (θ, collisions, 10 → 100 → 10,000 tries, ages) | 8 | 2 |
| (not separating) | No voice / music only | 9 | 2 |
| (not separating) | One continuous shot, black neon look | 10 | 3 |

**9 of 10 hits do at least 3 of the first six rows; 0 of 3 weak Shorts do.** The one hit that does none, "If Math Functions had a Sound" (12.8M), runs on a different engine: **recognition and laughter**. Each graph is drawn with an everyday sound (a siren, beeps, chirps: 11 sound events, 18.9 a minute, music under only 13 % of it, 😂😂😂 in the on-screen title): an equation that sounds like an ambulance. Its failed copy, "If Chaos Had a Sound" (103K), uses piano notes (16 electric-piano events) instead of sounds you know, has no named items, runs 61 s, drops its title after a few seconds and **starts with 3 s of silence** (0 of 10 hits do).

**Not used at all (0 of 13 Shorts):** outrage, fear of missing out, social proof on screen ("10M people…"), status or "only geniuses". The only tribe moves are in titles: "Which … is your favourite?" polls (5 Shorts, median 1.40M) and school memories ("Did you go through this in high school too?" 2.5M).

### The clearest pair: same set-up, 86 times the views
"You can't fight fate" (8.5M, 34 s) and "The Insane Butterfly Effect" (98K, 35 s) are almost the same Short: a white half-circle, things dropped into it, a ladder of 10 → 100 → 10,000 tries (balls: 100 → 1,000 → 10,000), rainbow colours, strings music, no voice. The hit ends with **a heart forming out of the chaos** and calls it fate; the weak one ends **in a mess that fades to black** under a label, "Visualizing Unpredictable Chaos". Order out of chaos + a meaning about you, against chaos that stays chaos. (The butterfly Short even appears to share its music track with the π-collisions hit: same tempo 129 BPM, same loudness, same instrument labels.)

**The life meaning is a payoff, not a title.** Across all 257 titles, the 32 with life words (life, love, fate, friend, crush, regret, age, parents…) have a median of only 0.40M, below the channel's 0.52M: 6 are in the top 25 and 5 in the bottom 25. The hits put the meaning **at the end of the video**, after a question; the flops put it in the title ("Love Story Written in Math." 125K, "The Curve of Regret in Math💔" 178K).

## Hooks (frames 0-3 s)
- **Frame one = the title question + a picture already moving** in 10 of 10 hits. No face, no "you" sentence, no statistic: the hook is a question sitting over a moving drawing.
- What moves first: a dense mesh sliding (π, 3.14), an extra chocolate square popping out (chocolate), a bar filling (midpoint), a graph drawing itself with a siren sound (sound), a spiral drawing (golden), a curve labelled "The Stranger" (us), two curves starting a race (consistency), a counter at 0 and two blocks (π collisions), coloured lines falling (fate).
- **Two hits open zoomed into the middle of the action** (π and 3.14 start inside a dense pattern and pull back to show what it is): you see detail before you know what it is.
- The one voiced hit opens Zack-style on a belief about you: "We all know that if you live to be 80, the midpoint of your life is 40." with "But" at 4.5 s. The voiced weak one also opens well by our rules ("You made a pizza for three people, but 32 showed up!", "but" at 2.1 s), so a good spoken hook did not save a low-stakes idea that the channel had posted three times before.
- Hook-oracle scores were not run in this step.

## Script formula (story blocks, chart in story_blocks.png)
Blocks were read from the pictures for the wordless Shorts (one beat = one new thing the picture does) and from the words for the 2 voiced ones.
| Block | Hits (10) | Weak (3) |
|---|---|---|
| HOOK (question + moving picture) | 10 | 3 |
| LADDER (each step bigger: 10 → 100 → 10,000, θ climbing, the next function) | 9 | 3 |
| TENSION just before the end | **8** | **0** |
| PAYOFF as the last block | **9** | 1 |
| WRONG BELIEF (x² leads; "the midpoint is 40") | 2 | 0 |
| "But" twist | 1 | 1 |
| SETBACK then COMEBACK (two ages drift apart and meet again; slow curve overtakes) | 2 | 0 (pizza: setback only) |
| LOOP | 0 | 0 |
The shape of a hit: **ask → climb → slow down on the moment of truth → land.** The weak ones climb and then stop climbing (butterfly, chaos sound: the line falls at the end) or land softly on a tip (pizza).

## Visuals, transitions, captions
- Neon line drawing on black in 13 of 13; real photos only in the pizza Shorts and 2 long-video covers.
- **On-screen "game board":** a counter or a labelled ladder in 8 of 10 hits ("Collisions 314,159", "θ = 712.14", "10 Tries / 100 Tries / 10000 Tries", "Age 4 … Age 80"), the formula in small type near the drawing in 7 of 10.
- Transitions: none. The camera zooms (in or out) inside one drawing.
- **The ending caption changes the meaning** in 3 hits: the title is replaced by "So Close, Yet So Far", "We'll meet again", "We will meet again".
- Captions only in the voiced Shorts: 1-3 words at a time, white bold italic, under the drawing.

## Covers and titles
- Shorts covers are frames from the video with the on-screen title on them (10 of 10); at least 2 show the finished picture (both hearts: golden spiral, fate). In the Shorts feed the cover matters little; the first frame does the work.
- Long-video covers: one object + a big question in 5 of 7 ("103 folds?", "WHAT DOES MATH SOUND LIKE?", "WHERE IS YOURS?", "1/0 = ?", "Why this one?").
- Hit titles: a question mark in 5 of 10, "really/actually/TRUE" in 4, starting with "What" in 4; the 3 weak titles: 0 questions, each a label.
- Whole channel (257 titles): starts with "What" 30 titles, median 1.19M, 8 of the top 25 vs 3 of the bottom 25; "really / actually / true / secret" 23 titles, median 0.83M, **6 of the top 25 vs 1 of the bottom 25**; the "Sound / Listening test" series 21 titles, **median 1.40M, 12 of 21 over 1M** (the most reliable format, but only 1 over 5M); pure-looks words ("chaos", "satisfying", "beautiful", "fractal") 18 titles, median 0.39M, **0 in the top 25**; hashtags in the title 32 titles, median 0.32M.

## Long videos (7)
Paper folds (614K, wordless, cold open "One sheet of paper. Folded 103 times. How tall could it get?"), 54 math sounds (526K, a compilation of the Shorts series), "I Found Every Birthday in Pi" (160K), a 4-minute math "workout" (53K), and three narrated explainers: collisions and pizza both making π (42K, opens "I'm sure you've seen this classic experiment"), dividing by zero (27K, opens "What happens when you divide by zero?"), and men's-room rules (2.6K, a topic outside math). No sponsors, no face. The Shorts audience does not follow to long videos; the narrated ones do worst.

## What our series can take
- A question on screen from frame one that only the last line answers; never put the answer in the title.
- The meaning about the viewer as the payoff, after the question.
- Two characters the eye can follow, and a slow-down on the moment of truth before the landing.
- End on a picture that settles (a shape, a number), not on chaos or a fade.
- Copy the structure only: our bright blocky world stays ours; nothing from their videos goes into ours.

## Honest notes
- **Rival channels were not measured.** "Why more than the niche" is reasoning from this channel's own numbers.
- **Only 3 weak Shorts**, and the pizza one is 11 days old. The whole-channel title counts (257 Shorts) are the larger test, and they agree on the question titles and the pure-looks titles.
- The "same idea, different title" groups are judged from titles only; we did not download the re-posts and don't know their order.
- The psychology rows are Claude's reading of the frames (definitions in techniques.md); the sound-effect detector also flags single "Television"/"Vehicle" moments inside music tracks, which are probably false.
- Views are not craft: topic, age and promotion are tangled. We say what the hits share and the weak ones lack, never what caused the views.
