# Lingualin: the secret sauce (15 Nov 2026)

## The recipe in one paragraph
**Ask a question about something the viewer thinks they already know (π from school, the middle of your life, a bar of chocolate) and keep it on screen from the first frame, with the answer held back. Give the eye two things to follow as they climb toward the answer, slow right down on the moment of truth, then land on one finished picture or number that suddenly means something about the viewer's own life ("So close, yet so far", "exactly 18", a heart out of chaos).** The look, the music, the silence and the pace are the same on his hits and his flops. What differs is what is left open in the viewer's head: a question they can't answer yet, someone to root for, a moment of suspense, and a picture that finishes and turns out to be about them.

## Format, not luck
- **Not one lucky clip:** the biggest Short is **5 %** of 421M Shorts views, the top 10 are **33 %**; the middle Short has **518K**, and **478K** with the top 10 taken out. 255 of 257 Shorts are over 100K.
- **But the ceiling is rare:** 7 of 257 Shorts are over 10M (about 1 in 37). We watched only 13, so we can't say how often the full recipe was tried and missed.
- **Same idea, answer first, then the question (the clearest proof).** His Shorts list runs newest-first (checked against 13 upload dates), so we can see the order. "The Midpoint of Life is Actually 18" got **518K**; about three months later "What is the TRUE Midpoint of Life?" got **17.3M (33 times more)**. The π curve went out four times with the answer or a label in the title ("3.14 means we'll meet again" 180K, "So close, yet so far" 119K, "The visualization of pi" 149K, "Across time and space…" 1.2M), then as "What 3.14 really means?": **22.7M**. Because the answer versions came *first*, "people had already seen it" can't explain the gap.
- **Remakes:** a quick second version that still holds the answer back won again 3 times ("What π really means?" 22.1M eleven days after the 3.14 one; pizza 5.0M then 6.5M; dance 6.8M then 7.3M). But of all 14 re-posts after a hit, **10 stayed under 0.6M** (median 0.48M, below his 0.52M): "Part 2", the answer in the title, or a fourth go. Re-posting is a weak bet after the first remake.
- **Decay check:** the floor is rising (middle Short of the oldest third 378K, middle third 508K, newest third 733K; 1M+ Shorts 28, 30, 34). The ceiling is not: **no 10M+ Short in the last 64 uploads** (since 12 Sep, best 7.1M). Young Shorts may still climb, so it is too early to call it decay. Flag it.

## What we copy (the craft)
1. **The question on the board from frame one**, under the safe line, answered only by the last line. Nothing earlier (title, cover, caption, picture) gives the answer away. Our title already asks; keep the answer words out of it.
2. **Two characters, not a crowd:** the anonymous hero and one rival or partner the eye follows to the end.
3. **The slow-down:** the 2-3 s before the reveal, the camera slows and the two things close in. The voice keeps going (no pause over 0.5 s); only the picture, and maybe one music layer, slow down.
4. **Land on one finished picture** that settles and holds, never a mess or a fade. It should echo frame one so our loop still works.
5. **The meaning about "you" is the payoff, said in the last line**, never put in the title.
6. **An ending caption that re-reads the opening question** in 1-3 words ("So Close, Yet So Far" did it in 3 hits): our bookend with a twist, on screen.

## What we don't copy
- His animations, his black-and-neon look, his music, his watermark, his on-screen wording. Our bright blocky lab stays ours.
- **Silence:** our series is narrated (Zack: voice on frame one, about 2.8 words/s, voice 14-18 LU above the music). Never a quiet first second.
- **Re-posting the same Short under new titles:** risky on a small channel (reused content) and it faded 10 of 14 times for him.
- Pure-looks words ("satisfying", "chaos", "beautiful": 18 titles, median 0.39M, 0 in his top 25) and hashtags in the title (32 titles, median 0.32M).
- Long videos in his style: his 7 long videos have a median of 53K; the narrated ones are the lowest.

## How this changes our next Short
A question on screen from frame one that only the last line answers; the hero and one rival as the two things the eye follows; the picture slows for 2-3 s before the reveal while the voice carries on; land on one settled picture that says something about "you", and let the ending caption re-read the opening question.
