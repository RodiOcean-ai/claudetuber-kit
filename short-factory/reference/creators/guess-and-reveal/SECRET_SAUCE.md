# Veritasium: the secret sauce (15 Nov 2026)

## The recipe in one paragraph
**Put a real thing in front of the viewer, let them make a guess about it, make them wait for it, and show the guess fail on screen, then stop.** Frame one is a real object already about to do something (balls on a pool, a foot over the water, two ladders about to drop), not a face, a map or a cartoon. The viewer's own brain makes the prediction ("he'll get wet", "they'll land together"), a visible wait-and-see moment holds them (a countdown, a hand on the rope, "let me see if I'm still dry"), the voice steps back while it happens, and the picture breaks the guess. Best of all, his own body is at stake. It ends on the "no way", in under a minute, with the title's promise paid and no advert. **The weak Shorts do the opposite: they talk you through an idea for two minutes, promise in words that something good is coming, and end on a sentence or an ask.** The hits don't win because they explain better; they win because the viewer's own broken guess is the hook.

## Why the hits win, in the viewer's head
Counted on his 10 most-viewed Shorts (24M-64M) and 3 weak 2026 Shorts (0.39M-0.69M). The counts are counted; the "why" is our reading.
1. **A guess, not a lesson.** A real object on frame one gives the eye a question before a word is understood (7 of 10 hits, 0 of 3 weak). A lesson can be "saved for later"; a prediction has to be checked now.
2. **A wait beats a promise.** A visible pending event (TENSION) is in 8 of 10 hits and 0 of 3 weak. Words promising a surprise (TEASE: "the answer begins with...") are in 0 of 10 hits and 2 of 3 weak. A promise asks for trust; a hand on the rope makes the body lean in (we "rehearse" what we watch).
3. **The break must be seen.** The surprise happens on camera in 6 of 10 hits (5 real, 1 animated) and 0 of 3 weak. The batteries Short *says* "a blue whale for half a second"; nobody sees it, so there is no jolt to rewatch or share.
4. **Something to lose.** His body, a priceless sphere, a telescope about to be switched off: 5 of 10 hits, 0 of 3 weak. People care more about what could be lost than about what could be learned (loss aversion). The cleanest proof is a remake, below.
5. **Quiet at the moment that matters.** In 4 of 10 hits the voice stops for 4.7-16.7 s while the thing happens; in the weak ones it never stops for more than 1.3 s and covers 90-93 % of the video (hits: 76 %). Wall-to-wall talk turns an event into a lecture.
6. **The last second decides the like.** Ends on the peak (the surprise, the "no way", a laugh) in 7 of 10 hits, 0 of 3 weak; pays the title's promise in 10 of 10 hits, 1 of 3 weak; no advert inside in 10 of 10, 2 of 3. People judge an experience by its best and its last moment (peak-end), and an unpaid promise feels like a small betrayal.

## Format, not luck
- **Hit-driven, but not one lucky clip.** 97 Shorts, 912M views. The #1 Short is **7.0 %** of all Shorts views (ColinAmazing's record clip: 39 %); the top 10 are **47 %**. Median Short 4.6M, average 9.4M; 23 of 97 are over 10M.
- **A remake proves it: the same black balls, 8 weeks apart.** "There are 96 million black balls on this lake" (24 Jun 2025, 66 s, a boat ride to find out why they're there): **8.4M**. "Can you swim in shade balls?" (19 Aug 2025, 147 s, he jumps into the deep end after the maker calls it dangerous, with a "Two. One." countdown): **64.6M**, **7.7 times** more. Same subject, same year, both real footage, and the longer one won: what changed is the body at stake and the wait. (View counts read twice: the channel list and each video's page, 15 Nov 2026.)
- **A second pair, weaker evidence:** aerogel on his own skin, "I'm about to step into this pool without getting wet" (2024, 58 s): **63.1M**; "How aerogel can make the ocean drinkable" (Aug 2025, 162 s, a fundraiser collab): **4.9M**. Same material, 13 times apart, but the second is also long and ends on a cause, so it can't prove one thing alone.
- **Decay: yes, and it is channel-wide.** Newest third of his Shorts (32): median **1.95M**, 1 over 10M. Middle third: 4.65M. Oldest third: **10.0M**, 15 over 10M. Since the green-rope hit (15 Jan 2026, 52M), 48 Shorts: median **3.1M**, none above 13M. **The recipe's 2026 proof is one Short** (the green rope, which has every piece: a real object, strangers guessing on camera, a wait, the result shown).
- **But the gap holds inside the slump.** Titles that open like a lesson ("Why...", "How...", "What is...", "Is there...", "Can you solve...") vs the rest: 2026, 20 Shorts, median **1.8M** vs **3.6M**; before 2026, 18 Shorts, **7.2M** vs **9.1M**. He did not drift into lessons (41 % of titles vs 38 % before), so the slump is the feed or the audience, not a change of recipe; the recipe still ranks first inside it. Title-level only.

## Against Zack's baseline and the other channels
- **Format: Zack wins.** Veritasium's hits run 58 s (Zack 30-35), about 140 words (Zack about 90), 2.6 words/s (2.8), voice only 10 LU above the music (about 16), hard cuts every 3.7 s (Zack flies one camera), and almost never loop (1 of 10; Zack's series loop is our rule).
- **What is new against Zack:** Zack's "But" is *spoken*: a wrong belief, then the twist in words. Veritasium's "But" is *shown*: the viewer holds the wrong belief themselves, and the picture breaks it. Zack also uses a spoken tease; on Veritasium's channel the tease belongs to the weak Shorts. And Zack never pauses over 0.5 s; Veritasium's best moments are silent.
- **Same family as:** MrBeast's one "will it land?" try and ColinAmazing's "will it land?" moments (the wait), GothamChess's setback (something goes wrong on screen), Nick's everyday object with one rule broken (a pool you don't get wet in). Dr Plants raises the music when his voice stops; Veritasium lets the real sound take over (splash, strain, laughter: music only 15-45 % of the time in three hits).
- **Mark Rober (same niche):** cuts every 1.8 s and talks at 3.3 words/s. Veritasium is the slowest-cut science channel we've measured and still wins: **the surprise carries the shot; he doesn't cut to look busy.**

## What we copy (the craft)
1. **Frame one: one real-looking object already about to do something odd**, big in the frame (in our lab: a foot over a pool, a lever, two things about to drop). Not the hero's face, not a chart.
2. **Make the viewer guess, then break the guess in the picture.** One line sets up the obvious guess ("You'd think he'd sink"); the 3D shows it fail. The "But" happens on screen.
3. **A wait-and-see beat instead of a promise.** A countdown, a hand on the rope, a foot over the water. Never "and the answer will surprise you".
4. **Never talk over the surprise.** The line before the reveal ends, the reveal plays on its own real sound (a splash, a snap), then the next line comments. Inside Zack's rule (no pause over 0.5 s); a longer 1-1.5 s silent beat only as an A/B test with the owner's OK.
5. **The hero's own body at stake** (our blocky hero is the one who jumps, steps in or holds the precious thing), and **a side character reacts** with body language (a faceless toy figure jumps back, a "whoa" in a gesture).
6. **End on the "no way"**: the reveal and the creator's name, never a summary; the title's promise paid inside the Short.
7. **A red arrow or A/B labels on the objects** (4 of 10 hits, 0 of 3 weak) to point the eye at what will surprise.

## What we don't copy
- His face, voice, name, catchphrases, real footage, his lab or locations; the borrowed lab clip with a credit line; the presenter-to-camera opening.
- **His Shorts format:** 58 s, 140 words, silent stretches of 5-17 s, hard cuts, voice only 10 LU over the music, no loop. Zack wins on all of these.
- Outrage and tribe calls to act (#SaveChandra), hashtag campaigns, sponsored Shorts, fundraiser asks.
- Danger words in Shorts titles right now: they won before 2026 but in 2026 they sit at 2.0M vs 3.3M. Good for long videos (43 long videos, median 13.0M vs 4.0M), not for our Shorts.
- Questions as a hook on their own (see above).
