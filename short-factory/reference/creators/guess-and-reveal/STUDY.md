# Veritasium study (@veritasium, 21.4M subscribers) - 15 Nov 2026

Studied: his **top 10 Shorts and top 10 long videos by views**, plus **3 weak Shorts** from the same channel picked for contrast (0.39M-0.69M views). All 23 measured by machine (cuts, loudness, voice/music split, Whisper words, voice style, sound classifier, hook frames) and reviewed by Claude (16 frames of every Short, the first 3 s, every Short transcript in full, the first 75 s and the last 35 s of every long video, all covers). Whole-channel title counts use the full lists (97 Shorts, 448 long videos).
Data: studies/veritasium ("veritasium - all data.xlsx", analysis/<id>/); copies of the workbook and sheets here, plus shorts_16frames_*.jpg (16 frames of each Short) and story_blocks.png.
Subscribers checked in two places (the channel list and each video's page data): 21.4M.

## The big picture
- **448 long videos (3.70B views in the list, median 4.8M, 133 of them over 10M) and 97 Shorts (912M views, median 4.6M, 23 over 10M).** An average Short (9.4M) earns about what an average long video earns (8.2M).
- **Shorts are hit-driven:** the top 10 Shorts are 47 % of all Shorts views. Long videos are spread: the top 10 are 17 % of long views.
- **The long channel changed its subject, not its craft.** His oldest 148 long videos are classroom lessons ("Atomic Theory" 350K, "What causes the seasons?" 302K): median **1.05M**. The middle 150: **4.85M**. The newest 150: **11.0M**. The newer titles are about danger, the impossible and the extreme (see Titles).
- **Shorts are in a slump in 2026:** the 49 Shorts posted since 15 Jan 2026 have a median of **3.2M** (3 over 10M; one is the green-rope hit, 52M); the 48 before them **8.7M** (20 over 10M). All 3 weak Shorts are from 2026. Part of every hit-vs-weak gap below is this era.
- Presenter-led science: he (or a guest presenter) is on camera in 7 of 10 hit Shorts; the rest are a lab clip, an animation and motion graphics. **We never copy a face**; what transfers is what the camera points at and when the voice stops.

## Hits vs weak: the psychology
Every row is counted on the 10 hit Shorts and the 3 weak Shorts. The mind-trick in the right column is our reading of why the count matters.

| What the hits do | Hits (of 10) | Weak (of 3) | What it does in the viewer's head |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Frame one is a real thing you can see**, doing or about to do something (balls in a pool, feet stepping into water, ladders dropping, scissors at a rope, a steel plate, a slinky, a spider on a ball) | **7** | **0** (a talking head, a satellite map, a 3D cartoon hotel) | **Seeing is believing.** A real object says "this really happened" and gives the eye a concrete question before a word is understood; a face or a map gives it nothing to predict. |
| **The surprise happens on camera**, against what anyone would guess: he steps out dry, the ladder hitting the table falls faster, the weight goes UP, the plate fights back both ways, the slinky's bottom hangs in the air (plus the black-hole animation) | **6** (5 real, 1 animated) | **0** (the batteries' "a blue whale for half a second" is said, not seen; the hotel never reaches its surprise) | **Prediction error.** The brain runs a guess, the picture breaks it, and that jolt is the strongest attention signal there is. It also makes people rewatch ("wait, what?") and share ("look at this"). |
| **A wait-and-see moment** (TENSION block): a countdown before the jump, "let me see if I'm still dry", "which one will fall faster?", "Cut", holding a priceless sphere | **8** | **0** | **Suspense through the body.** Watching a hand, a jump or a held breath, the viewer's own body "rehearses" it (the mirror effect). They stay to see it land. |
| **The voice stops and lets you watch** (4 s+ with no narration while the thing happens: 17 s round the jump, 6.5 s of ladders falling, 4.9 s in the pool, 4.7 s straining at the plate) | **4** | **0** (longest gaps 0.5, 1.3, 1.0 s; voice covers 90-93 %) | **Silence = something is about to happen.** A non-stop voice turns the video into a lesson the brain can "save for later"; a silent beat makes it an event you must see now. |
| **Something at stake**: his body in the deep end (the maker "refuses residential customers"), his skin in water, his strength, a 1-million-euro sphere he promised not to drop, a telescope about to be switched off | **5** | **0** | **Loss aversion and empathy.** People care more about what could be lost than about what could be learned. |
| **Real people react or guess on camera**: "Yo!", "That is trippy", "Oh my goodness... that is ridiculous", "It feels absolutely incredible", strangers guessing what the rope will do | **5** | **0** (the only "absolutely insane" is about a power bank's charging speed) | **Emotional contagion / social proof.** We copy the feeling we see; another person's amazement is proof it is worth watching. |
| **Short**: 67 s or less | **9** (the 10th, shade balls, 147 s, is a countdown story) | **0** (105-159 s) | **The completion urge.** A viewer can see the end coming; 2-2.5 minutes of explaining feels like homework and gets swiped. |
| **Ends on the peak** (the surprise, the "no way" or a laugh), not on a summary | **7** | **0** ("click the link in the description"; "these are the ponds that we're looking at"; "So everyone fits, no problem") | **Peak-end rule.** People judge an experience by its best moment and its last moment; the like and the rewatch come from the last second. |
| **Keeps the title's promise** (shows the thing the title or first line asks about) | **10** (the ladders show what happens and leave only "why" open, on purpose) | **1** (the hotel never runs out of room; the ponds' "why do these colours keep changing" and "half the people on Earth" are never answered) | **A curiosity gap must close.** An unanswered promise is felt as a small betrayal: no like, no trust for the next title. |
| **No advert inside** | **10** | **2** (the batteries Short is an advert from 52 s to the end) | **Reactance.** The reward (the answer) is swapped for a pitch; the viewer feels sold to. Channel-wide all 4 sponsored Shorts (2.1M, 3.8M, 1.3M, 0.39M) are under the 4.6M median. |

**What does NOT separate hits from weak (so don't copy it as "the secret"):**
- **Asking a question.** A spoken question in the first 11 s: hits 3 of 10, weak **2 of 3**. Across the whole channel, titles with "?" do worse: 35 Shorts, median **3.4M** vs 6.1M without (and worse inside both eras: 2026 1.8M vs 3.7M, older 7.4M vs 9.2M); 105 long videos, **3.0M** vs 5.0M. A question alone is homework; it only pulls when the viewer can SEE something they can't explain (a curiosity gap needs a concrete thing to hang on).
- **Editing speed:** hits 0-29 cuts a minute, weak 4-20. **Music:** 15-100 % in hits, 14-99 % in weak. **Sound effects:** about 3 a minute in both. **Loud opening:** 9 of 10 hits and 3 of 3 weak open louder than the rest.
- **The presenter's face as the opening picture:** 2 hits (the sphere, the Chandra appeal), 1 weak (batteries).
- **A topic that already won as a long video:** the 2 biggest Shorts retell long-video subjects (shade balls, aerogel), but so does the weak hotel Short (its long version has 38M).

**Rare levers that worked once each:** outrage and tribe (the #SaveChandra Short, 24M: "Congress is about to make a huge mistake", a 40 % cut, "an extinction-level event", a hashtag and a call to act; it has the highest likes per view, 5.0 %, and the most comments per view of the hits, 1.6 per 1,000); an open loop at the end (the ladders end on "Now, why did that happen?" and the description asks if people want the answer: 49,000 comments, the most of any hit Short). **Fear of missing out is not used** (0 of 13, apart from Chandra's deadline).

**In one line:** his hits put a real thing in front of you, let you guess, go quiet, and show the guess was wrong - in under a minute, ending on the "no way". The weak ones talk you through an idea for two minutes and end on a sentence.

## Why the channel earns more than a classroom science channel (what we can count)
We did not measure rival channels in this study (see the honest note). What we can count is how Veritasium's own views rose:
- **Titles about danger, the impossible or being wrong** ("danger", "deadly", "killed", "impossible", "disaster", "worry", "wrong", "mistake", "never", "no one"): 43 long videos, median **13.0M** vs 4.0M for the rest. It holds in his older videos (10.9M vs 2.0M) and is smaller but still there in the newest 150 (13.0M vs 11.0M). Mind trick: **negativity bias** - the brain checks threats first.
- **Superlatives** ("World's...", "Most...", "Simplest", "Lightest", "Biggest"): 31 long videos, median **14.0M** vs 4.2M; in Shorts 12 titles, median 8.7M vs 4.4M ("World's Roundest Object" 27M, "World's strongest magnet!" 17M, "The most dangerous problem in math" 17M). Mind trick: **status / social currency** - knowing the most extreme thing is something to tell friends.
- **Doing it on himself** (stunt verbs in the title: "Testing", "Renting a helicopter", "I buried myself", "I waterproofed myself", "We took $10,000", "Can you swim"): 7 Shorts, median **15.0M** vs 4.4M (6 of the 7 are pre-2026). "Can you..." titles: 5 Shorts, median 10.0M vs 4.5M.
- **"Contrary to what you may have heard"**: his #1 long video (112M) opens by correcting a belief ("their main purpose is not to reduce evaporation"), the blue LED (57M) by busting a myth ("LEDs don't get their color from their plastic covers"). That is prediction error in words.
- **Trust:** in the 10 long hits the sponsor never interrupts the story (0 of 10 mid-story; read at 91-100 % in 6, a one-line mention at the start in 3).
- **Older lessons were the same voice and the same science.** The jump from 1M to 11M medians came from choosing threat, impossibility, the extreme and real things filmed on location, not from faster editing (he cuts slower than any channel we studied).

## Hooks (first 3 s and first line, 13 Shorts)
- Voice at 0.0 s in 12 of 13 (batteries at 0.5 s). First line points at a real thing in 6 of 10 hits: "I've purchased 10,000 shade balls to try to swim in." / "I'm about to step into this pool without getting wet." / "These two chain ladders were dropped at exactly the same time..." / "What will happen to this weight if you cut the green rope?"
- A "you" first line in 4 of 10 hits ("If you drop a slinky...", "You can never see anything enter a black hole.") and 0 of 3 weak (the hotel's "you are the manager" comes at 8 s).
- A counter-intuitive claim stated in the first sentence in 3 hits (slinky, black hole, aerogel) - the picture then proves it.
- A **flash-forward**: the shade-balls Short opens at the jump, rewinds ("It was probably right about now that I thought back...") and returns with "Which brings us back to... Two. One." (also in the aerogel long video: "Let me back up for a moment").
- Weak openings: a presenter asking a question to camera; a satellite zoom with "There are electric blue ponds..."; a cartoon hotel with "Imagine there's a hotel with infinite rooms."
- Hook-oracle scores were not run in this pass.

| Block | Hit Shorts (10) | Weak Shorts (3) | Long openings, first 75 s (10) |
|---|---|---|---|
| HOOK | 10 | 3 | 10 |
| WRONG BELIEF (strangers or experts guess wrong; "you'd expect him to speed up") | 2 | 0 | 1 |
| "BUT" twist | 3 | 0 | 5 |
| ESCALATION | 9 | 3 | 10 |
| TEASE (a spoken promise of what's coming) | **0** | **2** | 6 |

| SETBACK | 3 | 0 | 1 |
| COMEBACK | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| PAYOFF | 10 | 3 (all flat or falling) | 4 |

- **The weak ones promise; the hits make you wait.** TEASE (words saying something good is coming) is in 2 of 3 weak and 0 of 10 hits; TENSION (a visible thing about to happen) in 8 of 10 hits and 0 of 3 weak.
- Hit shape: hook on the object -> short explanation (one cause, one picture) -> a wait-and-see -> the result shown -> stop. The explanation sits in the middle and is short; the weak ones are mostly explanation (6 to 8 steps: the hotel's guest, bus, infinite bus, infinite buses, spreadsheet, zigzag...).
- Long openings: "This is X" first words in 4 of 10 (shade balls, microchip, aerogel, "the most dangerous problem in mathematics"); a "But" turn inside 75 s in 5 of 10 (a belief the viewer holds is corrected in 2: the balls' real purpose, the LED's colour); death, danger or disaster named in the first 30 s in 4 of 10 (asteroid blast, "most dangerous problem", "deaths of millions", "not gonna be damaged? Not permanently.").

## Speed, transitions, scene lengths
- Hit Shorts: a new shot every 3.7 s (median), but three are nearly one take (ladders 0 cuts, slinky 2, black hole 2) and the longest single shots run 7-47 s. **The surprise carries the shot; he does not cut to look busy.**
- Hard cuts only; no whips or fly-throughs seen in any Short. Arrows or A/B labels in 4 of 10 hits (red on the ladders, the rope and the slinky; white in Chandra), 0 weak.
- One item per Short in 9 of 10 hits (the spiders Short has one experiment in 3 steps); the weak hotel has 5 escalating cases, the batteries 6 animals plus an advert.

## Visuals
- Real footage of a real object in 8 of 10 hits (animation only in the black hole; motion graphics in Chandra). Weak: real footage in 1 of 3 (the ponds' potash-making), a talking head with cartoon animals, a 3D animated hotel.
- Everyday objects made strange: a pool, a ladder, a slinky, a rope and two springs, a magnet and a plate, a ball. Special objects: a 1-million-euro silicon sphere, aerogel.
- The #3 hit (53M) is someone else's lab clip with a credit line ("Courtesy of Andy Ruina"), grey, one shot, 65 words.

## Sound and music
- Music audible most of the time in 7 of 10 hits (classifier 82-100 %); low in the plate (37 %), shade balls (45 %) and sphere (15 %) Shorts, where real sound (splash, strain, laughter) takes over. Music does not separate hits from weak.
- About 3 sound effects a minute (heartbeats under the aerogel step, ticks under the black hole); nothing in the first second.
- The weak ponds Short is mastered at **-31 LUFS**, 10-18 dB quieter than the hits (one-off; it plays faint on a phone).

## Captions
- Mixed: small white single words in a thin serif in 4 hits (aerogel, plate, black hole, spiders); bold 2-line captions in 3 (shade balls, rope in yellow, Chandra); none in 3 (ladders, slinky, sphere). All 3 weak have multi-word captions. Captions don't separate.

## Loop
- No designed loop: last frame vs first frame is 4-1,200x a normal frame step in 12 of 13 Shorts. The ladders Short is the only one whose ending ("Now, why did that happen?") reads straight back into its first line ("These two chain ladders were dropped..."), and it looks almost seamless (seam 1.3x a normal step: one grey shot).

## Titles and covers
- Shorts titles: median 6 words; "?" in 35 of 97 (worse, see above); first person / stunt verbs win (7 Shorts, median 15M); "AD -" titles 3 (median 2.1M).
- Long titles in the top 10: a big number ("96,000,000"), a threat ("asteroids to worry about", "Killed The Most People"), the impossible ("Almost Impossible", "No One Can Solve"), a mystery ("Alien Technology"), a superlative ("World's Lightest Solid!"), a stunt ("Flies Off Dam", "I Waterproofed Myself").
- Long covers (10 hits): one big object in the middle in 7 (aerogel twice, the three LEDs, the basketball, the asteroid, the machine, "3x+1"); text in 5, 1-4 words ("3x+1", "MAGNUS EFFECT", "EASY EASY ALMOST IMPOSSIBLE", "WHOOPS!", "This shouldn't exist."); an arrow in 3 (red on the asteroid, white on the LED and the ball); a real face in 2 (pointing at the pool, pointing at the balls), a cartoon face in 1. Same "one hero + few words" rule as MagnatesMedia's covers, but objects instead of faces.
- Shorts covers are frames from the video (8 of 10 hits show the object, 2 a face); covers barely matter in the Shorts feed.

## Long videos (10 hits)
- Median 20 min (3-55). First words at 0 s in 6 of 10 (7 within 1 s); a one-line sponsor mention near the start in 3 (at 8 s, 24 s and 177 s).
- Cold open on the thing itself in 5 (the balls being dumped, a chip on a fingertip, aerogel in a hand, a held LED, him standing at the wave pool), animation or space pictures in 3, the dam clip in 1, two people talking in 1 (the aerogel torch test).
- **Sponsor read last** in 6 of 10 (91-100 % in, tied to the story once: "a perpetual calendar, a reminder that each passing day brings the chance of an asteroid impact"); **never mid-story** (0 of 10).
- Endings: sponsor in 6; a tease of the next video in 2 ("the final episode of the Aerogel trilogy, so subscribe"); a credit to friends' channel in 1; a joke in 1 ("I'm waiting for you to say it looks like boba").
- About 1-2 "But"s a minute (0.8-2.0); questions to the viewer 0.3-2.3 a minute.

## What this means for our Shorts (for sf-secret-sauce)
1. Frame one: a real-looking object about to do something odd, filling the phone (we already have the rule; Veritasium's count is 7 of 10 vs 0 of 3).
2. Let the viewer guess, then show the guess was wrong ON SCREEN; a spoken "you'd think" only works if the picture proves it.
3. One silent beat (1-2 s in our 30 s format) while the thing happens; never talk over the "no way" moment.
4. Under a minute, one idea, end on the reveal - never on a summary or an ask.
5. The title promise must be paid inside the Short.
6. A question in the title is not a hook by itself.

## Honest note
- **3 weak Shorts is a small sample**, and all 3 are from 2026, when the whole channel's Shorts median fell from 8.7M to 3.2M. The ponds Short was 13 days old when measured. Counts say what the hits share and the weak ones lack, not what caused the views.
- We did **not** measure other science channels for this study, so "earns more than its niche" is not counted here. The two science channels we have measured are Mark Rober (86M subscribers, 13.6B views from 136 Shorts: far bigger in Shorts) and NileRed (about 11M subscribers). Veritasium sits between them; its edge over classroom science is shown by its own old-vs-new numbers (1.05M vs 11.0M medians).
- Whisper missed the strangers' voices in the green-rope Short (3-30 s), so its word count (124) and "voice covers" (51 %) are too low; it is left out of the silent-stretch count.
- "Surprise", "tension", "at stake", "ends on the peak" and the story-block levels are Claude's reading of frames and transcripts; the row counts are counted, the mind-trick column is interpretation.
- The sound classifier labels much of his room tone as "Zipper" or "Television"; treat its effect counts as rough.
- Likes are hidden on 2 hit Shorts. View counts are from YouTube on 15 Nov 2026 (the channel list rounds down; each video's own page agrees within 4 %).
- The long-video review covers the first 75 s and the last 35 s of each, not the middle.
