# Zack D. Films: how he does it

A study of his 10 most-viewed shorts and 10 most-viewed long videos, made on 29 October 2026.

Each video was downloaded, transcribed word by word, and split into voice and background. I measured loudness, cuts, speaking speed and sound effects, and looked at frames from every scene. A full breakdown of each long video sits in its own folder under "analysis".

---

## 1. The big picture

- **29.4 million subscribers and about 4,100 shorts, but only 12 long videos.** He is a shorts machine.
- **His top shorts get 124M to 255M views each. His best long video gets 20M.** The long videos are side projects.
- **He changed style around 2023.** His 2022 hits used real footage, stock clips and his own face at a microphone (the McFlurry spoon, the magic trick). Everything since is his own 3D animation.
- **Topic beats craft.** His top long video, the Ohio balloon disaster, has no animation at all, just 1986 news footage. In long form, the videos about death and money (Pepsi, Toyota, balloons) won.

## 2. Shorts: the recipe

### The numbers (top 10 shorts)

| | Range | Typical |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 23–40 s | **about 30 s** |
| Words | 70–115 | **about 85** |
| Speaking speed | 2.5–3.6 words a second | **about 2.8** (fast but clear) |
| When the voice starts | within the first half-second in **all 10** (caption already on screen at 0.4 s) | |
| Pauses longer than half a second | **0** in 9 of 10 | |
| Hard cuts | **none** in 7 of 10 (the 3D ones) | |
| Loudness of the whole mix | -8 to -15 LUFS* | about -11 |
| Voice above the music | 9–22 steps (LU) | **about 16** |
| Music under the voice | 51–100% of the time | **about 90%** |
| Sound effects | 0–16 a minute | **a few small whooshes only** |
| Like rate | 1.3–5% | about 3% |

\*LUFS is a loudness scale: closer to zero is louder. YouTube plays everything at about -14, so his loud files get turned down. What matters is the **gap**: the voice is always far above the music.

### Hook (first 3 seconds)

1. **The voice starts on frame one.** No logo, no music intro, no silence.
2. **The first 3 words name the subject:** "If you get a splinter", "When a firing squad", "If you shot a bullet".
3. **Something is already moving at 0.4 seconds:** a needle threads, a gun fires, veins slide out of a man's body, a finger jabs the wood.
4. **The subject sits alone** in the middle against a clean, pale-blue sky, so the eye has nowhere else to go.
5. **The first frame is the scene itself:** no text card, no title.

### Script formula (8 of 10 follow it exactly)

1. **"You" scenario:** "If you…", "When you…", "Have you ever…". It puts the viewer in the story.
2. **Wrong belief:** "most people…", "some people think…", "you might think…".
3. **The flip:** "But…". It appears in 8 of 10 scripts, usually around 6–10 seconds in.
4. **How it works, step by step,** with linking words: "As…", "Then…", "Afterwards…", "Once…".
5. **A payoff and stop.** Often it's a scale surprise ("ten times bigger than Central Park", "around the Earth 2 to 4 times") or a human sting ("sleep at night knowing…").
6. **No "subscribe", no outro, no logo at the end.** The video just ends on the last word.

The title is the question, and the first line restates it, so the viewer instantly knows what they'll learn.

### Footage

- **A semi-real 3D look:** believable people and props, soft daylight, and very clean, almost empty sets.
- **Medical cross-sections:** the camera dives into skin or veins and shows how it works inside, in bright red, yellow and blue.
- **Scale comparisons with familiar things:** a football field, the Grand Canyon, Central Park, the whole Earth.
- **One picture for every phrase.** When he says "bullet", you see a bullet; when he says "Japan", you see Japan. The picture follows the words almost word by word.
- **His brand lives inside the scene, not in a corner:** "ZACK D FILMS" is printed on the armour, the ammunition crate, the football field and the boat.

### Transitions

- **No hard cuts in his 3D shorts.** The camera flies the whole time. To change place he does a fast swipe with motion blur, only 2–3 frames long, or flies the camera through an object.
- **His biggest short (stitches, 255M) is one single camera move.** It starts on a walking leg, pushes into the wound, and pulls back out to the same walking leg. **The end matches the start, so the video loops.** People watch it twice without noticing, which pushes up the "watched more than 100%" figure the algorithm loves.
- The magic trick short asks for the loop directly: "Watch it again and see if you can notice."

### Sound and volume

- **The voice is king:** one calm, clear narrator, heavily compressed (squeezed to an even, steady loudness) and loud. It runs non-stop with no breathing gaps.
- **The music is a soft bed, 16 steps below the voice.** It's light, curious background music, present almost the whole time.
- **Sound effects are rare and small:** a whoosh on camera moves, a soft thud on an impact. He doesn't decorate.

### Captions

- **1 to 3 words at a time,** in white with a thin black outline, centred a little below the middle.
- They follow the voice exactly, word by word. The typos ("teh times bigger") show they aren't polished.

### Titles, descriptions, covers

- **Title = the question or "How / Why / The reason…", plus one emoji at the end:** 😯 😲 🤔 🤮 😧 😱 🤨. Sometimes "(explained)" is added.
- **Descriptions: none. Tags: none.** On shorts he puts zero effort here.
- **Covers: none designed.** The cover is just a frame from the middle of the video, with its caption visible.

### The numbers (top 10 long videos)

| 13 Unreleased Videos… | 11.2M | 12:13 | 5.8* | 12.1 | 2.6 |
| Toyota's $2,300,000,000 Mistake | 10.6M | 6:33 | 10.1 | 8.2 | 2.6 |

| The Disturbing Story Behind The Starbucks Logo | 4.8M | 3:51 | 9.4 | 13.5 | 2.7 |

| The Full Story of Norman | 2.8M | 3:05 | 9.1 | 11.0 | 2.4 |

\*The cut counter misses his flowing camera moves. In the 3D videos a new picture really comes about every 1.5 s.

### Hook

- **No intro and no "hi guys".** The premise is stated at 0 seconds, usually with a date or a number: "In 1992 Pepsi ran a contest…".
- **The first frame shows the strangest image of the story,** often the same as the thumbnail.
- **The title's promise is restated within 5 seconds.**

### Script

- Every section ends on a **pivot line** that reopens the question: "And this is where things went terribly wrong", "But there's just one problem", "Case closed. Well, not exactly".
- **The best payoff is held back to the end.** The balloon deaths come at 4:56 of 5:26, and the thumbnail image appears at 10:01.
- **No spoken call to action.** It ends on a one-line sting.
- **Before each reveal, the music drops to near silence.** Toyota cuts to 8 seconds of black screen with only a phone's busy tone.

### Footage

- The 2022 documentaries are collages: news footage, stock clips, newspaper scans, and Zack at a microphone.
- The 2026 videos are full 3D with **his mascot** (the guy with the blue headband and a "D" on his shirt).
- Brand placed inside scenes again: "Zack D. Mart", the "D" shirt, a "D" drawn in coffee foam.
- **Sponsor woven into the story:** in "5 Unreleased", the Zocdoc sponsor is a plot move inside the gorilla fight, not a break.

### Thumbnails

- **One strange, bright image, usually with no text** (7 of 10).
- **When there is text, it's 2–3 huge words** ("STUCK GAS PEDAL", "I'M A GENIUS!", "FICTION").
- **Saturated blue sky, or red and blood:** a bloody Pepsi cap, balloons swallowing a city, the mascot as a puppeteer.
- **An arrow** (3 of 10) points at the detail that creates the question.
- **The busy collage ("19 Creepiest") did worst** of the compilations.

### Titles and descriptions

- **Titles have a real name or number plus a dark word:** Disaster, Killed, Mistake, Lie, Disturbing. "$2,300,000,000" is written in full because the long number looks bigger.
- **Old descriptions:** a "dramatized footage" disclaimer, a 3–5 line story teaser ending on a question, and a fair-use line. **Tags: 6–16 search phrases**, including misspellings of his own name ("zac d films").
- **New descriptions:** chapters, a sources document, his Patreon, and the sponsor link at the top.

## 4. What we can use for our ads

Ranked by how much each would change our results:

1. **Start the voice (or the jingle's first line) on frame one, and show motion by 0.4 s.** In all 20 of his videos the voice starts within the first half-second.
2. **Put the subject in the first 3 words, and say it to "you".**
3. **Use the "wrong belief → But → how it works" spine.** For an ad: "You might think… But… Here's what [product] does…".
4. **Make the ending match the opening so it loops.** A seamless loop is the cheapest way to more than 100% watch time on shorts.
5. **No cuts: one flowing camera with quick motion-blur swipes.** It keeps the eye locked in, and it suits our video generator, since one long continuous shot beats many stitched clips.
6. **A clean, empty background with one subject in the middle,** like his pale-blue sky. Busy sets lose the eye on a phone screen.
7. **Keep the voice about 15 steps above the music, with almost no sound effects.** A clear voice wins over a rich mix.
8. **Captions of 1–3 words, white with a black outline, a bit below centre,** synced word by word.
9. **Put the brand inside the scene** (printed on a prop or a shirt), never as a logo in the corner and never as an end card.
10. **Shorts title = the question + one emoji.** Don't waste time on short descriptions or tags. Put the effort into the first frame instead, because that frame is also the cover.

**Honest note:** his formula works because each short answers a curiosity question people already have ("how do stitches heal?"). An ad doesn't start with that curiosity, so it has to create it in the first second. That's why lessons 1–3 matter most for us.

## 5. Update 12 Nov 2026: the scripts of his top 100 Shorts

The scripts of his 100 most-viewed Shorts (71M-255M views) were transcribed and measured. Full results: `skills/shorts-format/references/top-100-scripts.md` in the short-factory plugin; data in `reference/creators/shorts-format/top100/`. The owner's course built from it: "Zack Script School" ((a private page, not included)).

- **The box:** median 29 s, 81 words, 5 sentences of about 15 words, 2.8 words a second, no pauses, 0 of 100 say "subscribe".
- **Line one:** "If…" 35, cold open mid-action 29, "When/As…" 17, a question to the viewer 8.
- **Glue:** 85 % of the sentences after the first open with a linking word (59 %) or point back at what was just shown (26 %). "And" opens 69 sentences (and 29 last sentences), "But" 62, "Now" 23, "You see" 18.
- **The first "But":** in 64 of 100, at a median 9.0 s (middle half 6.4-12.6 s).
- **Seven story types:** chain 28, myth flip 24, true story 16, secret reason 13, guide 10, ladder 5, fable 4. "But" lives in flips, stories and ladders; chains and guides run on As / Once / Then / This.
- **Endings:** result 45, human sting 25, scale 9, back to you 8, punchline 8, question 4, watch again 1.
- **No fragments:** only 2.5 % of his sentences are under 6 words.
