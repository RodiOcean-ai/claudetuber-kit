## 2. Core beliefs about packaging

- **Packaging beats the video.** "I think it's actually more important than the video itself" (6:03). Later: "I will like die on this hill" (1:49:34).
- **Packaging = title + thumbnail + intro.** The intro counts "because of the hover play aspect" (when a viewer hovers over a thumbnail and the video starts playing) (7:10-7:15).
- **Packaging communicates the idea.** "Communicating a really good idea in a very good way" (19:06). The idea itself is "literally everything" (18:03).
- **Packaging has three layers: concept, angle, design.** Design is "the 10% frosting"; concept and angle are the 90% underneath (1:04:33-1:05:12, 1:07:43).
- **Video 5/10 is fine; packaging must be 10/10.** "Now let's make your packaging 10 out of 10" (28:16). First the video must be "good enough to serve a specific audience" (28:08).
- **Competitors with worse videos win on packaging.** "Their videos suck compared to mine." / "they're better at this than you" (6:28-6:31).
- **Viewers are won by packaging and kept by content.** "Earned by packaging and they stay because of content" (1:49:29).
- **Idea vs packaging depends on your level.** Aiming for 100K: "decent idea, great packaging" works. Aiming for 10M: the better idea matters more, because viewers must watch 80-90% (2:11:15-2:11:47).
- **YouTube is a partner, not a slot machine.** It is "a distribution platform" and is "begging you" for big videos (1:40:31-1:42:05). Skill, not money: one client spent $100K on sketch comedy and flopped (1:44:17).

## 3. TITLES: every rule

His four title tools (46:47): "irony... max loading... rhythm... three focal points."

1. **Irony or a contrarian take.** "There is an ironic element to the title" (1:43). Smart people aren't idiots, so the title claims the opposite, and the swear word adds aggression that fits Mark's brand (1:58-2:09). *Example:* "Why Intellectuals Are F***ing Idiots". Mark posted it as "Intellectuals Are F***ing Idiots", dropping "Why" (2:20).
2. **Max loading** (his own term). It goes beyond Aprilynne Alter's "front-loading" (captioned "April Lynn"), which puts key words first because titles get cut off after ~50 characters (47:08-47:20). "What if like every single word was just that" (47:28). It means the fewest words that still give all the information: "there's no fat" (47:33-47:43).
3. **Rhythm.** Read the title in your head, the way you read Shakespeare's sonnets. "Less resistance when reading the title internally" means a better title (48:19). It should "roll off the tongue" (48:31; e.g. *Much Ado About Nothing*). The failure test: "The moment they have to like read it again" (48:55-48:59).
4. **Three focal points, no more.** "You just want three things to point at" (49:04). *Example:* "I let food companies control my diet for 7 days": food companies / control diet / 7 days. It was slightly over, but he allowed it because the rest was simple (49:11-49:30).
5. **Use digits, not words.** "7" instead of "seven" "because it transcends language" (49:48-49:52).
6. **Don't summarise the video.** "A lot of people try to make their titles... a summary" (1:47:04). *A/B test:* "top 10 exercises for violin players" vs "only 2% of violinists understand this". The second had ~74% more watch time and won "by a long shot" (1:46:44-1:47:02). Why: even people who don't care about violin wonder what the 2% know (1:47:09-1:47:41).
7. **Use plain words everyone knows.** "How to be an autodidact" went to "how to work like a genius does". "Nobody knows what an autodidact is" (1:34:36). The video jumped from 8/10 to 6/10 on the creator's own ranking (1 = best), so it improved (1:34:56).
8. **A promise that lowers effort.** "How to master vibrato in 25 minutes" (the video was 25 minutes long). "Heavily relying on the fact that people are lazy" (5:34). "Master" plus a short time beats a plain "how to play" (5:38-5:47).
9. **Title and thumbnail text must not repeat each other.** The thumbnail said "perfect vibrato"; the title said "master... 25 minutes". "Not like reusing the same real estate" (5:21-5:25).
10. **Write broad, for people outside the niche.** "How do I get the people that don't give a [F] about any of that?" (1:48:53-1:48:56). Compare good journalism (1:49:05).
11. **Fear works.** "When you do fear based packaging, I know it crushes" (57:50). Drowning is "the number one fear" (57:59). Example: Michelle Khare's escape-box thumbnail, ~8M.
12. **Lock the title before you film.** The first sentence of the video is the title word for word, so "we can't really change the title too much now" (2:01:24-2:01:27).

Other titles he praises: "I convinced a stranger to rob a bank" (Mack, ~21M; 54:14). "I secretly hid in my parents' bedroom" (2:00:46). "Police at my house *2:00 a.m." (instant 1-2 of 10; 1:43:33). Airrack's "I lived in a grocery store" was the model for the bedroom video (1:58:51).

## 4. THUMBNAILS: every rule

1. **The cover-the-text test.** "Cover the text, and ask yourself, what does this tell you?" (14:07). Usually nothing. "80% of what's working in the thumbnail is the text" (14:16-14:21).
2. **First principles: strip it down.** "What elements can you take out... before it is no longer functioning" (13:47-13:53). A bare portrait photo failed: "didn't tell us enough" (13:58).
3. **Thumbnail text must be brief.** "Brevity is the soul of wit" (14:57). "Be as brief as possible" (15:00).
4. **The image and text work as a pair.** "The yin and yang to this text" (15:16). With the same face, different text changes how people read the video (14:32-14:41).
5. **"Guided intrigue" text.** It tells viewers "what they should be expecting without giving too much info" (5:02-5:05).
6. **The arrow.** "If you put an arrow on your thumbnail, you're way more likely" to get more impressions; A/B tests show "a crazy difference" (4:32-4:48).
7. **Kill the background and stand out from competitors.** A black background puts focus "on the things that matter" when rivals use white, green or blue (4:12-4:28).
8. **Faces: the eyes matter, not the open mouth.** "Not getting a higher CTR because your mouth is open" (51:06). "It's like the white in the eyes specifically" (51:11). "We look for the white in people's eyes" (1:59:50).
9. **Guide the eye.** At the top level the thumbnail tells a story: rule of thirds, depth of field. The subject sits in the left foreground, because people read left to right; then a diagonal leads to the parents asleep in bed; then an arrow and a word ("parents"/"asleep"). Without guidance, the eye "just going to go all over the place" (1:59:17-2:00:09).
10. **Mid-action.** "We try to give the still image motion" (2:00:20). Show conflict (2:00:13).
11. **One accent colour.** The arrow is "the only red thing in this whole thumbnail" (1:59:57-2:00:37).
12. **Title and thumbnail split the information, and are tested.** Ask "what did we leave out of the title". The title has "parents", so test thumbnail text with "sleeping" (2:00:44-2:00:58).
13. **Angle must stay on-brand.** For "I knitted medieval armor", the thumbnail shows an arrow hitting the knitted armor, with the text "It actually works" (1:06:33-1:06:43). Being hit by a bull would be "a bad angle on a good concept" (1:07:09-1:07:23). "Playing against their expectations with the angle" (1:07:00).
14. **The thumbnail must be paid off in the intro.** Ferran held his homework paper in the thumbnail and held the same paper at the start of the video: "we paid off the intro instantly" (1:43:12-1:43:26).
15. **Look for inspiration outside YouTube.** Twitter images, films, TV. MrBeast's packaging lead Chucky took a God's-eye-view thumbnail from *Severance* (2:04:44). Twitter is "a big one" (2:05:18).

## 5. INTRO / first seconds

- The intro is packaging (hover play) (7:13).
- **It doesn't have to be fast.** People think it must be "really fast-paced" (32:06). Fast cuts help, but "don't be fooled to think that's what does it" (32:15). Example: a Neil deGrasse Tyson lecture with a slow fade from black worked for its audience (32:20-32:47). Still, for the broadest audience, "you probably want it a little faster" (33:41).
- **Say the whole point in the first 5 seconds.** "I'm going to say the very point of the video in the first 5 seconds" (34:05). MrBeast: "I captured 10 people..." then the content starts at once (34:10-34:25).
- **Then over-deliver.** Add one thing "not advertised in the thumbnail... not advertised in the title" (34:36-34:40). "Started over delivering after this moment" (34:45). Jake calls this "a perfect 30-second intro" (35:08).
- **Milestones: 5 s, then 30 s, then 1 min, then 3 min.** "Once you get them to a 3-minute mark... you won" (35:26-35:30). Then about 30% are still watching at the end (35:33).
- **The first sentence is the title.** "You bet your ass it's I secretly lived in my parents' room" (2:01:11). "If it's anything else, that APV's dropping" (2:01:17-2:01:20). Show the thumbnail's scene in the opening (2:01:31-2:01:38).
- **Rush the setup and get to the conflict.** "Knock this building phase down as fast as possible" (2:01:46-2:01:51).
- Black screen plus on-screen text in the first 5 s (MrBeast style) was used on the ~50M Minecraft video (12:05-12:12).
- A poor intro score often means the intro doesn't match the packaging (1:13:32-1:13:36).

## 6. IDEATION and angles

- **The Dostoevsky story.** *Crime and Punishment* argues intellectuals, left isolated, rationalise evil; *The Idiot* is the inverse (19:21-20:03). He said aloud: "Oh, he thinks intellectuals are idiots." / "That's the title" (20:06-20:11). He wrote it down for later.
- **Look outside YouTube.** Fiction and philosophy "free your mind" (21:41). "I find more inspiration outside of YouTube" (22:05). YouTube is "my last resource" (2:05:01). The reason: YouTube is "a zero-sum game" and competitors aren't reading these books (22:16-22:27).
- **Beginners: outlier theory.** "You can just copy what someone else does" (13:20). Use 1of10 (captioned "one of 10"). A "1 of 10" is a video that beat that creator's last 10 uploads (23:24-24:01). Example: a creator copied the Founders podcast's thumbnail style, got 100K in week one, and went from 5-10K to 100K (38:58-39:31).
- **Don't stay on copying.** "Relying on Outlier Theory will kind of start to kill the growth" (39:56). Learn "why did this one work" (40:03).
- **Start with what only you have.** "What's the thing that you have that nobody else has" (0:45). With no "wow factor", "it's not going to work" (26:30-26:37).
- **Concept, then angle.** Boring niches can win: "knitting is boring... Good. I'm going to use that against you" (1:06:12-1:06:15).
- **100K to 1M: improve ideas through process.** "We can't be results-based" / "heavily process-orientated" (1:03:54-1:03:57). The bottleneck "almost always it's in packaging, ideation... the angle" (1:04:24-1:04:28).
- **1M to 10M: go broad with "niche integration".** Mix your niche with something everyone knows. Staplers alone are too narrow; staplers plus money becomes "I bought staplers until I went broke" (1:37:17-1:39:14). A Chipotle buy-now-pay-later thumbnail reaches "everyone who is feeling the pressure" of the economy (1:38:12). Johnny Harris's wealth-brackets video reaches people who "don't care about money" (1:39:48-1:40:29). "Ten is not an accident" (1:41:47).
- **Data only corrects.** Fixing drop-offs moves "800,000" toward 2-3M, not 10M (1:41:37-1:41:47).
- **Move a proven format to a new audience.** Airrack's "lived in a grocery store" became "hid in parents' bedroom" for younger viewers (1:58:51-1:59:01).
- **Keep asking what's wrong with the idea.** At the top level, "every idea is wrong"; ask "What's wrong with it?" every day (1:57:03-1:57:21).

## 7. METRICS

- **His three metrics: APV, AVD, impressions** (1:13:09-1:13:56).
- **APV (average percentage viewed) for the first 30 seconds.** He uses it as the intro score. Highest he has seen: "91%" on a 20M-view video (36:27-36:41). "If this started at 75, you don't get 91 later" (36:52-36:55). He calls it the most overlooked metric (1:45:44). It shows "how well did our packaging match our intro" (1:46:15). A/B tests are won on watch time, and the losers "don't make it past the 30 seconds" (1:46:06-1:46:14).
- **AVD (average view duration)** means "long-term growth" (1:13:43). Metrics falling over time are fine: "your video's being pushed out more" (37:21-37:27).
- **Impressions:** "the only way we are getting views is impressions" (1:13:54).
- **CTR (click-through rate) is "always overvalued"** (1:44:47). "What's good CTR? I don't know what that means" (1:14:06-1:14:08). 20% on 100 impressions is worthless; 1-2% on huge impressions is "wonderful" (1:14:13-1:14:42, 1:45:34-1:45:41). "You want your CTR to drop"; you don't want it "to start low" (1:45:05-1:45:12). Warning sign: about 2% at 48 hours "probably" means the packaging is wrong (1:45:14-1:45:26). He uses CTR only as a rough guide to angle and packaging (1:14:51).
- **Reading the retention graph:** find the "fall off" first (1:22:24). Expect an early drop to an APV of about 80%, then hold it as flat as possible. Normal end: 30-40%. Great: above 40% with a flat final stretch (1:22:37-1:22:58).
- **How to make a flat ending.** Build the whole video toward one decision ("she can only pick one to keep"). Slow it down rather than cutting faster; add music and heartbeat sounds (1:23:14-1:24:05). "The more stress and conflict" you add, the flatter it gets (1:24:04).
- **A/B testing:** test even on a new channel, "more so than you would with a more prominent channel" (17:19-17:25). He has seen single videos go "to a million views" because of split tests (16:28-16:35). But don't test 100 thumbnails in a few hours (1:32:54-1:33:06). The first ~5 days can look "dormant", and changing early can "kill the biggest growth spurt" (1:33:46-1:33:57). Change only for a real mistake (1:34:24-1:34:28).
- **RPM (ad money per 1,000 views):** $1-4 is low, $5-10 about average, $15+ is "really good". He has seen $95 (1:16:20-1:17:05). The same talking-head video can earn 10x more depending on the words used (1:17:10-1:17:32).
- Only 12% of long videos pass 1,000 views (38:01).
- Views are data: "I like data, and that's what views are" (1:25:29-1:25:31).

## 8. CADENCE

- **Moving from 100K toward 1M per video: weekly, or every two weeks at minimum.** "We cannot miss a week right now because we need data" (1:25:15-1:25:20). Every upload is a test (1:25:24). Uploading less than every two weeks loses data, growth and reps (1:25:51-1:26:03).
- **Rarely more than once a week.** "Very rarely do I suggest someone to upload more than once a week" (1:26:58). More "is probably going to hurt the channel" (1:27:44-1:27:46). Exceptions: low-effort gaming, reaction or stream-clip channels (1:27:31-1:27:58).
- **Big videos need low frequency.** About 1M per upload happens only at once a week, twice a month or once a month, because those videos "take way more time and way more money" (1:28:08-1:28:31).
- **One creator uploads once a quarter** and averages 8-12M per upload (1:24:46-1:24:52).
- **The Royalty Family.** "If we want to get more views per month, we should upload less" (1:28:35-1:28:38). Monthly views: about 150-160M at their peak, about 100M when he joined (a "fall off"), and 200M when he left (1:28:49-1:29:06). He admits "we changed a bunch" (intros, thumbnails, idea types), but agrees uploading less was a big part (1:29:08-1:29:12, 1:29:49, 1:30:24-1:30:27).
- **Once you hit your goal, cadence is a choice** (1:25:06-1:25:09, 1:26:07-1:26:19).

## 9. Packaging checklist (score a title + thumbnail + first line, 1 point each)

1. **One-glance idea.** Is the idea clear and appealing before any design is added? (concept and angle are 90%, 1:04:33)
2. **Irony or flip.** Does the title claim the opposite of what people expect, or take a strong side? (1:43)
3. **Max loaded.** Can any word be removed without losing meaning? If yes, fail. (47:28)
4. **Rhythm.** Read it in your head once. Does it flow without needing a second read? (48:55)
5. **Three focal points.** Three or fewer things to point at? (49:04)
6. **Not a summary.** Does it create a question ("only 2% understand this") instead of describing the contents? (1:46:21)
7. **Plain words, digits.** Would an outsider know every word? Are numbers written as digits? (1:34:36, 49:48)
8. **Outsider pull.** Would someone who doesn't care about the niche still click? (1:48:53)
9. **Cover-the-text test.** Does the thumbnail text carry the intrigue, and is it brief? (14:07, 14:57)
10. **No repeated words.** Does the thumbnail text add something the title doesn't say? (5:21)
11. **Eye path.** Is there one clear first stop (whites of the eyes or the hero), one accent colour or arrow, and a left-to-right path? (1:59:34, 4:32)
12. **Motion and conflict.** Is the still frame mid-action, with tension? (2:00:13-2:00:20)
13. **Thumbnail paid off at once.** Does the first shot show the thumbnail's scene? (1:43:24)
14. **First line = title.** Does the opening sentence state the title or the point within 5 s? (2:01:11, 34:05)
15. **Over-delivery hook.** Within ~30 s, is there one extra promise the packaging didn't advertise? (34:36)

## 10. Where he contradicts common advice or our habits

- **A/B test from the very first video.** Most advice says don't; he says new channels should test more (16:04-17:25).
- **CTR is not a target.** Judge by impressions, views and APV (1:44:47).
- **Intros don't have to be fast.** They must serve the audience and state the point (32:06-33:13).
- **Upload less to get more views** at higher levels; more than weekly usually hurts (1:26:58, 1:28:35).
- **Titles should not summarise.** Our "How did X get Y?" format is partly a summary and a question. He never discusses question titles directly. He does warn against summary titles, and his favourite openings are bold claims ("Only 2%...", "I secretly..."). Our question titles are worth testing against a claim version, e.g. "12 videos. 6 million subscribers." [inference, not his words]
- **Text on thumbnails is ~80% of what works** (14:16). Our long-video thumbnail rule of 0-3 words fits his "brevity", but he would say the words matter more than the picture.
- **Faces: "the white in the eyes"** is the first thing viewers look for (1:59:50). Our rule of "no faces / anonymous silhouettes" removes his main way of guiding the eye. If we keep silhouettes, we need another clear first stop (an arrow, the one red element). The open mouth doesn't matter (51:06).
- **The arrow** raises impressions in tests (4:32). Rarely mentioned elsewhere.
- **Emoji in titles: he never mentions them.** Neither for nor against.
- **Faceless/AI channels: "that's just not really what I do"** (26:46-26:50). He thinks viewers will reject AI stand-ins for people (1:56:09-1:56:17). Yet he predicts "the next Mr. Beast... going to be in animation", with AI making animation cheaper (2:12:07-2:13:11).
- **Ideas from books, film and Twitter, not from other YouTube videos**, once past the beginner stage (2:04:28-2:05:18).
- **Fixing data won't take you from 1M to 10M; only a broader idea will** (1:41:37). That argues for picking subjects with outsider appeal (e.g. "survival", "money"), not just more polish.
