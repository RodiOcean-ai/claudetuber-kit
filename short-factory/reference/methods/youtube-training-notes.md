## The five steps
| # | Step | His rule in one line | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Find ideas people already watch | Knowing your audience is not proof they will click. Get **evidence of demand on YouTube** before you record. | Before a new episode or a new series, prove the subject already pulls views in our niche. |
| 2 | Package it | Demand creates the chance; **title + thumbnail + hook + structure decide how much of it you catch.** Copy the *reason* a format works, never just its words. | Our "How did X get Y?" title and one-hero thumbnail must be checked against what is winning, and we must say *why* it wins for this audience. |
| 3 | Hook and satisfaction | After the click the viewer asks "did I pick the right video?" Answer with **promise, proof, plan**. The real goal is **viewer satisfaction** (they got what was promised, and more), not retention or click rate. | Our first line repeats the title's promise; the payoff must over-deliver on exactly that promise. |
| 4 | Turn viewers into customers | The call to action must feel like a gift and the "most useful next step". | Not used (our Shorts end on the last word). The pinned comment is our only "ask". |
| 5 | Double down with data | Each upload is evidence. Keep **why** a winner worked and find a new or better way to use the same signal. | After 24 h / 7 d stats, the next episode keeps the winning reason (topic, format, hook) and changes the rest. |

## Step 1 in detail: the depth-first search ("demand map")
The diagram the owner sent. Plain words:
- **Root:** the niche in simple words.
- **Seed list:** ask an AI for the core search lines of the niche (about 10). Push them onto the **stack** (a to-do pile: the newest find is explored first).
- **Branch:** take the top search line -> note the videos that clearly beat the rest -> open the first one's **channel** -> note that channel's **standout videos** (far above its own normal views) -> go back to the video and follow its **recommendations** -> repeat.
- When a branch stops giving new useful finds, **step back** and take the next item on the stack. Then the next search line.
- Record everything in one **research database** (video, channel, views, how far above the channel's normal).
- The goal is to understand the **whole niche**, not one competitor. "YouTube is just a game of decisions"; more visibility = better decisions.
- He says to hand this to an assistant or AI: it is long and tiring. Our tool for it: `niche_map.py`.

## Awareness levels (from the book *Breakthrough Advertising*)
- People can want the **outcome** without knowing the **mechanism**. Sell the outcome they already want, and use the unfamiliar mechanism as the fresh reason to care ("If you don't understand insulin, you don't understand fat loss": 230k views).
- If direct demand exists, build around it. If not, change the subject or **bridge** it to something people already want.
- For us: viewers want "how do I/they go viral"; our mechanism is "the craft under it" (hooks, story structure, music). Package the outcome, reveal the mechanism.

## Packaging rules
- The **thumbnail is read first**: the problem and the outcome must be clear in one glance, or they never read the title. YouTube is **winner-take-all**: about 20 videos on screen, one gets the click.
- Title and thumbnail make **one promise together**; a format is a proven way to frame that promise ("...so fast it feels like cheating": an outcome they want, faster than expected, like an unfair edge).
- **Never copy a format into the wrong audience** (a MrBeast "$1 vs $10,000" frame on a business audience makes no sense). Check the viewers' intent first.
- **Test the packaging beside real competitors** (mock it up next to the feed, ask friends "would you click this one?").

## Hook: promise, proof, plan
1. **Promise:** repeat exactly what they clicked for.
2. **Proof:** why listen to us (our edge: "we measured their top 10 Shorts").
3. **Plan:** where the video is going, so they commit.
Editing removes friction, but **cannot rescue a weak idea or an empty video**. Match the style to the promise (a raw "I show everything" video must look raw).

## The satisfaction loop
Right people click -> they watch and feel satisfied -> YouTube tests it with more people like them -> repeat. The video that wins the click **and** the watch takes most of the attention.

## Rules he "fires off" at the end
- **Your most-viewed video can be your least valuable:** an off-topic hit brings viewers who ignore everything after. The channel is **one campaign**; each video is one ad in it. Do not chase random topics.
- **One viral video is a clue, not proof.** Look for repeated evidence; a title format may have failed 100 times you did not see.
- **A result has more than one cause:** the "I made 300 videos and learned this" format worked because of its **20-40 point structure**, not the title alone. Copy title, thumbnail, structure *and* delivery style together.
- **Look at the losers too** (positivity bias proves any bad idea). Positive evidence shows an idea can work; negative evidence shows when and why it fails.
- **Specificity costs reach**: go as broad as you can **without losing the core viewer's "this is for me"**.
- A visual that does not **clarify, prove or help the viewer follow** is "just movement" (same as our rule: pictures carry the words).
- Pre-published research is a **guess**; the market votes. Diagnose each upload (views -> watched -> subscribed) and fix the bottleneck.
- Doubling down example: "The new rules of X" -> "Brutally honest advice about X in 7 minutes" -> he invented "The new era of X has just begun" by keeping the same psychology (a shift nobody noticed) in new words.
