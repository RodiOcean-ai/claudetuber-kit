# Study notes: "The Psychology of Storytelling That Will Change Your Life" (Joanna Wiebe)

Video oCnxnaVg0bY, 13:58, posted 29 Jan 2026, about 467k views; channel 152k subscribers (a copywriting agency founder). Studied from the full transcript on 4 Nov 2026 (`studies/method_videos/oCnxnaVg0bY`). The "studies show" numbers in the video are hers and unchecked; we keep the principles.

Eight principles, each with her test, then what it means for a 30-second Short.

| # | Principle | Her point | Her test | For our Shorts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Predictive processing** | The brain predicts what comes next; too predictable = boredom. A hook is not sensational, it is **uncertainty the brain can't predict, then a twist that resolves it** (Dan Pink: "I did something I regret..." -> "I went to law school"). Sensory anchor: "It all started with the smell of gasoline." | Read someone the first line: do they know what it's about? are they intrigued? Both must be yes. | Line 1 sets up a question the viewer can't guess the answer to; the "But" pays it with a twist. |
| 2 | **Establishing shot** | Orient first: Friends opens on Central Perk, not inside. Chris Rock makes Mississippi clear with one analogy (the scary van ride from the Caribbean airport). | Read only the opening: can they answer **where? who? what's the vibe? what to look at?** | Frame one shows the world and the subject at once (our "subject clear by 1.6 s"). |
| 3 | **Word pictures** | Concrete words beat abstract ones every time; the audience remembered "chicken" from an email about video lead capture. Concrete words are stored twice (words + image). | Close your eyes while it's read aloud (or text-to-speech): what do you **see**? Nothing = rewrite. Add colour, pattern, texture or shine; swap summary words. | Every line names a thing we can show (a crown, a rook, an hourglass), not "success" or "strategy". |
| 4 | **Pick the structure by the feeling** | Each structure is a promise to the brain. **Save the Cat** (opening image, theme, catalyst, midpoint, all-is-lost, finale) for satisfying rhythm; **Problem-Agitation-Solution** for urgency and momentum; **Hero's Journey** for transformation; **And-But-Therefore** for clarity. Wrong choice = the brain feels a clash. | First name what the audience should **feel**, then choose. | See the mapping table in the `sf-story-craft` skill. Our Zack spine is an And-But-Therefore with a ladder. |
| 5 | **Chekhov's gun** | Show a gun in act one, fire it by act three. Every emphasised detail is a promise; one that never pays off leaves an open loop that frustrates. When everything pays off, the audience trusts and leans in. | Read every line: **what job does it do?** (build desire, handle a doubt, give proof, create urgency). No job = it's fired. Edits alone lifted results 20-40%. | The line-job table: every line and every on-screen object has a job, and everything planted is paid off. |
| 6 | **Create a villain** ("mega mean mouse", after copywriter Eugene Schwartz: don't sell a better mousetrap, sell a bigger, scarier, hungrier mouse) | Ask what is destroying the person's happiness or success. Make it specific (not "disorganisation" but "your generic CRM that shows client files to the wrong people"). People see themselves in it. | Four steps: **stack the consequences, blame outside forces, list the dead solutions, say why they failed.** | Our villain is never a real person. It's the obstacle: the algorithm, boredom, the "you need a title / a studio / a face" belief, the swipe. |
| 7 | **Sugar coating** | Wrap the medicine (what they need to know) in sugar (what they want to feel): drama, conflict, humour, movement. "If it bleeds, it leads." | Name the medicine, name the sugar, pair them. | Each fact rides on a picture with motion or drama (the rook flying, phones piling up), never a list read out. |
| 8 | **Full-circle ending (bookending)** | Return to the opening, but **not the expected way**: Shrek doesn't become a prince; Fiona becomes an ogre. The ending the viewer wants, with a twist, so looking back the journey feels purposeful. | Remove the bookend: does the story still feel complete? If yes, the ending was decoration. | Our loop returns to frame one, but the meaning must have changed (the opening question gets a different answer than the viewer expected). |
