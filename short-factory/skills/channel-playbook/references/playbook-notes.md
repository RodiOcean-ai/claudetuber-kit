### I05 - I Tried YouTube Shorts for 365 Days! (4:58, 2023)
- **Hook (0:00-0:30):** two years, millions of views on a faceless Marvel-facts channel, but says he did it all wrong and it can be done in months; promises the exact method.
- **Arc:** status quo = faceless channel / call = do it faster / mentor = none (his own data and recipe list) / trials = first 3 weeks, first viral (2:06) / reward = monetised Dec 2022 (2:56), $20 -> $150+/month / crisis (after the reward) = Sony copyright strike, then demonetised for reused content (4:03-4:22) / return = back to voiceovers, re-apply.
- **Deeper problem:** determination (stated outright at 4:49).
- **Twist:** the cheap no-voiceover edits that made money killed the channel (not fair use).
- **Methods (in order):**
  - Treat the channel as a brand: fixed fonts and colours; logo/banner (made in Midjourney).
  - Pick a niche with proven demand: ask ChatGPT for popular niches, or the Wikipedia list of hobbies.
  - Study big channels in the niche for what works now (he found "did you know" movie facts); explain facts as simply as possible.
  - Do not overthink; publish fast; improve one thing per batch of ~5 videos (transitions, then subtitles, colour, SFX).
  - After ~10 shorts, check only views, retention and swipe rate, and re-watch your top shorts; write a "viral recipe list" (his: shorter subtitles, faster scene changes, a joke at the end).
  - The recipe decays ("what worked in the past may no longer work today"); when views drop, redo the analysis. Round 2 found: sound effect at the start, an attention-grabbing graphic, no voiceover.
- **Sponsor:** none. **Devices:** self-mockery about unfunny jokes, YouTube email shown on screen.

### I06 - How I Broke YouTube with 1 Video (5:54, 2023)
- **Hook (0:00-0:30):** stats of his first video on a new channel after 3 weeks: 10% click-through, 50% retention, 10K+ views (description: 20K, 1K subs). Plays the common advice to make 25-50 videos first, then rejects it: "This is terrible advice." (0:21)
- **Arc:** thin; the video is the proof (it says this very video was the experiment). Status quo = didn't understand storytelling (1:29) / mentor = a graph he found (2:04) / reward = the 12-step cycle / return = handing the chart to the viewer.
- **Deeper problem:** don't waste your first video; every life experience is a story.
- **Methods:**
  - Algorithm wants clicks and watching: idea, title+thumbnail, editing, story; max time on each. Master retention before click-through.
  - With existing skills, skip volume posting; plan one strong video. Beginning-middle-end advice is too vague.
  - 12-step hero's journey cycle taught with How to Train Your Dragon (2:58-5:30), translated to YouTube: status quo = introduce the main character / call = why you're making this video + preview / assistance = someone more experienced or a knowledge source / departure = start the challenge / trials = obstacles / approach = tease the biggest threat / crisis = darkest point / treasure = new idea, motivation or a friend's support / result = goal achieved / return = keep or lose the treasure / new life = visible transformation / resolution = new normal.
  - Tip: leaving a comfort zone and changing is enough of a story.
- **Sponsor:** none; lead magnet = free printable chart (5:30).

### I08 - I Tried Making a Viral AI Video! (20:44, 2024)
- **Hook (0:00-0:30):** montage of AI-automation guru intros cut off by "I hate AI videos." Rant about soulless AI content; mentor voice: there must be a better way to use it (1:06).
- **Arc:** status quo = AI hater / call = mentor's question, plan to make a full AI film for a new channel and count views (1:45) / audience ideas montage (1:56) / departure = story via ChatGPT, storyboard (2:31-5:15) / trials = tool comparison, Midjourney learning curve, style control (5:32-13:22) / crisis = consistent character impossible, nearly quits and pivots (13:22-14:11) / mentor = callback to an earlier pep talk, asks why come this far to quit (14:11-14:51) / reward = character-reference method (15:00) / result = animation, voice, edit / return + twist = results withheld; doubts story-only AI videos and asks if you noticed the whole video (20:19).
- **Deeper problem:** finishing what you start (determination); soul vs slop.
- **Twist:** the AI voice reveal (18:53: the narration you've been hearing is AI); ending implies the real use of AI is inside videos like this one, not standalone AI films. Subtitles end in music; final AI film linked in description.
- **Methods:**
  - Name the genre's known flaws (inconsistent style and characters, too-AI story, no views) and fix each. Crowd-source ideas from viewers.
  - Hand-draw a storyboard first. Image first, then animate (text-to-video only for B-roll).
  - Prompts: place characters and actions, add looks, time, season, palette, an emotion and a camera angle; commit to one clear style to dodge the in-between "AI look"; use a style reference image.
  - Character consistency: one 4-angle character sheet used as reference in every prompt.
  - Animation: very simple motion prompts, gentle camera moves, move only the parts that should move. Runway for realistic/3D, Pika for cartoon.
  - 9-step edit: VO in order, cut pauses / music / clips to VO + music / zooms and transitions / smoke-dust-light overlays / SFX on key points / colour grade / letterbox / export.

## 2. I04 shorts recipe in depth

1. **Niche (day 0, 0:57):** 3 hours scrolling Shorts, TikTok, Reels; ignore slop and memes; pick what keeps showing up: business stories (a company does something that sounds dumb until explained). Test: it makes the viewer feel smart. Save reference channels.
2. **Brand (1:30):** feel like a real person (person-style name), not a media company. Simple low-poly art style, font, palette, ChatGPT-tweaked mascot, Canva banner. Open YouTube, TikTok and Instagram at once.
3. **Core rule (5:03):** "Make people watch and keep them watching." Algorithm and trends change; this doesn't.
4. **Idea selection:** first pick (trending, proven on long-form, emotional, relatable: job-market doom) flopped; Shorts viewers want a quick dopamine hit, not doom. Winners: weird rule (Brazil prisoners cut sentences by reading), surprising fact (Yamaha makes everything), sneaky tactic (how Nescafé made Japan drink coffee). Formula: cool topic + surprising reveal + sneaky tactic.
5. **Script v1 failure list (7:54):** idea too negative; hook = shocking statistic with no stakes or reason to care; no payoff (ended on a joke, no solution).
6. **Script v2 (9:23):** feed a viral transcript from the format benchmark channel (see shorts-format) to ChatGPT, ask for a new script on your topic in the same structure; better hook + real ending + closing viewer question (should your country do this?). Result: flop -> 21K.
7. **Script v3, final (14:33), from a new fast-growing channel:** (a) second person: the viewer IS the protagonist and the problem is stated in line one (you are a giant company with a problem), so they are inside the story; (b) unfold the solution step by step; (c) ending reveals the real company behind it. Add own jokes, tighten with ChatGPT.
8. **Length:** not stated (Shorts, so under 60 s; his clips sound ~30-45 s).
9. **Voice:** ElevenLabs, then cut every silence in Premiere.
10. **Visuals v1-v2:** Canva animations + movie clips, keyframed. **v3 (12:16):** side-by-side with the format benchmark as a doom-scroller: script matched, visuals lost. Switched to AI video in one low-poly style: AI shot list failed, so hand-sketched storyboard -> scene descriptions -> images -> animate each. Took a full day.
11. **Edit:** fast auto-captions, trending music, SFX, a few polish effects; extra hour on real footage and animation for short 3.
12. **Posting:** ~1 short per day, all three platforms, wait 24 h, then check swipe rate and retention. Don't sit on a flop hoping it warms up (he lost a day).
13. **Benchmarks:** his old viral shorts had above 80% "viewed vs swiped away" and ~90% retention.
14. **Results:** short 1 (jobs) flop, weak metrics / short 2 (Brazil) 21K in 24 h, 80% viewed, 96% retention, first comments and subs / short 3 (Yamaha) one push then stalled; Brazil also stalled / day 6 still ~970K short of the goal / short 4 (Nescafé, AI low-poly) published day 7, result not shown.
15. **"Secret sauce" (claimed three times):** first the optimised script, then the visuals, finally: his old self cared and made shorts for fun, not to win a challenge or prove himself.

## 3. Cross-video patterns

**Long-video formula**
- **Cold open (0:00-1:00):** credentials or a strong opinion in the first breath (big view stats, or a rant/rejection), then a short skit with the mentor voice that ends in a deal or a mission with a number and deadline.
- **Title promise:** first-person experiment with a number (7 days, 365 days, 1 video, 8 videos); delivers the attempt, not a guaranteed win.
- **Structure:** day counter (I04) or step list (I05, I08), each step = one teachable method; inside it a loop of try -> result -> diagnose (problem 1/2/3) -> fix -> try again.
- **Pacing:** new beat every 30-60 s; skit or mentor interruption every 2-4 min; warning before technical parts; repeated steps as fast montage.
- **Mentor/inner voice:** appears at the call, at the midpoint (suggests the missing tool) and at the crisis (pep talk or hard truth). Can turn out to be an antagonist (I04).
- **Deeper problem:** named at the crisis, not at the start: determination (I05), finishing what you start (I08), caring vs chasing numbers (I04).
- **Twist:** final 30-60 s undercut: demonetised after success, results withheld and the premise questioned, the challenger wanted you to fail. Endings are abrupt and open, no standard outro.
- **Sponsor:** placed early (2-3 min), after setup and before the main work, inside the story world: a parody dream skit (I04) or a tool used for real in the project (I08), with a snap back to the plot. Discount code + scarcity.
- **Lead magnets:** free printable chart, PDF guide on his own store.

**Idea and packaging rules**
- The algorithm wants click + watch; win retention first.
- Niche = visible proven demand; outlier videos (I09) and fast-growing new channels are the idea and structure source.
- Ideas: curiosity, make the viewer feel smart; no doom for shorts.
- Hook = stakes + reason to care; always pay off at the end.
- Brand consistency (fonts, colours, art style, human name) from day one.
- Keep a "viral recipe list" from your own winners; re-analyse when it decays.
- Once script structure is matched, visuals are the gap.

**Where he argues against guru advice**
- Post 25-50 videos to find your niche = bad advice if you have skills; the first video can blow up (I06). Note: his own I05 says the opposite for beginners (publish fast, improve one thing at a time).
- Beginning-middle-end storytelling advice is useless; use a concrete 12-step map (I06).
- Mass AI automation (hundreds of shorts in minutes) is soulless and gets no views (I08).
- AI can't plan your shots; storyboard with your own brain (I04, I08).
- Feeding a viral transcript to ChatGPT works but isn't the secret; copying everyone else is the trap (I04).
- Waiting for a flop to warm up is a waste (I04).
- Probably the idea that it is too late to start (I09 chapter title; unverified).

## 4. Copy vs do not copy

**Do NOT copy (his identity)**
- Names: the creator's name and handle, the "Heisenberg" channel name, his store site and lead-magnet branding.
- The ginger bearded hero character and the mentor/friend character (Steven) with his lines and running dynamic.
- His ElevenLabs narrator voice and his AI-voice reveal line.
- His stock phrases: intro and description sign-offs, self-mocking joke lines, note-from-the-future inserts.
- His specific parody bits (Breaking Bad sponsor dream, Kung Fu Panda empty scroll, pterodactyl gag).
- His borrowed low-poly mascot look.

**Craft we MAY copy**
- Cold open with credentials + a deal with a number and deadline; a mentor voice (our own) at call, midpoint and crisis.
- Hero's journey map inside a tutorial; deeper problem revealed at the crisis; undercut, open ending.
- Try -> diagnose -> fix loop with numbered problems.
- Shorts script: second-person problem opener, step-by-step unfold, reveal ending.
- Viral recipe list; storyboard before generating; one style; multi-angle character sheet.
- Sponsor as an in-world skit or a real tool used in the project, early on; free lead magnet.
