# Curiosity-gap method: ask, climb, slow down, land on "you"

Learned from our study of 15 Nov 2026: the studied maths channel's top 10 Shorts (8.4M-22.7M), 3 weak Shorts in the same house style (98K-103K), all 257 Shorts titles in upload order and its 7 long videos. Evidence: `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/reference/creators/curiosity-gap/SECRET_SAUCE.md`. Numbers: `STUDY.md`, `techniques.md`, story chart `story_blocks.png` in the same folder.

**Copy the craft, never the identity:** no animation, look (black screen, neon lines), music, watermark or on-screen wording of the studied channel. No real footage, screenshots or thumbnails. Faceless toy figures in our underwater lab only; the creator stays anonymous until the last line (`reference/lessons.md`).

**Claude builds, the owner clicks.** This skill is free: titles, scripts, beat sheets and Blender plans. Voice and image generation follow the money rules in `reference/lessons.md`.

**The format benchmark keeps the final word on format.** The studied hits are silent (voice in 1 of 10), start the music quietly and never loop. None of that applies. The shorts-format rules win: 27-35 s, 75-95 words, about 2.8 words/s, voice on frame one, no pause over 0.5 s, voice 14-18 LU above the music, flights, loop to frame one. The standing panel stays shorts-format-critic + creator-breakdown-critic; call **curiosity-gap-critic** only when the owner asks or on an episode about the channel this method was studied from.

## Why it works (the viewer's head)
The studied channel's look, music, pace and silence are the same on hits and flops. The hits differ in six moves; **9 of 10 hits use 3 or more, 0 of 3 weak Shorts do.** Each move leaves something open in the viewer's head until the last second:

| Move | What the viewer feels | Hits | Weak |
|---|---|---|---|
| Question on screen from frame one, answered at the end | curiosity gap: leaving = leaving it unanswered | 7/10 | 0/3 |
| Two things the eye follows | someone to root for | 6/10 | 0/3 |
| The last step slows down | suspense: the answer is close, so we lean in | 8/10 | 0/3 |
| One finished picture or number, held | completion: relief when it closes | 9/10 | 1/3 |
| The payoff is about your life | "that's me": we attend most to ourselves | 7/10 | 0/3 |
| The result breaks the obvious guess | surprise | 5/10 | 0/3 |

## The recipe
1. **Ask about something the viewer thinks they already know.** π from school, the middle of your life, a chocolate bar. The question sits on screen from frame one and our spoken hook asks it too. Titles starting "What" have a median of 1.19M against the channel's 518K; a question mark alone does nothing (median 0.52M).
2. **Never give the answer early.** Not in the title, the cover, an early caption or a picture. The same idea with the answer in the title got 518K, then as a question 17.3M (33 times), and the answer version came first. Life words in the title flop (median 0.40M): the meaning is the payoff, not the packaging.
3. **Two characters, not a crowd.** The anonymous hero and one rival or partner the eye can follow to the end. The studied flops had 100-10,000 balls and 14 pendulums: nobody to root for.
4. **Climb.** A ladder or counter on screen (8 of 10 hits). It holds attention but is in 2 of 3 flops too, so it is the floor, not the separator. We already do it.
5. **Slow down on the moment of truth** (8 of 10 hits, 0 of 3 weak). The 2-3 s before the reveal: the camera slows into a push-in and the two characters close in. **The voice keeps going** (shorts-format rule: no pause over 0.5 s); only the picture, and at most one music layer, ease off. This is suspense without a setback (the studied hits have a setback in only 2 of 10).
6. **Land on one finished picture or number and hold it** (9 of 10 hits vs 1 of 3). Never a mess, a crowd or a fade: the same set-up ending in a heart got 8.5M, ending in chaos 98K. For us the settled picture echoes frame one so the loop still works.
7. **Make the payoff about "you"** (7 of 10 vs 0 of 3), said in the last line after the reveal: "So close, yet so far", "exactly 18". Our bookend with a twist.
8. **Ending caption re-reads the opening question** in 1-3 words (3 of 10 hits). The board that asked now answers.

## How to apply it in sf-pipeline
**Stage 1 (title):** the series pattern "How Did X Get Y? 🤯" already asks. X names the thing the viewer knows (3.14, burnt toast), never the answer or the secret. No life words, "satisfying"-type looks words or hashtags in the title.

**Stage 3 (script):** after the format spine (**shorts-format**) and **sf-story-craft** (villain, bookend):
- Line one asks the question about something the viewer has lived; the same question goes on the board.
- Pick the two characters before writing the middle; every ladder step moves one of them.
- Mark the TENSION stretch (2-3 s, the line before the reveal) in the script, so stage 5 slows the picture there.
- The last line reveals the creator and says what it means for "you"; check no earlier line has already said it.
- Run **curiosity-gap-critic** only when the owner asks (default panel: shorts-format-critic + creator-breakdown-critic).

**Stage 5 (3D, skill sf-blocky-world):**
- Frame one: the question on the board in the lab, the hero (mystery figure) already moving by 0.4 s.
- Two characters in every ladder shot, the hero and one rival, never a crowd as the subject.
- TENSION rows: slower push-in (still moving; longest still 0.3 s or less), the two characters closing the gap.
- Last shot: one settled picture held to the end, echoing frame one; the board's caption changes to the 1-3-word answer.

**Stage 7 (cover, sf-thumbnail):** the question, never the answer; the two characters about to meet; the creator unnamed.
