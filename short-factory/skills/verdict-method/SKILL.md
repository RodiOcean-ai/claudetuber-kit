# Verdict method: show the crime, show the judges, always give a verdict

Learned from our study of 15 Nov 2026: the studied food-reaction channel's top 10 Shorts (251M-717M), 2 weak Shorts from the same channel (598K, 681K), the titles and views of all 2,093 Shorts (30.0 billion views) and the top 10 long videos. Evidence: `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/reference/creators/verdict/SECRET_SAUCE.md`. Numbers: `STUDY.md`, `techniques.md`, story chart `story_blocks.png` in the same folder.

**Copy the craft, never the identity:** no face, name, voice, footage, the Italy national-team crest, their "Approved" catchphrase as ours, or the crucifix "sacred food" joke. We never react to other people's real clips. Faceless blocky figures only; the creator stays anonymous until the last line (`reference/lessons.md`).

**Claude builds, the owner clicks.** This skill is free: ideas, scripts, beat sheets, sound plans and Blender plans. Voice and image generation follow the money rules in `reference/lessons.md`.

**The format benchmark keeps the final word on format.** The studied channel's 14 s, 1.5 words a second, voices 2 LU over the background, hard cuts and no loop do not apply. The shorts-format rules win: 27-35 s, 75-95 words, about 2.8 words a second, voice on frame one, voice about 16 LU over the bed, flights, loop to frame one.

## The recipe
1. **The crime on frame one.** Something every stranger knows in a glance (spaghetti, a pizza, a microwave, a window, a phone) treated wrongly, already moving, readable by 1.5 s, no words needed. Hits 10 of 10 by 2.5 s, weak 0 of 2. Same trigger, wrong place: snapped spaghetti is the first beat of two hits (717M, 334M) and the last beat of a 598K flop. A vague teaser fails ("WHY? 😳": 5 of the top 40 titles vs 15 of the 45 under 1M); a question works only on a named thing ("CONCRETE PIZZA?" 327M).
2. **The judges in uniform.** The same two figures, always together, in a fixed colour pair (theirs: a blue national-team football shirt + a white T-shirt, 10 of 10 hits, weak 1 of 2). For us: a plain team-blue jersey and a white tee, colours only (no crest, number or badge). The tribe is shown, never said: "Italian" in their titles changes nothing (70 vs 70 of 209).
3. **Faces first.** Their reaction faces are on screen by 3 s in 8 of 10 hits (median 3.0 s); a gasp says "this is outrageous" in any language. For us: a horror or gasp expression from the blocky cast's 12 by 3 s, and the Short must pass the **no-voice test** (theirs read muted in 10 of 10 hits, weak 1 of 2).
4. **A court case, not a lecture: Problem, Agitation, Solution.** The crime is shown (problem), the judges' faces react (agitation), a worse crime or a second round climbs (ladder, 7 of 10), the verdict lands (solution). Mapped onto the format spine below.
5. **Always a verdict.** Every hit ends on a clear answer (10 of 10); the honey flop ended "half approved" and its "keep watching" curve was the only one that fell at the end. "Approved" in a title: 7 of the strongest 209 vs 0 of the weakest 209. For us: one big tick or cross stamp with its own sound (a ding or a buzzer), on the last beat before the reveal. Never a shrug, never "it depends".
6. **A villain to judge, an opponent in the story.** Their "@creator" titles: median 12.0M vs 4.6M, 0 of the 45 under 1M; "vs": 10.0M vs 5.1M. Our rules: the villain is a habit or a belief, never a real person, so a faceless blocky "cook" commits the crime; the "vs" lives inside the story (the crime vs the rule), the title keeps our series pattern.
7. **Repeat the judge, never the villain.** At least 15 of their Shorts name broken spaghetti in the title, median 14.0M (2.5 times their median): the same crime works again when the villain is new. For us: the judges and the stamp can come back in later episodes; the hero crime of each episode is new (check earlier episodes).
8. **Stay in the judge's chair.** Their songs, pranks on mum, break-up skits and tourist tips: 10 of the strongest 209 vs 35 of the weakest, median 2.6M vs 5.8M. For us: every episode stays inside the series role (the lab, the question title, the reveal).

## How to apply it in sf-pipeline
**Stage 1 (idea and title):** write the one-sentence idea first, touching something the viewer has lived (lessons: "One-sentence idea that resonates"). The crime is the hook object; the lesson is the judges and the verdict. Do not reuse "no words, so the whole world gets it" (that was an earlier episode) or "everyday food, one broken rule, never twice" (the everyday-object episode).

**Stage 3 (script):** after the format spine is drafted (**shorts-format**) and **sf-story-craft** has set the villain and the bookend:
- Line one: a "you" line about the crime while it happens ("You've snapped spaghetti to fit the pot...").
- The "But" at 5-9 s brings in the judges and what they protect.
- The ladder climbs like a court case: the crime, the judges' reaction, a worse crime; join steps with "so" and "but".
- The verdict sentence lands just before the reveal; the reveal ("two friends from Italy") is the last line and returns to frame one with a changed meaning.
- Panel: shorts-format-critic (lead) + creator-breakdown-critic (owner's rule); run **verdict-critic** only if the owner asks.

**Stage 5 (3D, skill sf-blocky-world, the underwater lab):**
- Frame one: a faceless cook figure commits the crime on the hero object, big in frame, moving at 0 s.
- The judges: two cast members restyled once (team-blue jersey, white tee), always in frame together when they appear; the reaction expression by 3 s.
- The verdict: a big tick or cross stamp hits a lab board or the hero object, with its own sound; plan it in the sound plan.
- Ginger's splat stays once per episode, away from the crime and the verdict; the water-bottle Easter egg stays out of the hook's focal point.

**Thumbnail (sf-thumbnail):** the crime mid-action in the centre, the two judges as faceless figures with grimace toy faces, 0-3 glowing words naming the crime, the creator unnamed.
