# YouTube titles: get the click, keep the promise

**For Claude:** talk in plain English, one step at a time, and end with one clear question. Never upload, never change a
title in YouTube Studio, never invent a number. The person picks the title.

## The one idea
A title is a **promise**. The thumbnail and the first 5 seconds of the video must make the **same** promise.

```
TITLE  ->  THUMBNAIL  ->  FIRST 5 SECONDS  ->  THE VIDEO KEEPS IT
"what you'll get"   "what it looks like"   "yes, this is it"     viewer is happy, YouTube shows it to more people
```
- If the video keeps the promise, people stay. If it doesn't, they leave, and YouTube's own tips say a title that doesn't
  match the video can hurt how often it gets shown.
- YouTube's spam policy bans "maliciously misleading" titles and thumbnails. Clickbait is not a trick; it is a risk.
- **Write the title before you make the video.** The first sentence of the script then says the title (or its point).

## Step 1. Write the promise as one plain line (not the title yet)
"**[Who]** gets **[what]**, and the surprise is **[why it's interesting]**."
- Example: "Beginners make a 30-second animated explainer with free tools in one evening; the surprise: no drawing at all."
- If you can't write this line, the idea isn't ready. Fix the idea, not the title.

## Step 2. Pick 2-3 patterns and write 5-10 options
Counts are from our study (see "Where this comes from"). They show what the winners share, not proof of what caused it.

| # | Pattern | Example | When it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **How to + wanted result + (tag)** | "How to Make Animated Shorts with Free AI (Full Guide)" | people search for it. 16 of 50 start "How to"; 20 of 50 say "Full" (Full Tutorial, Full Guide, Full Course) |
| 2 | **Result + the fear removed** | "How to Make AI Animation That Doesn't Look Generic" (a study top-10 title) | the viewer has tried and been let down |
| 3 | **Proof: "I did X in Y"** | "I Made 30 Animated Shorts in 30 Days" | you really did it and can show it |
| 4 | **Time squeeze** | "Give Me 20 Minutes, I'll Show You..." / "500 Hours of Learning X in 15 Minutes" | a big skill made short. 8 of 50 promise a time |
| 5 | **Number list** | "7 Free Tools for Animated Explainers" | a clear, countable payoff. 30 of 50 titles have a digit |
| 6 | **The only / the ultimate** | "The Only Claude Code Animation Guide You Need" | only if it is really that complete. 12 of 50 use a word like Only, Ultimate, Best, Complete, Insane |
| 7 | **Flip a belief** | "Stop Animating Every Frame" / "Why Most AI Videos Look Cheap" | you can prove the opposite of what people think |
| 8 | **Named tool + result** | "Make Explainer Videos with Claude Code (Step by Step)" | the tool is what people search. 19 of 50 name a tool; 6 of the top 10 do |
| 9 | **Question + one emoji (Shorts)** | "Why Do Cats Always Land on Their Feet? 🤔" | Shorts in a series (the kit's shorts-format pattern) |

**Tags in brackets** tell people what kind of video it is: "(Full Guide)", "(Step by Step)", "(100% Free)",
"(Beginner to Pro)". 24 of the 50 titles had one.

## Step 3. Max loading: no spare word
Every word must earn its place. Delete a word: if the meaning survives, it stays deleted.
- Before (78 characters): "In This Video I Will Show You How You Can Make Animated Videos Using AI Tools"
- After (52): "How to Make Animated Videos with Free AI (Full Guide)"
- **Three focal points at most** (for example: animated videos + free AI + full guide).
- **Rhythm:** read it once, out loud. If you stumble or have to read it twice, rewrite it.
- **Plain words.** Use the words your viewer types, not insider words ("keyframe interpolation" means nothing to a beginner).

## Step 4. Length, front-loading, digits, capitals, emoji
- **YouTube's limit is 100 characters,** but people often see only the start. YouTube's tips: keep it short, put the most
  important words first, put episode numbers and channel branding at the end.
- **Our study:** median 62 characters and 11 words; 40 of the 50 had 70 characters or fewer.
- **Our guide:** 40-65 characters, the hook words in the first 40.
- **Digits, not words:** "7", not "seven". "$0", "30 Days", "20 Minutes" are specific, and specific is believable.
- **Capitals:** one word in CAPS at most ("The ONLY Guide..."). YouTube's tips say to limit ALL CAPS and emoji.
- **Emoji:** rare on long videos (4 of 50). On Shorts, one at the end is fine if your series always does it.

## Step 5. Curiosity without clickbait
Curiosity = a gap the video closes. Clickbait = a gap the video never closes.

| Clickbait (don't) | Honest curiosity (do) |
|---|---|
| "You WON'T BELIEVE What AI Can Do Now" | "AI Made This Whole Video in 1 Hour (Here's How)" |
| "This Changes EVERYTHING" | "The 3-Second Trick That Keeps People Watching" |
| A result you didn't get, a person who isn't in it | the real number, the real tool, shown in the video |

- **The end-of-video test:** would a viewer who watched to the end say "yes, it did what the title said"?
- Pay the promise off early: a long video shows the payoff (or a first proof) within about 30 seconds; a Short keeps the
  answer for the last line but must show the subject from frame one.
- Hype words flop often. In the kit's own study of 185 videos about making videos with Claude, "changed forever"-style hype
  titles flopped 8 times and won 4 times.

## Step 6. Score your options
Write 5-10 options from at least 3 patterns. Score each out of 10:

| Test | Points |
|---|---|
| The promise is clear in one read | 2 |
| The right viewer wants it (their words, their problem) | 2 |
| **True: the video delivers it, early** (0 here = drop the title) | 2 |
| Curiosity or a flip | 1 |
| Max loaded (no word can go) | 1 |
| Key words first, about 65 characters or fewer | 1 |
| A digit or a specific detail | 1 |

Then:
- Keep the **best 2-3**, and make them **different promises**, not two wordings of the same one.
- Put each next to your thumbnail idea: the thumbnail words must **add** to the title, never repeat it (see **yt-thumbnails**).
- Read the top 10 titles for your main search on YouTube and ask: "Would I click mine over these?"

## Step 7. Test it on YouTube
**Long videos: A/B testing (YouTube Studio on a computer).**
1. Open the video in YouTube Studio (Content, then the video).
2. Under the title or the thumbnail, click **A/B Testing**.
3. Choose **Title only**, **Thumbnail only** or **Title and thumbnail**; add up to 3 versions; click **Done**.
- You need **advanced features** switched on for your channel.
- YouTube picks the winner by **watch time**, not by clicks. A test takes a few days up to 2 weeks.
- Results: **Winner**, **Performed Same** (you choose) or **Inconclusive** (your first version stays).
- Not possible for Shorts, made-for-kids, private or age-restricted videos. Changing the title during a test stops the test.

**Shorts: no A/B test.** Post, then judge at 24 hours on "viewed vs swiped away". Change the **next** Short's title, not this one.

**Patience:** don't swap titles in the first days; a new video can look quiet for about 5 days (packaging-method).

## Common mistakes
1. **A summary, not a promise:** "My Animation Process" says nothing the viewer wants.
2. **Insider words** the viewer doesn't use.
3. **Title and thumbnail say the same words.**
4. **The payoff comes too late** (or never).
5. **Copying a big channel's title word for word.** Copy the reason it works, then say it your way.
6. **The key words at the end** of a long title, where they get cut off.
7. **Fake urgency or hype:** "SHOCKING", "gone wrong", "changed forever".
8. **Changing the title every day.** Test once, properly, then leave it.
9. **A different title style every video.** A series with one pattern teaches viewers what to expect.

## Checklist
- [ ] The promise line is written and true.
- [ ] 5-10 options from at least 3 patterns.
- [ ] The winner reads once, about 65 characters or fewer, key words first.
- [ ] Digits, at most one word in capitals, no hype words.
- [ ] The video delivers it, early.
- [ ] The thumbnail words add to it and don't repeat it.
- [ ] The first sentence of the video says the title or its point within 5 seconds.
- [ ] Long video: A/B set ready. Short: the 24-hour check is planned.
- [ ] If the brand sheet (`assets/brand/BRAND.md`, skill **yt-branding**) has a series title pattern, the title follows it.
