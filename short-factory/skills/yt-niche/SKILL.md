# Your niche: what you know, what people want, what only you can show

**For Claude:** plain English, one question per message, choices first and "type your own" last. This skill is free:
no downloads of videos, no sign-ins, no spending. Never get around a YouTube sign-in or bot check.

## The one idea
A niche is not a topic. It is a **promise to one kind of viewer**: who it is for, and what they get.
"Animation" is a topic. "Nurses learn what really happens in the body during an emergency, in 60-second animations" is
a niche. A good niche passes three tests:
1. **You can make 50 videos** in it without running dry or getting bored.
2. **People already watch it** (proof on YouTube, not a hunch).
3. **You have an edge:** something you can show that most channels in it can't.

## Step 1. Two things you know, glued together
Write three short lists (5 lines each):
- **A. Your work and skills:** what you are paid for, or did for years.
- **B. Your passions:** what you read or watch for fun, what you'd talk about for an hour.
- **C. Your life:** parent, student, night-shift worker, lives abroad, recovering from something.

Glue one from A or C to one from B. Write 5-10 pairs. Examples:
| Glue | Niche idea |
|---|---|
| nurse + animation | "What happens in your body when..." 60-second animated explainers |
| accountant + video games | the money behind video games, explained with charts and toy characters |
| truck driver + history | the history of the roads, bridges and ports you drive past |
| teacher + Claude Code | maths ideas made visual, with the code to make them yourself |
| chef + chemistry | why cooking tricks work, shown as tiny animated molecules |

The glue is what makes it yours: thousands of channels do "history"; few are by someone who drives the roads.

## Step 2. Three things people always ask you about
- What do friends, family and colleagues ask you? What do you end up explaining at parties?
- Write **three questions, in their words.** Those questions already have an audience (the people who asked).
- Each one is a first video idea. Example: "Is it safe to...?", "How do you even...?", "Why does... happen?"

## Step 3. Your edge (one line)
What can you show that others can't? Pick at least one:
- **Access:** your job, your place, your tools, your data.
- **Skill:** you can animate, measure, build, explain simply.
- **Story:** you failed at it, or did it the hard way, and can tell what nobody says.
- **Format:** a style nobody in this niche uses yet (animated, 3D, whiteboard, a recurring character).
No edge? Try another pair from step 1.

## Step 4. The demand check (free, about 1-2 hours)
a. **Search lines.** Write 10 searches your viewer would really type, in their words. Type each into YouTube; the
   suggestions that drop down are free hints of what people search.
b. **Standouts.** For the top results, open the channel and compare the video's views with that channel's normal
   (typical) views. **A video at 3 times its channel's normal views or more is a standout:** proof that the idea pulled
   viewers, not just the channel's fame. Standouts on **small** channels are the best proof of all.
c. **Who else does it.** List 5-10 channels in the niche: size, how often they post, Shorts or long, the look, the
   voice. Then write what **none** of them do. That gap is your way in.
d. **YouTube Studio's Trends tab** (once you have a channel): Studio, Analytics, Trends. It shows top searches, breakout
   videos from similar creators and "content gaps" (searches with no good videos yet) over the last 28 days. Some parts
   only show in some countries, languages and devices.
e. **Shorts or long?** Check which form the standouts are. Example from the kit's own study of 565 videos about making
   videos with Claude: the Shorts had a median of 4,600 views, the long videos 26,000. The niche lived in long videos.

**Score each candidate niche** (yes / no):
| Test | Yes means |
|---|---|
| Demand | 5+ standouts in your 10 searches, some on small channels |
| Gap | something real that the channels in it don't do |
| Edge | your one line from step 3 is true |
| 50 videos | you can list 20 ideas today without stretching |
| You | you'd still enjoy video 30 |
Pick the niche with the most yeses. A tie? Pick the one you'd enjoy more.

## Step 5. Copy the reason, never the identity
| Copy (the reason it works) | Never copy (the identity) |
|---|---|
| the promise the title and thumbnail make | the channel's name, face, mascot or logo |
| the structure: hook, problem, turn, payoff | their footage, pictures or music |
| the pacing and length | their voice, or a clone of it |
| the kind of hook | their exact titles or a thumbnail look as a whole |
For every standout you like, write **one line: why did this work?** (the subject? the promise? the format? the first
seconds?). Then make that reason your own way. Re-uploading or lightly changing other people's videos is not paid by
YouTube ("reused content"), even with their permission.

## Step 6. Test it with 3-5 videos
1. Make **3-5 videos** inside your niche sentence, each a different angle (your 3 questions, or the best standouts'
   reasons). Same look and same voice on all of them (brand sheet, **yt-branding**).
2. Release them **1-3 days apart**, not all on one day. In the kit's study of 8 new channels in one niche, none put
   several videos up on the same day; the fastest risers posted every 1-3 days. (YouTube says nothing official about this.)
3. Judge each against your own others, not against big channels:
   - **Shorts** at 24 hours: "viewed vs swiped away" (how many stayed instead of swiping).
   - **Long videos** at 7 days: views, impressions (how often YouTube showed it), average view duration.
4. **Double down:** keep the reason the best one worked; make 3 more with that reason and a new subject.
   Never just remake the same video.

## Signs the niche is wrong (and what to do first)
- You can't list 20 video ideas. -> Widen the promise a little.
- Only big channels get views; no small-channel standouts. -> The demand is fame, not the topic: change the angle.
- Viewers watch but don't subscribe, or one off-topic video brings all the views. -> You drifted: back to the sentence.
- Anyone could make your video; the edge is gone. -> Bring your job, story or format back in.
- You dread the next video. -> Change the glue; you won't last 50 videos.
- After 10-15 videos, with titles and thumbnails fixed, every video stays below your own normal. -> Then change niche.
**Fix in this order:** packaging (title and thumbnail) first, then the format, then the angle, and only then the niche.
Three quiet videos is not a verdict.

## Write it down
Fill in one sentence:
> "I make **[format, e.g. 60-second animated explainers]** about **[topic]** for **[who]** so they **[get what]**,
> and only I **[edge]**."
Example: "I make 60-second animated explainers about the body in emergencies for nurses and students, so they understand
what they see on shift, and only I have 15 years on an emergency ward."
- Save it in the brand sheet `short-factory/assets/brand/BRAND.md` (`niche_sentence`, `audience`, `edge`, plus 3-5
  `first_ideas`; skill **yt-branding** makes the file). Every later skill reads it.
- Keep the research in your studies folder: `<studies_root>/niche_<name>/<date>/`.
- Any new idea that doesn't fit the sentence gets a "no", or a bridge back to it.
