# The story under a YouTube video

## Hero's journey, as the studied creator uses it
| Beat | What it is in a YouTube video | His editing tutorial (I02, 776k) | His AI-voice tutorial (I03, 722k) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Status quo | where the narrator stands; nothing's wrong, but something's off | successful channel, no editing tutorial | can't sound like a YouTuber on the mic |
| Call to adventure | a problem, chance, threat or curiosity | the flood of "where's the editing tutorial?" comments | the mentor's joke advice: if you don't sound like a YouTuber, use something that does |
| Mentor | the wise friend who pushes | inner-voice friend: "just be you, show past you the way" | the same friend |
| Departure | the real content starts | the tutorial begins | the 4 elements of a human voice |
| Trials | every point being taught, wins and losses | script → assets → edit → text → sound | voice choice → model → settings → batches → comping → EQ |
| Approach | the deeper problem creeps in | perfectionism | "it feels like cheating, I'm a fraud" |
| Crisis | lowest point; admit weakness; identity change | 60 days on the same video | the fraud talk: silence is the real fraud |
| Reward | the lesson | "say it's good enough" | "it's your thoughts that matter, not your throat" |
| Result | the act | presses publish | keeps the AI voice |
| Return / new life | the change shows | positive comments; a new mindset | the twist: AI voice's real power is dubbing |
| Resolution | loose ends close | the motivational speech gets cut off (joke) | the dubbing results; "the friend was right" |

Rules:
- Without conflict, stakes and change, it's "just a tutorial with tips". With them, the viewer sees themselves in the narrator, and that's what builds a loyal audience.
- The crisis needs real vulnerability. Show that the narrator doesn't know everything.
- The twist is a **bonus** that reframes the topic (voice → dubbing). Keep the best part for last and say so ("I kept the best part for the end") so people stay.
- His growth stories (I04, I05, I06, I08) are challenges: a deal or a bet (1M views in 7 days), a ticking clock, numbers shown at each stage, a low point, a result.

## Cold-open patterns (first 30-60 s)
1. **Comment montage:** many voices asking for the video → "Yep, this is the X tutorial. But why did it take 9 months?" (I02).
2. **Broken recording:** the narrator fails at the very thing the video fixes, then the mentor's joke gives the call (I03).
3. **Fake-guru hook, interrupted:** a cliché "secret 5-step framework" opening, the mentor/inner voice objects, "let's try that again, no secret formulas" (I07). It mocks the genre and promises honesty in one move.
4. **Proof first:** "I made only three videos and all got 100k+ views. It comes down to these four steps." (I01).
5. **The deal:** a challenge with a number and a deadline (I04).
The title line ("So, this is how I actually...") lands right after the skit, under the intro music sting.

## Sponsors inside the story
He writes the sponsor as a scene that fits the lesson (in the scripts video: a messy store link as a "packaging" mistake → the sponsor's clean domain), then returns with a joke. Never a cold "this video is sponsored by". Our version: only with a real sponsor, and labelled.

## Running devices
- A recurring mentor voice and in-jokes across videos.
- Self-aware jokes about the format ("what kind of psycho watches a tutorial for the story?").
- Movie and TV references as illustrations (he names Breaking Bad, Marvel, South Park).
- Fake precise numbers as a gag. Ours must be our own gag, not his.
