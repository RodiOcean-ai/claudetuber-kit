# What the owner's own read of a voice script taught us (the survival-story long video, 7 Nov 2026)

The owner read the whole marked-up voice script (voice_script_v20, 52 batches, 7.5 min) in Transcript Recorder. Measured with Whisper word timings plus loudness and pitch per word (scratch tool owner_read.py; report in the episode's voice/read_v20/owner_read_report.json).

## Numbers
- Pace inside sentences: **2.3 words a second** (batches 1.2-3.2). A careful read; the creator-breakdown study's whole-video pace is about 3.1.
- He **slows down by himself on the dramatic lines**: "Then in 2024…" 1.5, the Peanut lines 2.1, "people fall in love with characters, not spectacle" 1.3, the answer ("So, how do twelve videos…", "the easy bit") 1.2-1.5.
- He **speeds up on lists and set-ups**: the idea checklist, the titles, the stranger rule (about 3.0).
- One line went the wrong way: "He doesn't make it." came out fast (5.1 words a second). The written [sighs] alone didn't slow it.

## His natural pauses that work (keep them in the AI version)
- before the turn in a contrast: "people fall in love with characters… not spectacle" (1.9 s)
- after "The subscribers are the easy bit." (1.0 s)
- before the name: "one chick… called Peanut" (1.0 s)
- after the false lead: "…between the jungle and the desert." (1.2 s), then the correction
- "A few videos after the dip… came the ants" (1.2 s)
- after "outside," and "graveyard." in the ant fact (0.8 s each)

## What didn't work
- **Written-in fillers trip a human reader.** Of 8 scripted slips he skipped or swapped 5: "you know" and "hmm" skipped, both "um"s turned into "and", "I, uh, I didn't want to stop" broke the line. Real hesitations happen where the thought turns, not where they're typed. For an AI voice keep 2-3 at most, and only where he paused anyway.
- **Hard to say aloud:** "a sixth" (came out "six"), the three-verb list "watched… measured… listened" (two long stumbles), "a thousand days" ("thousands day"), "rival colony". Prefer "one sixth", one verb, simple nouns.
- **His accent and grammar** (dropped -s endings, "you will" for "you'll", articles) are his natural voice, not script problems. They don't go into an ElevenLabs script. If he ever narrates himself, keep sentences short.

## His natural words worth keeping
- "everybody" (not "everyone"), "OK" (not "Okay, so"), "I sorted out", "around" (not "about").

## Stress he put on content words
shoved · didn't · textbook · a third · scorpion · strangest · easy (and hard) · prove · danger · twelve · eighty-three. Use these for CAPS before guessing.
