# Survival-story method: the creator's growth told as "will it survive?"

Learned from our study of 6 Nov 2026: all 12 long videos of the studied nature channel (17M-123M each, 852M in all) plus a 42-minute public interview with its creator. **The studied channel has no Shorts**, so this skill carries premise, story, sound design and covers, never Shorts format. Evidence: `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/reference/creators/survival-story/SECRET_SAUCE.md`. Numbers: `STUDY.md`, `techniques.md`, `hook_scores.md`, his own words `INTERVIEW_NOTES.md`, story chart `story_blocks.png`, all in the same folder.

**Copy the craft, never the identity:** no face, name or voice of the studied creator, no real footage of him or his animals, none of his catchphrases ("Within this tank lives a miniature world", "we must go back to day one", "Let me introduce you to…"), no mother-in-law gag (our running gag is Ginger), no licensed famous songs. Faceless toy figures and silhouettes only; the creator stays anonymous until the last line (`reference/lessons.md`).

**Claude builds, the owner clicks.** This skill is free: premises, scripts, beat sheets, sound plans and Blender plans only. Any voice, music or image generation follows the money rules in `reference/lessons.md`.

**The format benchmark keeps the final word on format.** The studied creator makes 8-14 minute videos at 1.9 words a second with 5 holds a minute and music only 7 dB under his voice. None of that goes into a Short. The shorts-format rules win: 27-35 s, 75-95 words, about 2.8 words a second, voice on frame one, no pause over 0.5 s, voice about 16 LU above the music, flights, loop to frame one.

## The recipe
1. **The premise is a survival question with a clock** (9 of 9 strong videos vs 0 of 3 weak). One hero who might not make it, a fixed time, and what is lost if they fail. His weak videos were a diary, a list and a stunt. For our series: "will this channel survive?", never "here are 3 tips".
2. **Line 1 points at the hero and names the clock** (his best hooks: ant 8.2, hamster 7.7, egg 7.2 on the hook critic; wide-world openings 4.9-6.0). "This is a queen ant and she has the next thousand days to create an empire." Our version keeps the subject anonymous: "This is one kid with one phone, and he has one video to save his dream." Where our you-line rule fits better, the you-line comes first and the clock follows in line 2.
3. **The catch by 5-9 s makes it life or death** (8 of 9 strong): "But a rival colony is watching their every move." / "The only catch? She's lived her entire life in a pet store." That is our "But" twist on the format benchmark's timing.
4. **The middle is a chain, not a list:** a problem grows → **so** the hero brings in a fix → **but** the fix causes the next problem (his "I needed a predator… Meet the…", 12 times in 4 videos). Three links in 30 s, joined with "so" and "but".
5. **One real setback about two-thirds in** (9 of 9 strong vs 1 of 3 weak): a loss the viewer feels, shown on screen, then the turn. In 30 s that's about second 18-22.
6. **Facts only as plot turns** ("ants are one of the few animals that respect their dead, so the remaining few build a graveyard"). If a number doesn't change what happens next, cut it. At most 4 numbers (our rule).
7. **The ending returns to the opening with a changed meaning** (7 of 9 strong vs 0 of 3 weak): "so much we're told about hamsters is wrong; there is one thing we got right". Our last line reveals who it is and lands back on frame one, now meaning something else (sf-story-craft's bookend with a twist).
8. **Sound is the second narrator** (his own test: "remove all voice over and see if it's watchable with just sound"). For us, inside the shorts-format levels: every action in the 3D world gets its own real-sounding sound (a crack, a thud, a rip, a heartbeat), the music bed carries the mood change at the setback, and the Short must pass the no-voice test before the final render.
9. **Cover: before → after split with one arrow** (his DAY 1 → DAY 100 covers: median 85M; day-count titles median 82M): one hero, 0-2 words, the subject anonymous.

## How to apply it in sf-pipeline
**Stage 3 (script):** after the format spine is drafted (**shorts-format**) and **sf-story-craft** has set the villain and the bookend:
- Write the one-sentence idea as a survival question: "Can one kid with a phone save his dream with one video?"
- Line 1 = the hero + the clock (anonymous). Line 2 or the "But" at 5-9 s = what is lost if it fails.
- Three chain links, each one sentence, joined by "so" and "but". Check: does each link cause the next? A list of steps fails.
- One setback sentence at about 18-22 s, something that really went wrong, then the turn.
- Every fact checked: does it push the story? If not, cut it.
- Last line = the reveal of who it is, worded to return to frame one with a changed meaning.
- Run **survival-story-critic** in the expert panel only when the owner asks for it (the default panel is shorts-format-critic + creator-breakdown-critic, owner's rule of 5 Nov 2026); it advises, shorts-format-critic leads.

**Stage 5 (3D, skill sf-blender-world or sf-blocky-world):**
- Frame one: the anonymous hero alone, small against a big world, with one object and a **clock on screen** (a day counter or a timer that moves).
- The chain = stations along the world's line; each station shows the problem the last fix caused.
- The setback station: something visibly breaks, empties or goes dark (owner's rule: pictures carry the words).
- **Sound plan for the edit:** list every on-screen action (a door, a fall, a crack, a phone buzz) and give each one real-sounding sound; mark the setback beat for the music change.

**Stage 6 (edit, skill sf-edit):** keep the shorts-format levels and our rotation bed. Then the **no-voice test**: render a muted-voice preview (music + effects only), watch it once and write down any beat that doesn't read. Fix the picture or add a sound, never more words.

**Stage 7 (cover, skill sf-thumbnail):** a before → after split (small and alone on the left, big and thriving on the right) with one white arrow, one hero, 0-3 glowing words, the creator anonymous.

## If the episode is about the studied creator himself
The interview gives the survival story: a high-schooler hiding an animal channel, "one more video" or college, 3 months, $300, out of phone storage, finished on his mom's phone, and that video now at about 110 million views. The hook contrast: **12 videos, 6 million subscribers.** Every number goes through **fact-checker** with two sources (his own words are one source at most); which video was the "one more video" is our inference (most likely the Desert one) until confirmed. He was 17 then: faceless figures only, no likeness.
