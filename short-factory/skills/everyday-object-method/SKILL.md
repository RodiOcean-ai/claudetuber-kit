# Everyday-object method: an everyday thing, one broken rule, a joke that flips it

Learned from our study of 5 Nov 2026: the studied channel's top 10 Shorts (182M-669M), 4 weaker same-format Shorts (10M-30M), all 489 Shorts titles and its top 10 long videos. Evidence: `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/reference/creators/everyday-object/SECRET_SAUCE.md`. Numbers: `STUDY.md`, `techniques.md`, story chart `story_blocks.png` in the same folder.

**Copy the craft, never the identity:** no face, name, voice, kitchen look or catchphrases of the studied creator or his partners. No real footage, screenshots or thumbnails. Faceless toy figures only; the creator stays anonymous until the last line (`reference/lessons.md`).

**Claude builds, the owner clicks.** This skill is free: ideas, scripts, beat sheets and Blender plans. Voice and image generation follow the money rules in `reference/lessons.md`.

**The format benchmark keeps the final word on format.** The studied channel's 27 s, 3.5 words/s, a cut every 1.2 s and narrated Shorts with no music do not apply. The shorts-format rule wins: 27-35 s, 75-95 words, about 2.8 words/s, voice on frame one, voice about 16 LU above the music, flights, loop to frame one.

## The recipe
1. **One everyday object, one broken rule, on frame one.** A thing every stranger knows in a glance (toast, a phone, a play button, a coffee cup) doing something impossible: gigantic, tiny, "gourmet", alive, cooked by tiny chefs. Twist openings scored a median 7.9 with the hook critic against 6.4 for giant-object and crowd openings. Everyday foods in the studied titles: median 28M (151); luxury words: 22.5M (44).
2. **A line said in the moment, not an announcement.** "Hey Chef, can you turn my ice gourmet?" (8.8) beats "Let's make the world's largest…" (6.4 median). For us: a "you" line while the object is already moving.
3. **Climb step by step.** Every one of the top 10 has a ladder ("now we… now we…"). Each step is a new station in our world and a bigger version of the rule-break.
4. **Payoff at the top, then a joke sting that flips the scale or status** (7 of 10 hits, 1 of 4 weaker): "Why are yours so small?" "It's average." For us it's the bookend with a twist: one short line after the reveal that turns the meaning, and it leads back to frame one.
5. **Borrowed fame inside the idea** (8 of 10 hits had a famous partner who *is* the idea). We can't show partners: the creator we break down is our partner, revealed in the last line.
6. **Never twice.** Every weaker Short we checked repeats one of the channel's own hits and lost 7-18 times the views (sushi 290M → donut 10M; the same cake-pop video re-posted 182M → 10M). Each of our episodes needs a new hero object and a new rule-break; only the series packaging repeats.
7. **Cover words name the object and the rule-break** in 2 words: "GOURMET ICE", "SMALLEST PASTA" → for us 0-3 glowing words, the creator unnamed.

## How to apply it in sf-pipeline
**Stage 3 (script):** after the format spine is drafted (**shorts-format**) and **sf-story-craft** has set the villain and the bookend:
- Frame one + line one = the everyday object with its rule broken, and a "you" line about it.
- Steps = a ladder, each one bigger; join them with the creator-breakdown "But… so…" chain when the episode uses **creator-breakdown-method**.
- Last step gets a 2-3-word name.
- Reveal in the last line, then the sting (under 2 s) that flips the scale and leads back to frame one.
- Run **everyday-object-critic** in the expert panel (it advises; shorts-format-critic leads).

**Stage 5 (3D, skill sf-blender-world):**
- Frame one: the hero object alone, big in the frame, already doing the impossible thing.
- Each ladder step = a station where the object is bigger, smaller or stranger.
- The sting: a small physical gag on the hero object (it burns, it shrinks, it pops), the same object as frame one so the loop closes.
- The series Easter egg (the bottle) stays out of the hero object's focal point.

**Thumbnail (sf-thumbnail):** the hero object centred, its rule-break visible at phone size, 0-3 glowing words naming object + break, the creator unnamed.
