# Packaging method: concept and angle first, design last

From one public podcast interview, "This YouTube Genius Proves Anyone Can Crack The Algorithm With Your First Upload" (2h14m, 179k views), with a YouTube strategist who works with large channels ("I stopped counting around 2.5 billion" views). Full notes with timestamps: `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/reference/methods/packaging-interview-notes.md`.

**These are the strategist's claims and examples from one interview, not our measurements.** Where our own studies disagree, say so (see "Where the strategist clashes with our rules"). **Claude builds, the owner clicks:** this skill is free text work; any paid image generation follows the money rules in `reference/lessons.md`.

## The core idea
Packaging (title, thumbnail, intro) decides whether a video gets watched: "I think it's actually more important than the video itself" (6:03). A 5/10 video with 10/10 packaging beats the reverse. Packaging has three layers: **concept** and **angle** (90 %) and **design** (the 10 % frosting, 1:04:33). The intro counts as packaging because of hover-play (7:13).

## Titles (the four tools, 46:47, plus the strategist's rules)
1. **Irony or a flip:** claim the opposite of what people expect, or take a strong side ("Why Intellectuals Are F***ing Idiots").
2. **Max loading:** the fewest words that still say everything; "there's no fat" (47:33). Beyond front-loading.
3. **Rhythm:** read it once in your head; if anyone has to read it twice, it fails (48:55).
4. **Three focal points, no more** ("I let food companies control my diet for 7 days", 49:04).
5. **Digits, not words** ("7", 49:48). **Plain words** an outsider knows ("autodidact" → "work like a genius", 1:34:36).
6. **Never a summary of the video.** "Only 2% of violinists understand this" beat "top 10 exercises for violin players" by about 74 % more watch time (1:46:44).
7. **Outsider pull:** write for people who don't care about the niche (1:48:53). For 1M → 10M views per video: **niche integration**, mix the niche with something everyone feels (money, fear, family) (1:37:17).
8. **Lock the title before production**, because the first sentence of the video is the title (2:01:24).

## Thumbnails
1. **Cover-the-text test:** cover the words and ask what's left; "80% of what's working in the thumbnail is the text" (14:16).
2. **Brief text** that adds what the title leaves out; **never repeat the title's words** (5:21, 2:00:44).
3. **One first stop for the eye** (for faces: the whites of the eyes, not an open mouth, 51:11), then **one accent colour or an arrow** ("the only red thing", 1:59:57; arrows win A/B tests, 4:32), a **left-to-right path** with a diagonal.
4. **Mid-action, with conflict:** "we try to give the still image motion" (2:00:20).
5. **Strip to first principles:** remove elements until it stops working, then put the last one back (13:47). A plain or black background to stand out (4:12).
6. **The thumbnail's scene is paid off at once** in the opening (1:43:24).
7. **On-brand angle:** play against expectations without leaving the channel's world (the knitted armour hit by an arrow, "It actually works", 1:06:33).

## The opening (first 5 s → 30 s → 3 min)
1. **The first sentence is the title** (or the whole point) within 5 s, and the first shot shows the thumbnail's scene (2:01:11, 34:05).
2. **By about 30 s, over-deliver one thing the packaging didn't advertise** (34:36).
3. Rush the setup, reach the conflict; it doesn't have to be fast-cut, it has to serve the audience (32:06).
4. Milestones: 5 s, 30 s, 1 min, 3 min ("once you get them to a 3-minute mark… you won", 35:26).

## Metrics and cadence
- Watch **APV of the first 30 s** (his intro score; best he's seen 91 %), **AVD** and **impressions**. **CTR is "always overvalued"** (1:44:47); a CTR that starts high and drops as impressions grow is good; about 2 % at 48 h probably means the packaging is wrong.
- Don't swap thumbnails in the first days; the first ~5 days can look dormant (1:33:46). A/B test even on a new channel.
- Cadence: weekly (at least every two weeks) while growing from 100K toward 1M per video; rarely more than weekly; big videos need less frequent uploads (one large family channel he cites: about 100M → 200M monthly views while uploading less, among other changes).

## Where the strategist clashes with our rules (say it, don't hide it)
- **Our question titles** ("How Did 12 Videos Get 6 Million Subscribers? 🤯") read as a summary question. He never discusses question titles or emoji; he warns against summaries and favours bold claims. **Test a claim version** against ours; don't drop the series pattern without the owner.
- **No faces** (our rule): his main eye-guide is the whites of the eyes. With faceless figures we need another clear first stop: one red element, an arrow, a glowing object.
- **Shorts:** most of his advice is for long videos. For Shorts the feed autoplays: the thumbnail matters less, the first line and frame one matter more. The format benchmark (shorts-format) keeps the final word on Shorts format.

## How to apply it in sf-pipeline
- **Stage 1 (title):** write 5-8 titles; score each on the checklist below; keep the 2 best (one in our series pattern, one claim version) and show the owner.
- **Stage 3 (script):** the first sentence states the title or the point; plan the over-delivery beat by 30 s.
- **Stage 7 (thumbnail, skill sf-thumbnail):** 5-8 cover concepts as text first; cover-the-text test; text that adds, not repeats; one accent colour or an arrow; price only the best 1-2.
- **After posting (sf-posting):** read APV at 30 s and impressions, not CTR alone; no thumbnail swap in the first ~5 days unless it's a real mistake.

## The packaging checklist (score title + thumbnail + first line, 1 point each)
1. One-glance idea: clear and appealing before design. 2. Irony or flip. 3. Max loaded (no word can go). 4. Rhythm (one read). 5. Three focal points at most. 6. Not a summary. 7. Plain words, digits. 8. Outsider pull. 9. Cover-the-text test passes; text brief. 10. Thumbnail text adds, doesn't repeat. 11. One first stop + one accent colour/arrow + left-to-right path. 12. Mid-action, conflict. 13. Thumbnail scene shown at once. 14. First line = title/point within 5 s. 15. Over-delivery by about 30 s.
