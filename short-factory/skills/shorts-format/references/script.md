# The script

**Read `top-100-scripts.md` first.** It measures the scripts of his top 100 (12 Nov 2026) and refines the top-10 rules below: pick one of the seven story types before writing; the first "But" lands at a median 9 s (6-13 s) and only flips, stories and ladders need it (chains and guides run on As/Once/Then/This); 85 % of his sentences after the first open with a glue word or point back; a cold open mid-action is a full second opener (29 of 100). Where the two files disagree, the top-100 file wins.

## The spine (8 of his top 10 follow it)
| Part | Job | His wording | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. "You" situation | puts the viewer in it; the first 3 words name the subject | "If you…", "When you…", "When a doctor gives you…", "Have you ever noticed…" | 0-4 s |
| 2. Wrong belief | the obvious answer, so the flip has something to flip | "most people just…", "some people think…", "you might think…" | 3-8 s |
| 3. The flip | the real answer starts | "But…" (8 of 10), "Well, it turns out…", "You see,…" | 5-10 s |
| 4. How it works | step by step, one picture per step | "As…", "Then…", "Afterwards…", "Once…", "This creates…", "And with all of them combined…" | 8-25 s |
| 5. Payoff, then stop | a scale surprise, a human sting, or the last step completing | "…ten times bigger than Central Park", "…sleep at night knowing…", "…the stitches are removed" | last 3-5 s |

Variants:
- **Mid-story start** for a story fact: begin inside the action ("They opened the pallet of ammunition only to find…"), then "You see," explains it.
- **Rewatch ending:** "Watch it again and see if you can notice." Only when the picture rewards a second look.

## Style rules
- 75-95 words. Read time = words ÷ 2.8 (85 words = about 30 s).
- Sentences of 8-24 words (median 15 across the top 100), chained with "and", "as", "which". He almost never uses short dramatic fragments (2.5 % of his sentences are under 6 words).
- Every sentence after the first opens with a glue word (And, But, You see, Now, Then, Once, So, Well) or points back (This, He, The + thing). End a sentence with a comma + "-ing" result where it saves a sentence (about half his scripts).
- Second person throughout. Concrete nouns (needle, pus, rifle, Central Park), no jargon without a picture.
- One number at most per sentence, and always with a familiar anchor ("about this long", "from New York to the Grand Canyon").
- No greeting, no "subscribe", no "follow for part 2", no brand name in the words.
- Every sentence must have a picture. If a line can't be shown literally, cut it.

## Worked example (an original topic; the topic check said "fresh")
**Title:** How Do Divers Get The Bends? 😨 (96 words, ≈34 s; trim to 85 if the read is slow)

> If you dive deep and swim straight back up, your body can start to fizz from the inside. You see, the deeper you go, the harder the water squeezes you, forcing extra nitrogen from your air into your blood. You might think it just stays there. But if you rise too fast, the pressure drops and that nitrogen bubbles out, like opening a shaken soda. The bubbles lodge in your joints, folding you over in pain, which is why divers call it the bends. And the only cure is a chamber that squeezes you back down.

The facts behind it (to verify in `facts.md`):
- Pressure raises how much nitrogen dissolves into the blood and tissues (Henry's law).
- A fast ascent lets it come out as bubbles.
- Joint pain is the classic sign, hence "the bends".
- Treatment is recompression in a hyperbaric chamber.
- Prevention: slow ascents and safety stops.
