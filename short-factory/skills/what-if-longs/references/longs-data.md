## What all ten do
- **Premise at 0 s,** usually with a date or a number ("In 1986, 1.5 million balloons…", "In 1992 Pepsi ran a contest…", "If you poured a giant bucket of water onto the Sun…"). No intro, no greeting, no channel plug.
- **The first frame shows the strangest picture of the story** before the sentence ends: the bucket over the Sun, the logo seen from behind.
- **Pivot lines** end every section: "But…", "Meanwhile…", "And this is where things went terribly wrong", "But there's just one problem", "Case closed. Well, not exactly", "Well,…", "You see,…".
- **The payoff is held back:** balloon deaths at 4:56 of 5:26; the thumbnail item at 10:01 of 12:13.
- **Music is pulled out before reveals:** a black frame with near-silence after the stakes line (balloons, 25 s), 8 s of black with a busy tone (Toyota), and a whole segment with no music (the coffin in L03).
- **No spoken call to action.** It ends on a one-line kicker, often ironic ("Guinness ended the category altogether").
- **The brand sits inside scenes** (L03/L06: the channel's name on a shop sign, its initial on a shirt, its initial drawn in coffee), never as a corner logo.
- **Thumbnails:** no text in 7 of 10, one strange bright image, giant-vs-tiny scale, an arrow in 3 of 10.

## Why the winners won
1. **Paradox topics:** a harmless thing plus a terrible outcome (balloons, a soda contest, a gas pedal). The curiosity is built into the title.
2. **A planted second thread:** the missing fishermen are introduced before the launch, so viewers connect the dots themselves.
3. **Real voices carry the emotion** in true stories (a countdown, a 911 call). In animated what-ifs, the picture does it.
4. **Compilations of proven short formats with zero filler** (L03): every 45-80 s a new question and a new emotion.
5. **Packaging beats craft:** the same compilation idea got 11.2M with new 16:9 material and a single-image thumbnail, and 2.9M with re-used vertical shorts and a busy collage.

## What underperformed
- Too long for the story (Flamin' Hot, 10:50); static talking-head footage.
- Re-uploaded vertical shorts in a 16:9 compilation.
- A rushed payoff (Norman).
- A "disturbing" title the video only half delivers (Starbucks got away with it thanks to its first image).
